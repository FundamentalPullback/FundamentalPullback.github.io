# FIVE and FLS: fresh Stage 3 research, 3 November 2026

## Decision

**WAIT / not attractive at the scan reference price, neither approved for purchase.** Both have actual operating improvements, but neither offers a robust margin of safety in the ten-year FCFF cases below. FIVE has the clearer organic growth recovery. FLS has a stronger near-term booking signal than its organic revenue signal, and substantially more normalization complexity.

No prior completed FIVE/FLS valuation was found in the current A001–A144 research library. These are initial models, not revisions of an old price target. They should enter the research history as fresh initial assessments. Full current-rule compliance and transaction readiness are **not certified**.

## Common valuation framework and limits

The model projects ten annual periods from the valuation date, using the FY2026 guidance midpoint as the revenue anchor. Year 1 is an **analyst next-12-month operating approximation**, not company guidance or a detailed quarterly consensus build. Five explicit years are followed by five years fading toward stable growth, margin and reinvestment. Forecast years use an end-year discount convention. The absence of a quarter-by-quarter seasonal/stub valuation adds uncertainty, especially for FIVE.

FCFF = EBIT × (1−tax) + D&A − capex − increase in operating working capital − any separate economic intangible replacement reserve. SBC remains in EBIT; no future repurchase assumption reduces shares. For FLS, total D&A is added back and a 0.6%-of-sales intangible replacement reserve is explicitly reinvested, rather than treating amortizing acquired assets as free. During years 6–10, total reinvestment blends toward NOPAT × revenue growth / terminal ROIC; year 10 is fully consistent with the terminal reinvestment rule. The terminal uses NOPAT11 × (1−g/ROIC) / (WACC−g).

Current Treasury 10-year yield verified from the official 3 November table is 4.95%. Analyst ERP is 5%, with unverified risk coefficients roughly 1.2 for FLS and 1.3 for FIVE. FLS's approximately 17% gross-debt market weight and 5.7% recent bond coupon, after a 23% tax shield, put an illustrative WACC near 10%; FIVE has no funded debt and an illustrative cost of equity around 11.5%. These are **risk scenarios, not a claimed fitted beta or observed current bond yield**. The full sensitivity ranges are more reliable than a hundredth-of-a-dollar target.

Base assumptions:
- FLS: first-year revenue $5.235bn, then +6%, +5%, +5%, +5%; fade to 2.5%. EBIT margin 14% initially, 16.5% in year 5, 17% in year 10. Tax 23%, D&A 2.5% of revenue, initial capex 2.6%, incremental NWC 20% of revenue growth, intangible reinvestment 0.6%. WACC 10%, terminal g 2.5%, incremental ROIC 15%.
- FIVE: first-year revenue $6.350bn, then +11%, +10%, +9%, +8%; fade to 2.5%. EBIT margin 12.3% initially and 13% by year 5. Tax 25%, D&A 3.5% of revenue, initial capex 5.0%, incremental NWC 10% of revenue growth. WACC 11.5%, terminal g 2.5%, incremental ROIC 18%. The $318m first-year capex is above FY2026 guidance, intentionally funding growth/maintenance rather than repeating the low FY2025 spend.

Bear cases test slower growth, lower margins, weaker returns on reinvestment and higher capital costs. Bull cases assume much stronger operating execution and lower risk: FLS margins reach 20%, and FIVE reaches 14%; they are not probabilities or guaranteed bounds. FLS's official 2030 20% goal is adjusted, whereas the analyst model retains more economic costs. Merely repeating management's goal would be more generous than it appears.

## Cross-checks, priorities and what would change the decision

FIVE at $236.19 is about 23.5× FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint, versus approximately 26.7× refund-normalized TTM EPS using 55.5m shares. The scanner's 21.18× reported P/E understates that normalized multiple. Its normalized cash/SBC bracket implies approximately a 3.0–3.4% economic cash yield on current diluted market capitalization. Strong growth is necessary, not merely optional, at this price.

FLS is 19.5× management's FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint before removing tariff benefits and judging recurring adjustments. The DCF base market enterprise claim of about $12.14bn implies roughly 18.8× the illustrative normalized $644.5m EBIT. A clean cheap-earnings interpretation of the price is not supported. Neither reported P/E nor the exceptional TTM cash-flow comparison is a reliable standalone valuation.

These are within-company cash/earnings cross-checks, **not a finished date-matched peer-multiple study**. A homogeneous peer/history comparison, precise market beta/cost-of-debt calibration, current pension/minority claims, final technical eligibility and cause-of-pullback verification remain open before any future approval. FIVE's next release date also remains unconfirmed. The models are sufficient to explain WAIT at these references, not to certify every current-rule requirement.

A 20% discount to base would mechanically correspond to about $120 FIVE and $41 FLS. This is only an illustration of uncertainty protection, **not an agreed mandatory plan threshold, purchase limit or automatic entry trigger**. The current plan permits a partial-entry review at a reasonable price where supported; these prices do not pass the present base-case evidence. Current FLS price is supported only nearer the favorable operating case, and FIVE is above even the specified bull case.

Highest fresh research priority between the two: **FIVE**, because the customer/transaction recovery survives refund removal and the balance sheet offers more flexibility. That priority does not translate to a buy at $236.19. Reassess when its next verified release confirms normalized holiday margin, inventory/cash conversion and traffic durability, or when price changes substantially. For FLS, look for organic backlog conversion, earnings without refunds/gains, actual restructuring cash costs falling, and debt reduction. Do not equate order intake or acquired revenue with completed organic recovery.

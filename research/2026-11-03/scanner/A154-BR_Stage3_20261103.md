## November 3 evidence
Q1 FY2027 ended September 30. Recurring revenue grew 11%, approximately 9% organically, while total revenue grew 5%. Event-driven revenue declined 41%, and adjusted operating income declined 9%. Adjusted net earnings were essentially flat; adjusted EPS rose 3%, helped by fewer shares. Management reaffirmed FY2027 recurring growth of 6–8% at constant currency, adjusted operating margin approximately 21%, GAAP operating margin approximately 19%, and adjusted EPS growth 8–12%. [1]–[2]

The GAAP net-income decline is not an adequate core-business measure. Q1 includes $40.2m digital-asset losses versus $45.8m gains a year earlier, and a tax rate of 13.6% helped by transferable federal tax credits relating to the prior year. Core profitability nevertheless did weaken in the quarter: EBIT $173.5m versus $188.8m, adjusted EBIT $229.6m versus $251.2m. Strong recurring growth does not eliminate event-driven cyclicality or implementation costs.

## FCFF construction and reinvestment
Ten forward annual periods, 5 explicit + 5 transition; end-year discounting. Starting revenue is FY2026 $7,476.8m minus $16.5m coin-service revenue = $7,460.3m. No future Canton mark-to-market gain is projected. Year 1 is an annual forward run-rate near FY2027, not a remaining-nine-month stub.

Central total-revenue growth is 5% for five years, then 4.5/4/3.5/3/2.5%. It is below the 6–8% recurring-revenue guide because recurring fees were approximately 65% of FY2026 sales, with the rest comprising event-driven and largely distribution/postage revenue. It is a research assumption, not total-revenue guidance.

EBIT margin, **after SBC**, is 19/19.25/19.5/19.75/20% over the first five years, then 20%. Year 1 follows the GAAP margin guide; later expansion is an assumption. No SBC addback is made in FCFF. Tax is 25% throughout rather than the exceptional first-quarter rate. D&A addback is 4.3% of sales, covering physical/software depreciation and acquired-intangible amortization. TTM corresponding D&A was approximately $333.3m, or 4.41% of sales.

Initial investment budget is 4.5% of sales: roughly 1.65% physical/software investment, consistent with the $123m FY2027 guidance, plus 2.85% to replace acquired technology/customer capabilities and support growth. This is an analyst economic reinvestment reserve, not an announced acquisition commitment and not an assumption that every past acquisition must recur. At $223m in year 1, the intangible/acquisition portion is close to the $228.2m TTM actual. The entire D&A addback is not treated as free cash.

Deferred-client-conversion and sales-commission amortization remains within EBIT and is **not** added back. Working capital is 10% of incremental sales and includes net growth in capitalized client/contract balances, not their gross expenditures again. This avoids adding back the $49.8m Q1 amortization of other assets without funding replacement. A higher capex reserve does not duplicate payroll/R&D already expensed.

FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax) + D&A − investment − incremental operating working capital. Years 6–10 blend net reinvestment toward NOPAT × growth / 20% incremental ROIC at 20/40/60/80/100% transition weights. Terminal FCFF = year-11 NOPAT × (1 − 2.5%/20%). The 20% ROIC is assumed, not measured. Central year-1 FCFF is $1,063.279m; year-10 is $1,484.066m. Terminal value is 54.55% of operating EV.

WACC is a 9% research assumption, tested at 8–10%. A rough capital-weight check using 10% required equity return, 5.5% debt cost, 25% tax and current market weights gives about 9.2%; using 9% is not a punitive rate. Existing debt coupons range from 2.6% to 5.75%, but coupon rates are not the cost of equity. No beta/CAPM estimate is claimed.

## Balance-sheet bridge and non-operating assets
Central operating EV $18,122.015m, plus $8m credited excess cash, less $3,919.1m claims, divided by 114.3m diluted shares = **$124.33**.

- Cash $284.1m less $76.1m held by regulated entities and an analyst operating reserve $200m gives only $8m distributable credit. Cash held by foreign subsidiaries is $151.7m; this bridge does not presume all foreign cash is freely repatriated.
- Debt uses $3,398.7m principal rather than $3,347.1m book carrying value. The difference includes fees and fair-value hedge adjustments, not debt forgiveness. No scheduled principal matures before FY2030. The unused revolver is $1,101.3m.
- Additional conservative book-value claims are net retirement obligations $197.2m ($266.3m liabilities less $69.1m Rabbi Trust assets), software-license liabilities $124.1m, acquisition-related current and non-current liabilities $74.8m, and accrued dividend payable $124.3m. Pension assets excluded from non-operating credit are used only once. The dividend is treated as a claim against September cash, avoiding a free credit when pairing that balance with a later ex-dividend price.
- Operating leases remain in costs, not debt. Existing software-license obligations are recognized in the bridge; forecast investment funds new replacement and growth rather than repayment of that opening balance. Retirement service cost stays in operating earnings; the balance-sheet overlay recognizes unfunded accrued obligations. This is a deliberately conservative claims treatment, not an actuarial liquidation appraisal.
- Zero central credit is assigned to non-operating investments/tokens/warrants. The $185m token balance includes $162.3m subject to transfer restrictions; unlocks are released over a year and can reduce validator economics. Warrants are not freely transferable. Crediting all long-term investments excluding the already-counted Rabbi Trust, all tokens and warrants at September book value adds only $4.68/share, to $129.01. Excluding all four additional claims raises central value only to $128.88. These uncertain classifications therefore cannot bridge the gap to $183.76. [2]

No later buybacks are added for free. 114.3m is the reported quarter's diluted weighted count, slightly conservative relative to September basic shares plus known dilution; the valuation does not combine a lower basic count with pre-buyback cash.

## Scenarios and reverse DCF
- Adverse $70.19: growth 3%, EBIT margin 17.5→18%, investment 5%, incremental working capital 15%, WACC 10%, g 2%, ROIC 15%.
- Central $124.33: growth 5%, EBIT margin 19→20%, investment 4.5%, incremental working capital 10%, WACC 9%, g 2.5%, ROIC 20%.
- Favorable $212.65: growth 7%, EBIT margin 19→22%, investment 4.5%, working capital 10%, WACC 8%, g 3%, ROIC 25%. This requires faster consolidated growth, significant margin expansion and a lower discount rate together.
- Central WACC/g sensitivity (8–10%, 2–3%) is $99.46–$163.24. Lowering initial investment to 3.5% gives $128.24; raising it to 5.5% gives $120.42. Tax 23% gives $128.63. These sensitivity cases are not likelihood-weighted targets.
- At $183.76, reverse DCF requires **11.13% first-five-year total revenue growth**, or a **28.52% year-5 GAAP EBIT margin**, each changing only that driver. The total-growth requirement exceeds management's recurring-only range; the margin requirement is far above the approximately 19% GAAP guide. The current price can be supported only by a materially stronger combination of economics than the central case.

1. Scope, prior work, dates and thesis

Prior record A119 was a partial post-results review, not a full valuation. This work adds comparable earnings, the useful-life change, capital-payable reconciliation, a complete ten-year operating model, same-model reverse valuation, and limited homogeneous peer cross-checks. Latest results were released October 29, outside the user’s November 2–6 earnings exclusion. November 9 Investor/Capital Markets Day remains a separate event risk; its future targets are not available evidence today. [S1–S3]

Entegris supplies process materials and contamination-control products that become more important as semiconductor processes grow more complex. Recurring unit-related demand and customer qualification provide a stronger revenue foundation than pure equipment orders, but customer fab investment, utilization, advanced-node mix and export restrictions still make the business cyclical. The present case is operational improvement and monetization of installed capacity, not buybacks or a rerating assumption.

The principal positive surprise is real operating growth beyond accounting effects. The principal challenge is that the share price already capitalizes a long, strong expansion. Q3 Materials Solutions sales grew 15.9%; Advanced Purity Solutions grew 31.3%, with particularly strong segment profitability. Broad demand growth outside China supports the recovery, while China sales were flat. The stock’s pullback is not, by itself, evidence of mispricing. No single issuer-specific adverse-news explanation for the whole pullback has been independently established here; it is not labeled harmless profit-taking. [S1–S3]

3. Capacity, perimeter, asset quality and financing

The 2026 proxy shareholder letter states that the multiyear manufacturing investment cycle has concluded and the added capacity can support more than $1bn of incremental revenue with limited further investment. Relative to FY2025 revenue near $3.2bn, this supports a near-term capacity path around $4.2bn, not unlimited growth without capex. The base first forecast year reaches $4,229.6m. Beyond that point, explicit growth/replacement investment is retained. No primary maintenance-versus-growth capex split has been claimed. [S8]

CMC Materials/Cabot-related acquired assets are not free: acquired book amortization is added back only alongside an explicit replacement-investment reserve and ongoing expensed R&D. The 2023/24 QED, electronic chemicals and pipeline/industrial-material divestitures mean those years cannot establish current organic growth unaided. The small industrial specialty chemicals sale in 2025 and Life Sciences Fluid Management wind-down/restructuring are noted rather than used to erase recurring costs. The latest 9M revenue bridge attributes $341.2m of increase primarily to volume and negative $13.1m to FX, supporting a demand-driven current comparison. [S1, S2]

At September 26: cash $356.5m, debt principal $3,295m, not the $3,260.6m accounting carrying amount. Principal maturities are zero through 2027, $400m in 2028, $2,000m in 2029 and $895m in 2030. The $750m revolver is undrawn and matures 2031. Nine-month principal repayments of $450m improve financial risk; they are not deducted again from FCFF. Leverage still magnifies the equity downside. Goodwill $3,950m and intangibles $769.1m require skepticism toward book-based returns. [S2]

Operating leases remain operating costs; they are not added again to funded debt. The model reserves $150m of cash for operations (analyst assumption), giving net claims $3,088.5m = $3,295 − $356.5 + $150. It does not add present value of debt service on top of debt principal.

October 26 outstanding shares were 152.8m; Q3 diluted weighted shares 153.9m. The model uses 155m shares, a conservative existing-award allowance, not an exact November treasury-stock reconstruction. FY2025 outstanding options were about 1.3m at $89.66 and unvested RSUs about 1.3m; 2026 exercises/grants make that year-end schedule stale. No future buybacks are credited and forecast SBC remains expensed. At the proxy date, all directors/executives held 807,514 shares, less than 1%; BlackRock, T. Rowe Price and Vanguard were significant holders. These are dated ownership observations, not current trading signals. [S1, S2, S8]

4. Ten-year FCFF construction and assumptions

The reproducible model is model.py; all annual outputs and source-basis assumptions are in calculations.json. It is a controlled support calculation for the research workflow, not a banker formula workbook. Figures are nominal USD; no currency conversion is needed. Year 1 is the rolling forward twelve months from the November 2 valuation date, calibrated to the latest reported September 26 TTM. It is not a literal September fiscal endpoint or an assertion that October actuals are available. Each annual cash flow is discounted at the end of its rolling forecast year; there is no separately modeled stub or double-counted FY2026 cash flow.

Forecast EBITA is after SBC, expensed R&D and physical depreciation, but before acquired intangible amortization. Finite acquired amortization is then deducted to display EBIT. Conservatively, cash tax is charged on EBITA: no uncertain acquired-book-amortization tax shield is credited. FCFF = EBIT − cash tax + physical depreciation + acquired amortization − physical capex − intangible replacement investment − incremental working capital. The 2% base replacement reserve is an analyst economic cost, not reported capex or a second SBC deduction.

Base margin expansion requires higher utilization and better mix; it does not merely perpetuate the accounting life extension. Favorable growth and margins are deliberately demanding. 6. Entry conditioning, confidence and concrete next decisions

No new money at $151.56 under this underwriting. A provisional 25% discount to the base would be about $50.89, but this is merely a research-review threshold, not an approved entry ceiling. A move toward $50 would likely coincide with changed fundamentals and must trigger a full refresh rather than an automatic order. A price near the favorable value is not a margin of safety to the base.

Confidence is high in the reported data and need to normalize useful lives/capex payables; medium in the current no-margin decision; low-to-medium in exact ten-year fair value. Cyclicality, finite capacity headroom, customer mix and terminal returns justify a larger rather than smaller cushion. A tighter ownership roll-forward, exact maintenance split or tax-amortization schedule will improve precision, but a much stronger sustained growth thesis is what would change the conclusion materially.

November 9 questions: quantify remaining capacity by product and utilization; distinguish expansion, maintenance and compliance capex; explain growth investment after roughly $4.2bn sales; provide sustainable incremental margins after SBC; clarify Life Sciences exit costs, replacement R&D needs, working-capital accrual reversals and debt reduction priorities. A credible higher organic growth path with funded capacity and durable margins would trigger a new model, not a mechanical target increase.

Technical check: close 151.56 versus EMA20/50/200 of 150.128/148.292/135.649; EMA200 is rising; ATR 9.225 and average volume 3.057m. Intermediate/long trends pass. This does not cure valuation. No prospective trigger, structural stop, reward/risk qualification or portfolio size has been selected; funds and risk budget are unknown. If valuation and thesis later qualify, require a fresh signal and M/S record and size conditionally from risk budget and entry-to-stop distance, not from a fabricated allocation.

Thesis deterioration would be evidenced by order/design-win weakness, China restrictions spreading to other regions, repeated capacity spend without cash returns, sustained working-capital deterioration, or debt reduction stalling. Better accounting EPS alone would not upgrade the thesis. This publication records research only; no trade is authorized or executed.

VFC — recovery watch, essential normalization blocked

Research date: November 3, 2026. Price reference: November 2 regular close, $19.39. Latest earnings were October 28, outside the user’s excluded November 2–6 earnings week.

Decision

No approved entry. Do not commission the full DCF yet. VF shows genuine recovery evidence, but the retained-business tariff timing and cash-flow bridge are not sufficiently complete to certify sustainable TTM growth. This is an evidence blocker, not a conclusion that the business has failed. No fair value, target, margin of safety, reverse DCF or entry range is presented.

The North Face and Timberland remain core businesses; Timberland was not sold. Dickies was sold November 12, 2025, but remains in historical GAAP continuing operations. Supreme was sold October 1, 2024 and is excluded from the continuing-operation histories below. Confusing these perimeters materially distorts recovery.

What improved—and what has not been proved

The exact Dickies recast was recovered from the issuer’s November 2025 supplemental filing. Management-adjusted retained EBIT is:

  • FY2025: $529.419 million; FY2026: $649.974 million.
  • H1 FY2025: $194.966 million; H1 FY2026: $255.108 million; H1 FY2027: $269.168 million.
  • Current TTM: $664.034 million; prior TTM: $589.561 million; mechanical growth 12.63%.

These are management-adjusted figures, not yet economically normalized earnings. H1 retained sales growth includes currency; organic growth is modest. The North Face/Timberland improvement coexists with continuing Vans weakness. EPS growth also benefits from lower interest and tax, rather than solely operating improvement.

The load-bearing tariff mismatch

FY2026 recorded a $149.7 million refund receivable, with $93.8 million reducing COGS for goods already sold and $55.9 million offsetting tariffs in inventory. The inventory offset subsequently passed through COGS as goods sold in H1 FY2027. It is incorrect to remove that $55.9 million without also removing the matched tariff inventory cost.

However, the FY2026 Q4 catch-up also reverses some earlier expenses. Let x be retained-business H1 FY2026 tariff expense later refunded. The tariff-neutral rolling comparison becomes:

Current EBIT = 664.034 − x; prior EBIT = 589.561 + x.

The unresolved input changes that classification, so it cannot be silently assumed zero.

Cash normalization must be symmetric

Reported continuing TTM FCF is $635.879 million versus $232.907 million. TTM SBC is $78.883 million versus $80.946 million; subtracting it as an ownership-cost diagnostic yields $556.996 million versus $151.961 million, still not sustainable retained-business FCFF.

It would be misleading to subtract current tariff refunds while leaving the earlier refunded tariff payments untouched. Let p be tariff cash paid in H1 FY2026, distinct from expense x. Under the simplified assumptions that all $149.7 million was refunded in the current TTM and that the $100 million pension benefit is removed:

Current tariff-neutral FCF = 535.879 − p; prior = 232.907 + p.

Across p from zero to $149.7 million, this illustrative TTM cash improvement remains positive. That does not certify sustainable cash: the issuer says “substantially all” refunds were received; $37.6 million vendor/partner reimbursements were payable; Dickies CFO, capex, software and working capital are not separately recast. Exact cash timing and the retained perimeter remain essential gaps. No underlying cash deterioration is asserted from a one-sided refund subtraction.

Ownership costs, debt and liquidity

SBC remains an economic cost. Recurring software and lease cash also remain. Company-adjusted Reinvent exclusions require scrutiny: H1 FY2027 removes $15.680 million of gains, while prior H1 excluded $46.265 million costs. Stock-price-contingent consulting fees measured through June 2027 are not automatically free or permanently finished.

September carrying financial debt totals $3,538.589 million, with $442.951 million unrestricted cash; carrying net debt is $3,095.638 million. This is not a completed principal-value bridge. A $500 million maturity falls in April 2027. Operating lease liabilities total $1,462.225 million and H1 lease cost is $211.880 million; do not capitalize leases in EV while also double charging their expense.

The $1.5 billion asset-based facility had approximately $1.47 billion availability, borrowing-base dependent. Reported covenant compliance and liquidity do not imply investment-grade credit: disclosed ratings were BB/Ba2. Supplier-finance eligible payables are another working-capital consideration. No immediate financing failure is concluded, but leverage materially reduces valuation tolerance.

Outlook and what would reopen the case

Management reiterated FY2027 approximately 8% adjusted operating margin and normalized FCF flat/up versus approximately $405 million. Reported FCF of at least $515 million is a different measure; do not capitalize refund cash as perpetuity. FY2027 has a 53rd week. The longer-term 10% exit-margin ambition is a management target, not an achieved base case.

Before full Stage 3 valuation, obtain: 1. Retained-business quarterly tariff-in-COGS, cash payments, refunds and inventory reserve bridge. 2. Dickies cash-flow/working-capital carve-out and stranded-cost treatment for both TTMs. 3. Economically recurring restructuring/consulting and compensation schedule.

Only then build ten-year FCFF, same-model reverse, peers and scenarios. Stage 3.5 remains inactive. Technical recovery cannot cure missing fundamental evidence.

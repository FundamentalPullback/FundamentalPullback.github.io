CNX Resources — Stage 3 investment review

Research completed November 3, 2026 using information available through November 2 and the November 2 regular close of $37.20. USD millions unless indicated.

Decision

WAIT — no approved new entry. The fundamental gate warrants valuation work, rather than an automatic rejection on one weak quarter. Contracted 2027 realizations support a near-term recovery. However, the completed operating-driver FCFF assessment does not demonstrate a margin of safety at $37.20. The valuation model is screen-grade; independent source/history and model QA passed. This publication records research only. No trade, stop or portfolio allocation is authorized by this report.

The illustrative central value is $21.05/share. Central-deck WACC sensitivities of 9%–11% produce roughly $17–26; capital sensitivities are shown separately below. These are conditional model outputs, not a certified confidence interval. A stronger gas-price case can support substantially more than the current price. Therefore the conclusion is insufficient demonstrated safety margin, not certain overvaluation.

Why the business is worth researching

CNX combines Appalachian shale and coalbed-methane production with owned midstream infrastructure and environmental-attribute activities. Hedging reduces near-term cash volatility. It is not a pure unhedged gas-price bet, but protection expires and reinvestment is necessary to offset depletion. Management’s maintenance-of-production strategy, positive recurring cash generation and a better 2027 contracted price justify completing valuation despite weak recent cash comparisons. Buybacks are capital allocation, not evidence by themselves that the stock is cheap.

Sustainable operating profit and cash

  • Removing unrealized derivative gains, while retaining settled hedges, SBC, depletion and recurring operating costs, gives TTM operating EBIT $686.181m versus $592.812m, up 15.8%. This is not organic, constant-price growth. Asset-sale gains are below operating profit and are not added to normalized EBIT.
  • At a constant physical-price deck of gas $3/Mcf, NGLs $25/bbl and oil $60/bbl, TTM EBIT is approximately $815.413m versus $815.078m: essentially flat. Cash operating surplus after SBC slips about 0.5%. Other tested commodity decks also produce approximately flat underlying earnings.
  • Q1 2026 had a net $9m credit for unused transportation/processing capacity versus $4m prior-year expense, with colder-weather optimization contributing. Removing the entire $13m comparative benefit as a conservative sensitivity makes constant-deck earnings decline about 1.6%. That is a sensitivity, not a proven isolated nonrecurring charge or an automatic gate failure.
  • Owner cash calculated as CFO less capital investment and economic SBC, plus net equity-affiliate investment cash flow but excluding asset-sale proceeds, falls to $460.432m from $546.613m. Capex rises to $625.472m from $426.069m. Higher investment, tax timing and hedge settlement timing must be examined before calling this operational deterioration.
  • The $30m July 2026 sale of credits relating to 2025 remediation is not recurring current-year operating cash. Removing that receipt alone gives $430.432m, versus $546.613m. Removing aggregate working-capital sources instead gives $429.858m versus $563.517m. Do not subtract both mechanically: the credit collection may already be within the working-capital movements. The joint $399.858m result is preserved only as a conservative stress, not certified normalized cash.
  • Derivative cash timing also matters: nine-month realized hedge losses were $123.710m, while actual cash settlements paid were $208.527m; the $84.817m timing drag compares with $39.265m a year earlier. Exact Note 12 reconciliations are in independent QA. Negative cash growth cannot be attributed entirely to economic deterioration.

Gate conclusion: conditional pass to valuation, not purchase approval

The constant-price business is flat, but the earnings threshold is not a rigid growth-only screen. The relevant recovery evidence is contractual: 2027 fully covered gas volume is 409.4 Bcf at $3.30/Mcf, versus 447.2 Bcf at $2.73 in 2026. The company’s October 14 strip illustration projects a $204.99m 2027 realized hedge gain versus a $106.09m 2026 loss. These are scenario-dependent settlements, not guaranteed incremental profit irrespective of physical gas prices. The model uses the actual covered prices and remaining unhedged exposure, not a perpetual $205m addback.

The current maintenance program is funded and cash remains positive. This supports a conditional business-quality pass to DCF despite weak current cash growth. It does not establish durable high growth: production is modeled flat centrally, and the stock must compensate for commodity, reserve and reinvestment uncertainty.

Environmental economics: three different items

  1. AEC/environmental-attribute sales are operating revenue. Nine-month revenue declined to $46.746m from $61.234m. Management expects approximately $50m of 2026 net Pennsylvania Tier 1 AEC sales. The model uses $60m gross revenue and approximately $10m related fees inside operating expense, giving $50m net—not an assumed 20% revenue increase.
  2. The July $30m 45Z credit sale monetized previously recognized tax benefits. The proceeds approximated carrying value, so there was no material additional income-tax benefit from the sale. It is removed from current normalized cash diagnostics, not blindly deducted from EBIT.
  3. Future 45Z sales are a separate conditional opportunity. Prepared remarks anticipate roughly $40m annually from 2027, while explicitly noting pending implementation rules. Central valuation allows $20m per year only in 2027–29; adverse allows zero and favorable $40m. No credit enters terminal value. Zero versus $40m changes value by only about $0.68/share, so the current WAIT does not depend on receiving the credits. Tax qualification, rulemaking and monetization remain risks.

Capital, reserves, perimeter and balance sheet

The October 29 remarks describe a maintenance-of-production program of about 1.5 rigs and a partial-year completion crew. Updated base capital is $563m, plus the first of three approximately $16m annual Utica-rights payments in 2026. All base capital is retained in the model; no supposed maintenance/growth split is used to inflate cash. The remaining 2027 and 2028 rights payments are also charged.

Apex was acquired in January 2025, so reported TTM comparisons are not perfectly equal-perimeter. Current capex includes $16.5m for Utica rights; the acquisition itself and disposal proceeds are not recurring FCF. The forecast assumes no additional acquisitions and no free growth from optional acreage. Separate favorable-case growth requires additional investment.

Year-end 2025 proved reserves were 9.662 Tcfe, including 6.972 developed and 2.690 undeveloped. The central ten-year forecast consumes 6.15 Tcfe before considering 2026 production. A 15-year flat-production continuation approaches the entire dated reserve base; perpetual value therefore requires conversion of additional resources and ongoing reserve replacement. Five-year development costs and the $2.221bn reported future development/abandonment schedule are not interchangeable with annual maintenance capex.

Principal debt is $2,299.850m, versus $2,278.924m carrying value after discounts/fees. Finance leases are $33.245m. Only $1.977m of cash is unrestricted; restricted cash is not surplus. Asset-retirement obligations total $189.043m, including $21.075m current. The model reserves this existing claim once and does not add operating-lease liabilities when the corresponding expenses remain in operating cash flows. CNX and CNXM credit facilities have approximately $1.140bn and $533m unused capacity respectively, but are separate facilities, not freely interchangeable unrestricted cash.

The May 2026 convertible notes were settled; there is no residual convertible debt or duplicate conversion dilution. The share denominator is a 144.677225m proxy: October 14 common shares plus identified Q3 diluted award claims and anti-dilutive RSUs. This is not a complete future-grant roll-forward. Future SBC stays in operating expense, and there are no free assumed buybacks. A conservative $37m post-quarter buyback cash reserve matches the newer share-count basis without giving credit for undisclosed October cash generation; the exact dates do not align perfectly, a small disclosed bridge limitation.

Ten-year FCFF model

The valuation date is November 2. A 59-day residual 2026 stub is followed by 2027–31 detailed operating years and 2032–36 transition years. Cash flows are discounted at 1 + 59/365 through 10 + 59/365 years. No elapsed October cash flow is counted again.

Central assumptions, all mapped by source and classification in source_map.json: - Production: 615 Bcfe annually, flat physical volume. Product mix is 562.5 Bcf gas, 8.6m barrels NGL and 0.15m barrels oil. - Henry Hub: $3.16 in 2027 from the EIA October outlook, then analyst midcycle $3.35/$3.50 in 2028–29 and 2% nominal escalation. Unhedged gas realizes (Henry Hub + regional basis) × BTU conversion; starting basis is −$0.70/MMBtu. - Covered gas: 409.4 Bcf at $3.30 in 2027, 233.1 at $3.20 in 2028, and 38.2 at $3.32 in 2029. These prices are already dollars per Mcf and are not multiplied by BTU again. No derivative balance-sheet asset is added on top of its modeled cash. Extra basis-only positions are disclosed as a sensitivity limitation, not a permanent benefit. - Initial NGL/oil prices: $25/$60 per barrel, then 2% inflation. Midstream, environmental and other revenue are separately identified. - LOE $90m, transport $395m, production tax 1.5% of physical sales, G&A $145m including $25m economic SBC, recurring other operating costs $68m, exploration $14m and other net corporate expense $14m. The fully burdened company cost metric is not used because it includes financing costs. - DD&A $550m is retained and scaled with activity/inflation. Base investment $563m is inflated and fully funded; finite rights payments and $5m annual affiliate investment are additional. Working capital consumes 3% of revenue growth. - Normalized tax is 25%, with only the explicit finite 45Z credit schedule. Tax-loss carryforwards are not assigned unverified permanent value. - WACC: 10% central, 11.5% adverse, 9% favorable. The framework uses verified October 30 Treasury 10-year 4.94% plus analyst equity-risk/beta and borrowing-cost assumptions, not an asserted market-consensus discount rate.

Terminal nominal growth is 2% with no central real production growth. Net reinvestment is the greater of NOPAT × g / 10% incremental ROIC or the replacement-capital/affiliate/working-capital floor less DD&A. The model therefore does not erase depletion spending. Terminal value contributes 41.0% of central operating EV. Finite 10/15/20/25-year alternatives remain exhaustion approximations, not an engineered field-by-field reserve NAV.

Same-model reverse

With all other central inputs unchanged, the current price requires approximately $0.531/MMBtu more in the unhedged gas deck, scaled with inflation: Henry Hub about $3.69 in 2027 and $4.05 in 2029. Fixed covered hedge prices remain unchanged in this calculation. This is a plausible commodity outcome, not an obviously impossible expectation; it simply does not provide a conservative safety margin. No multiple-based shortcut replaces the FCFF reverse.

Peers

The historical, common-tax crosscheck uses AR and EXE, not CNX as its own peer. CNX trades at about 6.17× TTM EBITDA after exploration and SBC and 17.33× the historical tax-normalized FCFF proxy, versus AR upstream approximately 5.53×/16.62× and EXE 4.41×/12.65×. These are not common-commodity-deck fair values: integrated midstream exposure, acquisition timing, reserves, environmental credits and AR minority-history adjustments differ. AR’s AM stake is excluded consistently from upstream EV and operating cash; no double-counted AM dividend value. No peer median is applied as a target.

Margin, confidence and Stage 3.5

The current price is above the central conditional estimate; the model therefore supplies no positive margin of safety. For a commodity producer, the wide scenario range and reserve/hedge uncertainties argue for a larger discount, not a token one. A 25% discount to $21.05 is approximately $15.79 arithmetically, but it is not an approved entry level: the commodity deck, financial statements and technical trend must be refreshed at any future price.

Confidence is moderate in the current WAIT decision and low-to-moderate in a single intrinsic-value number. The near-term contractual recovery is real evidence; it is not a perpetual growth franchise. A clearer margin could arise from a lower share price, durable improvement in unhedged realizations, lower verified sustaining costs, or better reserve conversion—not from changing the valuation method to force approval.

The technical review shows price above EMA200 and a weakly rising EMA200, with EMA50 slightly below EMA200; the long-term trend is recovering rather than unequivocally strong. This does not override the valuation conclusion. Approved entry, stop, sizing and portfolio allocation remain null.

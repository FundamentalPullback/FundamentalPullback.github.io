Celestica Stage 3 valuation refresh

Research completed 3 November 2026 UTC. Market snapshot: regular close on 2 November 2026, $249.22. NYSE common shares, USD; financial statements and models use USD millions unless per-share figures are specified. This is a refresh of a previously researched watchlist company, not a claim that CLS passes the current scanner. No trade, position change, stop or entry is authorized.

Decision

Wait for a better combination of price, cash conversion and technical repair. Celestica’s new operating outlook materially improves the investment case, but $249.22 is not an obvious bargain after financing the growth. The ten-year FCFF model gives $236 central, $108 adverse and $344 favorable. A central sensitivity region of approximately $222–252 varies WACC from 12.5% to 11.5%, holding the operating case and 3% terminal growth unchanged. This is a plausible-value sensitivity, not a confidence interval or a buy range. The full adverse-to-favorable span is not called fair value.

At $249.22, margin of safety to the central estimate is −5.5%, using (value−price)/value. The price does not require the whole favorable case: a sustained economic-margin improvement of approximately 0.33 percentage points over the base path would reproduce it. That is plausible but not a cushion against execution failures. A 20% research-only discount reference is about $189. This is a deliberately rounded review reference for a concentrated, capital-hungry expansion, not an approved entry ceiling, target or prediction. It should not be mechanically enforced if new evidence changes value. The discount addresses forecast error and the asymmetry of committing capacity before demand converts into cash; it is not another probability-weighted loss layered into the scenarios.

Valuation confidence: medium-low. Actual operating growth is well supported, but the ten-year sales and reinvestment path is not contracted revenue. The price decision is complete; a future approval requires renewed evidence and the plan’s technical/risk gates. The current technical failure independently blocks entry: price is below EMA50 and EMA200, EMA20 is below EMA50, and EMA200 is falling. The technical appendix is included below.

What changed since the previous review

The preserved 10 October report evaluated $362.16 and estimated $205 central value. At the new close, price is 31.2% lower, while this model’s central value is 15.2% higher. Both changes matter. Neither the prior $205 nor the earlier exploratory $251 should be treated as an immutable sell or buy level.

The 26 October results raised FY2026 revenue outlook from $20.5 billion to $21 billion and adjusted EPS from $11.30 to $11.55. However, FY2026 company-defined FCF guidance fell from $600 million to $460 million, and management explicitly expects a net use of funds in FY2027 FCF as working capital and capex rise. Earnings and cash are therefore telling different, reconcilable stories. [S3, S4 slides 46–49]

The extra proceeds and extra dilution must both count. The current September cash balance already contains the proceeds; they are not added again. [S2 pp 13–14, 36]

Business and evidence for demand

Celestica supplies design, engineering and manufacturing for data-center networking, compute and storage, plus diversified Advanced Technology Solutions. Q3 CCS revenue was $4,872.3 million, 84% of company revenue, up 102% year over year; ATS was $957.4 million, up 23%. HPS revenue was approximately $2.3 billion, up 70%. ATS is not solely a healthcare business; the issuer is a technology/manufacturing company, although HealthTech is one end market. Networking and Compute & Storage are renamed Communications and Enterprise categories, not a new reporting perimeter. [S2 note 3, S3]

The current evidence is more substantive than a generic AI narrative:

  • Investor Day projects FY2027 CCS revenue growth of about 110% and ATS about 10%, supporting the $40 billion total. Networking growth involves 800G and 1.6T products; management expects next-generation compute program ramps during 2027. [S4 slides 27–40, 46]
  • The presentation describes a rack-scale OpenAI Jalapeño program, with production ramp planned in 2027, and scale-up switching for AMD Helios. These are issuer-disclosed program expectations, not independently verified, take-or-pay orders. [S4 slides 33, 38, 40]
  • Management says 2028 customer demand forecasts are meaningfully above the 2027 revenue outlook and describes multi-year capacity alignment with anchor customers. It does not supply a firm 2028 revenue number. The model’s 30% FY2028 growth is an analyst assumption, not guidance. [S3; S4 slide 47]
  • Capacity investment includes U.S. and Thailand facilities; the presentation also describes broader North American and Asian expansion and automation. Capex rises from about $1 billion in 2026 to $1.5–2 billion in 2027. Delivering trained labor, utilities, components and qualified production on time is essential. [S2 p 36; S4 slides 10–11, 46–49]

The moat is qualification, engineering integration, speed and scale, rather than proprietary control over every AI dollar. Competitors include EMS/ODM manufacturers and customer in-sourcing. Customer designs and silicon selections can change. More high-value pass-through components can lift sales faster than gross profit; revenue alone is an incomplete measure of value added.

Concentration is material: the three largest customers represented 35%, 17% and 14% of Q3 sales, versus 30%, 15% and 14% a year earlier. Top-ten customers represented about 85% of Q3 sales. Publicly displayed partner logos must not be mapped to these anonymous percentages. A single delayed program can affect working capital, utilization and valuation disproportionately. [S2 note 3 and customer discussion]

Why GAAP and adjusted margins diverge

Q3 GAAP margin fell from 10.2% to 7.9%, while company-adjusted margin rose from 7.6% to 8.5%. The primary explanation is the total-return swap tied to CLS shares: Q3 2025 recognized a $113.3 million gain, while Q3 2026 recognized a $3.9 million loss. Removing only those operating marks produces EBIT of $211.7 million versus $463.9 million, growth of about 119%, and margins of 6.63% versus 7.96%. The operational improvement survives normalization. [S2 note 9; S3 reconciliation]

The model does not simply use 8.5% adjusted margin. Employee SBC, director compensation, acquired-intangible amortization and ordinary restructuring remain economic costs. Q3 employee SBC was $21.3 million, acquired-intangible amortization excluded by management was $10 million, and restructuring was $0.9 million. Retaining these costs explains the difference between 8.5% adjusted and approximately 8.0% economic EBIT. FY2023’s $45.6 million TRS gain was recorded below EBIT, so it is not subtracted from EBIT again. The fixed-tax EPS proxy excludes miscellaneous income in all periods.

GAAP gross-margin decline also needs nuance: after removing TRS marks but retaining SBC, Q3 gross margin is about 11.01%, versus 11.51% a year earlier. Better operating leverage in SG&A and mix helped operating margin despite this gross-margin pressure. Higher revenue is not sufficient if gross profit per unit of capital weakens.

The Q3 GAAP tax rate was 13%, adjusted rate 18%, with favorable profit mix and a discrete benefit. The model does not perpetuate either. It uses 21% cash/operating tax in 2027, then 22%, 23% and 24%, converging to 24%, rather than treating deferred-tax benefits as recurring cash income. YTD cash taxes were $284.4 million, versus GAAP tax expense $175.4 million. [S2; S3]

Cash conversion and the expansion funding requirement

The strongest caution is receivables financing. During 9M2026 the company sold $2,176.9 million of receivables; $700.8 million remained outstanding at September 30 versus none at December 2025. The sold receivables disappear from receivables and their proceeds enter CFO. We subtract the increase in outstanding sold receivables when diagnosing conversion, not the entire turnover of receivables sold. The facility was increased to $1 billion in September and is uncommitted. [S2 note 4]

Actual financing-classified cash outlays differ from expense: YTD withholding payments on vesting awards were $373.6 million, and treasury-share purchases to deliver awards were $37.6 million. Strict PP&E FCF after those actual payments is approximately −$21.7 million, before adjusting receivables financing. This is an alternative cash-burden view; it is not deducted in addition to SBC expense in FCFF. Nor is the withholding payment mislabeled a new liability. No TRS cash settlement was received in 9M2026; the large FY2025 settlement receipts belong in financing, not recurring operating FCF. [S2 note 9 and cash-flow statement]

Unpaid PP&E purchases rose from $59.4 million in December to $238.3 million in September. Supplier-funded capital expenditure can make accounts payable look like operating financing. The model adds the capital payable back when constructing operating working capital, models capex as cash spending including settlement of prior capital purchases, and does not deduct the $238.3 million again in the equity bridge.

Computer software sits in intangibles, not PP&E. FY2025 software carrying value was $15.1 million; its separate cash purchases are not disclosed. Thus CFO less gross PP&E is labeled narrowly. Deducting the entire investing “Other” line gives a conservative expanded diagnostic: FY2025 $455.8 million and 9M2026 $387.0 million before SBC/factoring adjustments. “Other” is not asserted to be all software. Forecast capex explicitly includes software and finance-lease additions; all D&A is retained in EBIT before the cash add-back. This small historical disclosure limit does not change the cash conclusion. [S1 note 8; S2 cash flows]

Balance sheet dilution and ownership

September cash was $3,961.3 million. Face term debt was $735.6 million, finance-lease obligations $88 million, and pension/other post-employment liabilities $90.8 million. Debt carrying value of $818.8 million includes $4.8 million unamortized issuance costs; the valuation uses the $823.6 million face debt plus finance leases instead. Current debt/finance lease obligations were only $27.3 million. Term A/revolver mature in April 2031 following the April 2026 amendment; Term B matures in June 2031. Available revolver was approximately $1,739.3 million, and covenant compliance was reported. [S2 note 8 and liquidity discussion]

About 37% of cash was outside Canada. A $500 million operating-cash reserve is withheld from the equity bridge; this is an analyst liquidity assumption, not reported restricted cash. Growth’s funding needs are separately charged through capex and working capital, so cash is not both reserved for the entire expansion and spent again in the model. Restricted pension assets are not treated as distributable cash. Ordinary operating-lease costs remain in margins, and operating-lease liabilities of $212.3 million are not deducted a second time. Finance leases are in debt, with depreciation/additional leased-capital investment consistently modeled.

The TRS had 1.25 million shares of notional exposure at a $288.87 strike. Its September $90.4 million asset should not be added unadjusted at a $249.22 market price. An illustrative spot mark is a $49.6 million liability, before financing carry and contractual settlement detail; the bridge deducts this. Every $10 change in the share price changes that simple mark by $12.5 million, about $0.10 per diluted share. The model does not forecast stock-price gains as operating earnings.

The denominator is 127.412 million shares, using 126.112 million actual shares at October 21 plus the Q3 1.3 million incremental award effect. The company’s FY2027 presentation assumes roughly 127 million diluted shares, so the proxy is broadly consistent. The 121.8 million quarterly average is inappropriate for valuing all shares after the issue. Future SBC stays in margins; there are no assumed free repurchases and no automatic additional annual dilution charge for the same future expense. Existing award dilution remains an uncertainty tested through share-count sensitivity.

The October 30 buyback announcement authorizes up to 3,132,127 shares, beginning November 3. It is capacity, not evidence that shares have been bought. The prior program had cancelled only 205,184 shares through October 20 at $271.75 average; neither authorization offsets the 11.129 million new issue for free. [S9]

Ownership evidence is dated, not a live cap table: the April proxy lists FMR 6.4%, BlackRock 5.5%, and directors/executives collectively 1.10% on its March 27 basis. CEO Rob Mionis held approximately 1.010 million shares on that basis. Those percentages predate the August issue and must not be presented as November ownership. The combined chair/CEO role makes independent board oversight relevant; the proxy names a lead independent director. No unsupported conclusion about recent insider selling is drawn. [S8]

The central revenue path recognizes the official 2027 outlook but does not carry 83% or 90% growth indefinitely. Economic margins are below management’s adjusted metrics and ultimately fall as competition and mix normalize. The favorable case assumes continued strong design wins and conversion, not merely a higher valuation multiple. The adverse case combines program shortfalls, utilization pressure and greater capital intensity. Scenarios are not probabilities or worst-case loss limits.

WACC stays at the prior model’s 12%, a USD risk judgment for volatile, concentrated, cyclical manufacturing, rather than a newly claimed CAPM estimate. The small debt share means cost of equity dominates. A live beta/risk-premium estimate was not independently calibrated; 11–13% sensitivity is therefore essential. A 20% terminal post-tax incremental ROIC is materially below the company’s reported adjusted ROIC, which is a pre-tax earnings measure on a different capital base, excludes ROU assets and is affected by receivable financing. Those numbers must not be equated.

Central D&A/revenue runs 1.0%, 1.3%, 1.5%, 1.6%, 1.7%, reaching 1.8% in transition. This reflects a growing depreciable asset base, rather than keeping today’s low depreciation ratio forever. Reverse valuation and sensitivities

The same FCFF model, not a second method, reproduces $249.22 under each of these separate changes:

  • Add 0.332 percentage points to the entire economic-margin path, giving approximately 8.78% in 2027 and 7.33% terminal margin.
  • Increase 2027 revenue to approximately $42.50 billion, keeping later percentage growth unchanged. This is about 6.25% above guidance, not a catastrophic expectation bar.
  • Add 1.24 percentage points to growth in each of years 1–5, with the same transition rules.
  • Reduce WACC from 12% to approximately 11.59%, with the operating case unchanged.

These alternatives are conditional combinations. They are not all simultaneously required, uniquely inferred market forecasts, or independent confirmations of value. They show why $249 is closer to reasonable than the previous $362, yet still exposed to cash and execution risks.

One-variable checks: central value is $270.63 at 11% WACC and $208.93 at 13%; operating NWC at 8% or 12% of sales gives $246.62 or $225.87; terminal ROIC 15% or 25% gives $226.91 or $241.84; terminal EBIT margin 6% or 8% gives $214.04 or $258.45. A 1% increase in diluted shares, with cash unchanged, reduces value by approximately 1%. The full WACC/g grid and ten-year schedules are reproducible in calculations.json and CSV files.

Price decline event timing and next review

The historical price decline cannot be assigned to one causal story merely because earnings were strong. The evidence supports an operating upgrade alongside a larger funding requirement, concentration risk and a much lower market price. The actual quarter and Investor Day were on October 26–27, outside the November 2–6 earnings exclusion window. The exact next earnings date is not confirmed in the reviewed issuer materials; that does not constitute blanket clearance of future dates. Recheck the official event calendar before any future entry.

At the November 2 close, the separate technical review records EMA20 298.15, EMA50 318.76, EMA200 318.68, with EMA200 below its value 20 sessions earlier. Price $249.22 fails both intermediate conditions and is below the long-term average. The technical review reconstructed the final Yahoo daily close from that response’s regularMarketPrice/time when the final daily-row close was null, and independently matched StockAnalysis. No after-hours price is substituted. This is not a new scanner-qualified candidate and there is no entry to size.

Revisit the investment case if:

  1. Cash usage, receivable financing, capital payables and inventory support the scale-up without eroding liquidity or requiring materially more dilution.
  2. The $40 billion 2027 revenue and economic-margin assumptions are confirmed by production ramps, rather than only enlarged customer forecasts.
  3. Price offers a defensible discount or new cash evidence supports a higher central value, and the plan’s technical trend/entry/risk conditions recover.
  4. A major customer loss, program delay, cancellation, tariff recovery failure, capacity overrun or sustained gross-margin deterioration changes the thesis. Such verified deterioration triggers review regardless of price.

This research does not change any existing position or Stage 4 protection. Above-central price alone is not an automatic sale under the plan. No monitoring automation or future trade is implied.

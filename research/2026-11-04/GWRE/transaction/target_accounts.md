## Revenue transition: Note 3, effective 1 February 2026

Revised FRS 102 Section 23 changed professional-services recognition timing. The current period includes **£204,716 additional revenue**, with an equal reduction of deferred income; comparatives were not restated.

- March revenue excluding the disclosed transition uplift: **£7,664,528**.
- EBIT excluding that uplift alone, with all other costs unchanged: **−£144,493**, rather than positive £60,223.
- Also excluding the **£444,274 operating FX gain** gives an analytical EBIT of **−£588,767**. January EBIT excluding its £874,277 FX expense is **−£5,279,371**.
- These are transparent partial bridges, not full restatements or forecasts. In particular, lease accounting remains different.
- Multiplying adjusted stub revenue by six gives £45,987,168 only as a mechanical scale indicator, not ARR or a forecast. Reported revenue times six is £47,215,464. Do not describe either as a company-guided run rate.

## Lease transition: Note 24

Revised Section 20 was adopted on 1 February 2026 using modified retrospective application; January comparatives were not restated. March recognizes a £2,010,405 right-of-use addition and £83,767 ROU depreciation, leaving £1,926,638 carrying value. Closing lease liabilities total £1,932,883 (£977,950 current; £954,933 noncurrent). The discount rate is 8%. Short-term leases still incurred £117,450 operating expense.

Note 24 describes the initial lease liability as approximately **£1.9m**, while the closing balance plus £77,522 principal cash implies £2,010,405 if there were no other movements. The accounts do not give a complete exact opening-liability rollforward. Preserve that discrepancy instead of silently treating the inferred figure as reported.

The cash-flow statement places **£77,522 principal and £12,801 lease interest in financing**. Therefore, a cash comparison that retains all lease cash as an operating cost reduces March CFO less capex from £7,323,347 to **£7,233,024**. This is a cash classification bridge, not an exact prior-GAAP EBIT reconstruction. The latter needs the former straight-line rental/accrual schedule, which is not disclosed. Do not add back ROU depreciation while also ignoring the lease cash obligation. Do not count the £2.01m noncash ROU addition as ordinary cash capex. Be consistent about lease debt treatment in any EV bridge.

## Cash quality: do not annualize the stub

March CFO comprises **£7,220,741 net cash inflow from debtor and creditor movements**: £14,281,700 debtor collection less £7,060,959 creditor outflow. This is 98.2% of CFO. Trade receivables dropped from £20,875,299 to £6,607,099. Accruals plus deferred income fell from £34,535,417 to £27,909,864; management reports deferred income alone falling from approximately £31.0m to £25.2m. Collection and billing timing, plausibly seasonal, dominate; these two periods alone do not establish a stable seasonal pattern.

- March CFO before debtor/creditor movements: **£129,944**.
- Less £27,338 capex and £90,323 total lease cash: **£12,283**.
- Also excluding the £315,546 cash tax receipt gives **−£303,263**. This is diagnostic, not normalized annual FCF; other effects, including FX and SBC, remain.
- January CFO before debtor/creditor movements is **−£5,187,867**, versus reported positive CFO £1,914,824. Its £9,399,462 creditor inflow was important too.

The annual plus stub spans fourteen months, not TTM. March CFO times six would be particularly misleading. Do not subtract the revenue-transition uplift from CFO again mechanically: its deferred-income counterpart is noncash, and the full cash-flow bridge already reconciles.

## Software, R&D, SBC and forecasting constraints

The consolidated statements show tangible assets, not a separately capitalized software/development asset. There is no separate R&D-expense figure. Reported investing purchases are tangible equipment; the lease asset is noncash. Technology/product average headcount rose from 56 to 60; total average headcount was 198 and 200. Staff expense was £19,286,324 annually and £3,864,300 for the stub. These are useful cost context, not a defensible R&D capitalization schedule. The £315,545 R&D tax-credit adjustment is not R&D spending.

SBC of £431,910 and £76,437 is expensed in the P&L and added back in CFO. Treat it as an economic cost through an explicit expense/dilution/replacement-cost policy; do not grant a second addback or assume acquisition retention awards equal these historical numbers.

Use the annual period for a clearly labeled historical baseline and the adjusted stub as a limited recent datapoint. Forecasting still needs subscription/services mix, ARR and retention, customer concentration, full-year working-capital seasonality, sustainable hiring/retention costs, old/new lease comparability, acquisition-date cash and liabilities, US-GAAP conversion and purchase-accounting adjustments. Historical sterling cash is not acquisition-date USD cash. No standalone fair value or durable synergy margin is established here.

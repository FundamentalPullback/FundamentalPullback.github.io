## Decision

**Research complete. The maintenance-capital issue no longer justifies a blanket block. AR is a conditional reasonable-price candidate, not an unconditional Stage 3 pass at $39.93.** The central model is $32.53; that is 18.5% below market, while a plausible combined improvement case supports $44.37 and approximately 12.2% annual illustrative equity return. The market does not require an extraordinary gas boom: the central reverse needs roughly $3.40 Henry Hub in 2027 and $3.75 in 2029, versus the central $3.16/$3.50 deck.

A partial can be reasonable for an investor deliberately underwriting sustained modestly firmer gas/NGL prices and around $950m sustaining D&C per 4.3 Bcfe/day. It is not justified merely because the stock pulled back. The central return estimate of 7.9% is below the roughly 11.2% analytical cost of equity, and the historical constant-price book return is only around 8.9%. A weaker gas/capital case is $7.55; finite-inventory continuation is also materially less valuable than a perpetuity. These are the real reasons to keep any fundamental acceptance conditional. There is **no universal 15% discount rule** and no demand that the company disclose an exact maintenance line before analysis can be completed.

**Stage 3.5 is separate:** no actual entry, technical invalidation, trend confirmation, current market/sector filters, position size or stop has been established. A favorable DCF scenario does not establish a technical 3:1 trade.

## Replacing the maintenance-capital block with a defensible estimate

The historical block treated $1.2bn as maintenance while also charging growth capital. That is not the only defensible reading. February's explicit plan was **$900m maintenance D&C**, $100m additional capital from not using a drilling joint venture, and up to $200m discretionary growth. The present $1.2bn annual D&C includes extra pads intended to increase 2027 production. Conversely, the October guidance deck labels Q4's $325–350m as maintenance. Quarterly completion timing and the additional pads prevent treating four times that label as a settled perpetual replacement requirement. [February capital-plan disclosure](https://www.anteroresources.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/255/antero-resources-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-results-and); [October guidance label](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_69d22111deb87532f98551ac86e18027/anteroresources/db/732/7944/pdf/Guidance_Hedges_October_2026_vF.pdf); [October growth-pad discussion](https://www.anteroresources.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/262/antero-resources-announces-third-quarter-2026-financial-and)

The central assumption is **$1.00bn annual sustaining D&C at 4.3 Bcfe/day, in 2027 dollars**. $0.90–1.20bn is the main sensitivity range; $1.35bn is a tougher stress. These are **analyst estimates, not newly disclosed company guidance**. They are triangulated rather than inferred solely from depreciation:

| Crosscheck | Calculation | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Original maintenance plan | $900m × 4.3/4.1 × 1.02 = $963m | Adjusts stated 2026 plan to reference output and modest inflation; retained JV economics remain a caveat |
| Proved development economics | $2.3bn future PUD development / 4.671 Tcfe = $0.492/Mcfe | At 1.570 Tcfe annual output, about $773m before execution, timing, land and non-D&C reserves |
| Broader 2025 development burden | $678m / 1.262 Tcfe converted = $0.537/Mcfe | About $843m for equivalent reserve replacement; approximately $1.01bn with a 20% productivity/cost buffer |
| Decline and well requirement | 4.3 Bcfe/day × 22–28% decline / 14–18 MMcfe/day effective annual contribution | Roughly 53–86 net-equivalent wells × $12–15m = $0.63–1.29bn |
| Middle well case | 25% decline / 16 MMcfe/day × $13m | About $873m, or roughly $973m after a $100m execution/timing reserve |

The decline and effective-well-contribution ranges are analytical assumptions, **not measured AR decline curves**. The company reported five recent wells averaging 30 MMcfe/day over about 60 days; this selected short sample is deliberately not treated as a full-year portfolio type curve. 2025 development spending of $678m/61 net completed wells is about $11.1m per well, but DUC timing and lateral lengths differ; initial 2026 capital/well guidance implies roughly $12.5–14.3m. These observations bound the well-cost assumptions rather than prove them. Reserve conversion also includes prior DUC investment, reserve revisions and development timing; $457m direct conversion spend alone would understate the broader capital burden. [Annual report, drilling and reserve development schedules](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1433270/000110465926013386/ar-20251231x10k.htm); [Recent drilling results](https://www.anteroresources.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/262/antero-resources-announces-third-quarter-2026-financial-and)

The model separately includes $100m land per reference output, $25m other capital and $10m provision for retirement of newly developed assets, all inflated and appropriately scaled. Existing ARO is deducted as a claim, so its future settlement is not charged a second time. Growth capital funds the **following year's** incremental daily volume at $1bn per added Bcfe/day, based on the disclosed $200m potentially adding roughly 0.2 Bcfe/day. The higher 2027 starting production is chiefly funded by 2026 spending; it is not charged again in 2027. Base 2027 total capital is **$1,286m**, including $1,047m maintenance D&C, $105m land, $35m other/new ARO and $100m next-year growth. That is an explicit economic estimate, not a hidden reduction to zero maintenance.

## Discount rate, taxes and terminal discipline

The central **10% WACC** uses the November 3 Treasury ten-year yield of **4.95%**, an analyst 5% equity risk premium and 1.25 beta, giving about 11.2% cost of equity. Prospective debt cost of 6%, 25% normalized tax and roughly 80% operating equity/20% debt weights imply approximately 9.9%, rounded to 10%. Beta and ERP are explicit judgments, not verified vendor estimates. Joint cases use 9–11.5%, and the matrix extends to 12%. [Treasury daily curve](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2026&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve)

Cash taxes start at 15%/20% and reach 25%, reflecting documented NOLs/credits without assuming permanent tax exemption. All-years 25% lowers base value only to $32.03. No separate NOL or deferred-tax asset is added, and the $1.221bn deferred-tax liability is not also deducted as debt after future cash taxes are modeled. Working capital absorbs 3% of incremental physical sales, including release on a decline.

Ten explicit years are 2027–36, following a 58-day residual 2026 cash stub. September debt is retained: no unverified October cash generation or debt paydown is credited. Residual-stub assumptions are HH $3.75, C3+ $40, ethane $12 and oil $65, with 4.3 Bcfe/day production and Q4 midpoint D&C, giving $133.9m FCFF before interest. These are analyst estimates; no October earnings are invented. Year-end discounting is used. **FCFF = operating EBIT − cash operating tax + DD&A − full economic capital − change in working capital.** Interest and AM earnings/dividends are excluded from this operating FCFF.

Terminal growth is 2%, representing nominal inflation with flat real output, and terminal net reinvestment is the greater of NOPAT × g / 10% incremental ROIC and sustaining capital plus land/other/new ARO less DD&A. The base terminal year has $1,359.5m NOPAT, $271.9m net reinvestment and $1,087.6m FCFF. The economic-return requirement, not the lower $222.6m maintenance-only floor, binds. At $1.2bn maintenance the replacement floor becomes more important. The model never assumes growth without reinvestment.

Year-end 2025 reserves were **19.149 Tcfe**, including 14.478 developed and 4.671 undeveloped. Those precede HG and the Utica sale. Base ten-year production is **17.684 Tcfe**. An updated combined reserve audit is not yet available; no invented current reserve total is substituted. The 1,279 gross potential locations in the 10-K and October's 1,428 normalized 10,000-foot operated locations are different measures, not quantities that can be added. Maintaining production beyond the explicit period requires ongoing replacement and economically viable inventory. [Reserve and location disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1433270/000110465926013386/ar-20251231x10k.htm); [Current normalized location presentation](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_69d22111deb87532f98551ac86e18027/anteroresources/db/732/7949/pdf/3Q2026_Earnings_Call_Presentation_10.29.2026_vF.pdf)

The terminal component is 48.9% of operating EV. Replacing perpetuity with finite total 15/20/25/30-year continuation, retaining full capital and allowing no residual upstream value, gives **$21.05/$24.66/$27.13/$28.83**. These are transparent duration stresses, not engineered reserve-runoff forecasts. They demonstrate why a perpetuity-only upside is not sufficient evidence of low risk.

## Bridge from the earlier A132 conclusion

The earlier dossier's $19.38 central reference used $1.2bn maintenance plus separate growth capital, 25% tax from year one, a simplified gas hedge map and 310.047m gross-unvested shares. It acknowledged that those capital assumptions might double count growth, then made the uncertainty an approval block.

1. **Repair the analytical treatment:** use a source-bounded maintenance range rather than requiring an unavailable exact line. Under this new model, retaining $1.2bn maintenance gives about $24.19; moving to the triangulated $1.0bn raises it to $32.53. This within-model comparison isolates the major capital assumption.
2. **Rebuild rather than relabel:** prices, separate regional/option hedges, taxes, explicit production-linked costs and future savings, terminal reinvestment, the current AM mark and share denominator are revised and all coded. The difference from the old $19.38 to the new $1.2bn-maintenance case is a combined model revision, not falsely attributed to one item without the old executable.
3. **Strengthen history:** full annual/quarterly/TTM tables and a consistent recurring-price normalization are included. The new TTM $1,150.0m/$826.7m constant-deck EBIT differs from the old $1,169.0m/$852.6m because this method retains recurring contingency/termination expense and uses explicitly rounded disclosed volumes. Neither comparison establishes same-perimeter organic growth.
4. **Change the status, not the facts:** the research can be complete while the investment case remains conditional. The market-implied gas path is ordinary enough that a modestly stronger view can support a partial at today's price. It is still a cyclical, inventory-consuming business with a real adverse case, not a bond-like bargain.

## What would strengthen or invalidate the partial framework

- Strengthen: annual 2027 production and spending plan consistent with the $0.9–1.0bn reference sustaining range; continued output per dollar without borrowing growth from DUC inventory; incremental transport savings; broader realized-basis improvement; current reserve/acreage evidence supporting inventory duration; normalized returns improving above capital cost.
- Weaken: replacement needs above roughly $1.1–1.2bn before growth; sustained HH below the reverse-required path; weaker NGL/basis realization; material new royalty/environmental liabilities; inventory quality or debt refinancing disappointment.
- Next known information point: EIA November 10 forecast. Company 2027 budget and updated reserve information are future verification items, not reasons to mark today's completed scenario analysis unfinished.

The balanced-improvement value has about 11% price appreciation to its present estimate. The bear has roughly 81% valuation downside. That produces neither a guaranteed payoff nor a technical stop. A valid **3:1 technical entry** requires an independently justified target, invalidation level and current market/sector/trend setup. No stop is reverse-engineered from a DCF bear case, and no transaction has been placed.

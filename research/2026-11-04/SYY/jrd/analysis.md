# JRD transaction and historical cash-flow diligence
As of November 4, 2026. USD millions unless stated otherwise. This is a sourced input pack, not a price target or a probability-weighted recommendation.

## Result
The October 8 prospectus includes audited combined JRD financial statements for fiscal 2024 and 2025 and unaudited first-half 2026 comparatives. These support a standalone operating DCF with explicitly modeled assumptions, rather than requiring a valuation based solely on management EBITDA. They do not supply maintenance/growth capex separation, a complete future store-level capital schedule, or a final closing funds flow. Do not label a forecast fully verified simply because historical statements are available.

Key historical outputs (FY2025 / LTM June 2026): revenue 15,812.178 / 16,009.1701; EBIT 1,940.8745 / 2,073.3487; CFO 1,218.6781 / 1,421.6212; capex 137.2486 / 168.4653; CFO less capex 1,081.4295 / 1,253.1559. LTM is FY2025 minus H1 2025 plus H1 2026, not annualized H1. Source: prospectus combined statements F-5, F-8, F-24, F-27–28. Exact-component LTM sales differ from the rounded pro forma aligned sales table's 16,008; retain exact source components and flag rather than force a tie.

The announced approximately 1,900 FCF is **adjusted EBITDA less capex**, explicitly defined on the May 18 acquisition presentation slides 5–6. It is before interest, cash taxes, and working-capital investment. It is not distributable cash, GAAP CFO less capex, or after-tax unlevered cash flow. FY2025 cash taxes alone were 505.6449; external plus related-party cash interest totaled 283.3018. The presentation metric can describe low capital intensity but cannot be capitalized as FCFF.

## Perimeter and normalization
- Combined accounts include Warehouse Realty LLC alongside JRD and its subsidiaries; common ownership/management explains combination. Intercompany rent/accounts are eliminated. Owned realty is already economically represented by the operating cash flows and capex. Do not add a real-estate appraisal to the operating DCF without an explicit sale/leaseback adjustment that reduces operating value appropriately.
- FY2025 effective tax rate was 28.19%, versus 26.83% in 2024; H1 2026 rate 27.2%. Sysco's acquisition pro forma uses 27.5%. This is a reasonable transparent modeling anchor, not a guaranteed marginal/cash rate.
- Core working-capital cash contribution (receivables, inventory, prepaids, payables, accrued expenses) was -42.8978 in 2025, +106.4695 in H1 2026, and +13.2577 LTM. H1's strong cash conversion includes a 148.4003 payable inflow. Do not grow that inflow mechanically forever.
- FY2025 depreciation/amortization 67.4339 is below capex 137.2486. LTM D&A 66.4977 is below capex 168.4653. Forecast new stores and sustaining investment separately where possible; never assume all capex is discretionary or set capex equal to D&A solely to boost terminal FCF.
- External operating lease liabilities were 279.2655 at June 2026; FY2025 operating lease cash payments were 41.5979. Under an expense-based DCF, retain rent in EBIT/CFO and do not also subtract operating leases as debt. An alternative lease-capitalized approach requires both cash-flow and bridge changes.
- JRD uses LIFO. Sysco pro forma eliminates the 410.6775 reserve (rounded 411), with 28 current taxes payable and 85 deferred taxes. Pro forma annual EBIT benefits by 43 from FIFO alignment. This accounting adjustment is not a recurring free cash windfall; reconcile cash working capital and tax recapture.
- Post-acquisition purchase accounting introduces 91 annual depreciation and 626 intangible amortization while reversing 66 historical depreciation in the pro forma. Separate noncash purchase accounting from maintenance/growth capex. The intangible step-up does not automatically create cash tax deductions. Add back only expenses actually included in projected EBIT.
- There is an unexplained first-half margin/SG&A improvement in the raw statements: H1 2026 EBIT margin 13.37%, versus 12.02% H1 2025. The reviewed notes do not establish that every basis point is recurring. Preserve the reported fact, but use a margin sensitivity rather than silently treating H1 as a permanent run rate.

An illustrative operating formula is EBIT × (1 − normalized tax) + D&A − capex + cash contribution from operating working capital. That is only a starting point: distinguish tax-payable timing, lease adjustments, other operating balances, and non-operating income. A funded acquisition model also needs the parent's final financing, fees, dilution, and timing. Do not put financing interest into FCFF and then subtract the same financing through net debt as if both were operating costs.

## Consideration bridge and funding controls
Contract headline: 21,600 cash subject to adjustment plus **91.5 million new shares**. Purchase-price adjustments include cash, indebtedness, transaction expenses and net working capital. The prospectus's dated illustration explicitly bridges:

15,578 cash to holders + 5,812 JRD debt retired + 92 EAUs + 118 seller retention bonuses = 21,600.

The purchase-accounting consideration is 15,578 + 5,812 + 92 + 7,514 stock = 28,996. The 118 seller retention payment reduces seller cash and is treated outside accounting consideration; it does not increase total economics for Sysco. Separate buyer transaction costs 191 and transfer taxes 49 exist in the pro forma. These are estimates, not a final funds-flow statement.

At June 2026 historical third-party debt carrying amount was 4,638.6767, related-party shareholder notes 1,139.8955, total 5,778.5722. The pro forma repayment 5,812 is not the same as net carrying debt; avoid inventing a precise unexplained reconciliation. Historical seller debt should not be added on top of the gross 21,600 requirement and then subtracted again from combined equity value.

The prospectus expressly says its approximately 21,000 debt/hybrid and 1,000 equity funding assumptions do not reflect final financing. The parent's November 4 10-Q funding schedule supersedes them. Do not add an old bridge commitment to permanent debt. September's 12.345679 million financing shares are separate from the 91.5 million seller shares, and already-issued shares remain outstanding in a failure case absent an actual repurchase.

## Contract-verified economic bridge (supersedes any simplistic cash uplift)
The actual [merger agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/96021/000095014226000922/eh260755710_ex0201.htm) §2.11 specifies 19,308.75 OpCo plus 2,291.25 PropCo base cash value, totaling 21,600. Seller payment = base cash minus 60 adjustment escrow plus contractual Closing Cash minus Closing Indebtedness minus seller Transaction Expenses plus NWC adjustment. Sysco separately funds that 60 escrow under §2.13; it is not a discount. NWC targets are -103.2 for OpCo and -0.4 for PropCo.

Consequently the same acquired cash that remains on the balance sheet increases seller consideration. It is not a free increment to combined equity when deducting the 21,600 cash-free/debt-free base. A conceptual closing bridge is EV Sysco + EV JRD + net synergies - existing Sysco net debt - 21,600 - incremental costs/adjustments, divided by current shares +91.5 million, provided the existing Sysco net debt contains financing proceeds consistently and no acquisition use has already been deducted. Use the final funds flow to replace this base bridge when available. If financing cash has been excluded from current net debt, adding the acquisition use again can double count debt.

Contractual Cash is broader than balance-sheet cash: it includes restricted cash, cash collateral, card receivables, deposits and cash in transit, nets uncleared payments, excludes Closing In-Store Cash and contains a cap in a non-public disclosure letter. Therefore neither 283 unrestricted cash nor 283+42 is an exact contractual closing adjustment. The inaccessible cash cap/balance-sheet rules prevent a fully exact final bridge from public information alone.

Debt includes shareholder notes and other debt-like obligations, but excludes operating leases, derivatives, undrawn commitments and certain non-shareholder-note breakage/make-whole costs. Some debt repayment premiums can therefore be incremental buyer leakage rather than reductions of seller proceeds. §5.14(c) requires shareholder-note payoff; external borrowings may instead be amended/refinanced/retained. The prospectus assumes full repayment, but that is not a confirmed actual close outcome.

## Closing and failure cases
Latest primary 10-Q Note 4 still presents the acquisition as pending, with expected Q3 FY2027 close, subject to regulatory clearance. No primary closing/clearance announcement was found in this research. Financing completion is not acquisition completion.

The FTC issued second requests May 27. The October prospectus says the parties were responding. Secondary transcripts report substantial completion of responses on November 3, but this pack does not upgrade that to verified clearance or attach a new mandatory closing date. Even substantial compliance is not itself an approval.

Closing conditions include HSR waiting-period expiration/termination (and any timing agreement), no prohibiting injunction/law, effective S-4 with no stop order, NYSE listing authorization, covenant/representation conditions and tax opinions. S-4 effectiveness is already satisfied; seller approvals obtained. Expected calendar Q1 2027 close remains a forecast.

Outside date September 30, 2027, automatically extendable to March 30, 2028 if specified remaining regulatory conditions apply. The 1,164 reverse termination fee applies to specified regulatory/deadline failures, including other-condition qualifications. It is not automatically payable for every possible failure.

**Close case:** combine operating values with realty already inside JRD; include separately phased net cost synergies (management target 250 annualized by year three), implementation/retention costs and any remedies. Use actual final funds flow and share count. Neither 100% synergy realization nor unchanged store economics is established.

**Failure case:** no JRD operating cash flows, no seller 91.5 million shares and no seller purchase payment. Keep costs already incurred and financing shares already issued; model actual mandatory redemption terms, premiums, accrued interest, debt/hedge unwind and applicable termination fee. Do not simply revert to a pre-announcement balance sheet or charge the entire 21,600 as a loss. Distinguish regulatory failure from other termination paths. No probabilities assigned.

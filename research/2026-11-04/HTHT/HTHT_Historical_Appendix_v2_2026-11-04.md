## Correction and conclusion

The earlier report supplied three fiscal years and H1 but omitted the complete four-quarter reconstruction and numerical current/prior TTM comparison. This appendix supplies both, including matching prior-year quarters, consistent cash definitions and a symmetric normalized operating cash bridge. These were recoverable disclosures, not an irreducible information gap.

A separately defined normalized FCFF proxy, using identical tax and working-capital assumptions, improves from **3.896 to 5.238**, up **34.5%**.

**The existing valuation is not automatically changed by adding these comparisons.** The original model already incorporated the H1 operating improvement and forecasts 2027 FCFF of 5.262, close to the reconstructed TTM proxy, while imposing higher capital expenditure and closure cash requirements. It still yields central value **USD 40.11** and moderately firm value **USD 48.05**, versus USD 48.91. The historical deficiency is resolved. The original conditional reasonable-price watch, rather than preferred partial entry, remains. This evidence supports business quality without eliminating price, China/FX, lease or international-execution risks.

## 4. Headline earnings, unusual items and cash seasonality

H1 2026 EBIT rose 29.2%, from 2.869 to 3.706, while attributable net income fell 1.8%, from 2.438 to 2.394. The income-statement FX line moved from +0.574 to −0.117, a 0.691 swing, and reported tax rose by 0.226. Financial marks, FX and taxes therefore obscure some improvement in hotel operations. They are not recurring hotel cash in the operating DCF.

Q4 2024 had only 0.049 of parent net income on 0.693 of pretax income and 0.578 of tax expense, alongside investment and other effects. Growth off that base is not a sustainable run-rate. The cash-flow impairment adjustment also falls from 0.539 in prior TTM to 0.270 in current TTM. That decline contributes 0.269 to the improvement. The normalized EBIT bridge retains impairment rather than automatically adding it back. For context, imposing a common 0.400 impairment burden on both periods would produce normalized EBIT of 5.271 versus 6.996, growth of 32.7% rather than 38.9%. This diagnostic is not a second valuation deduction.

Seasonality is material. Q1 2026 CFO of 0.233 and cash after capex/SBC of −0.035 are followed by Q2 CFO of 3.408 and cash after capex/SBC of 3.062. The operating-asset/liability cash aggregate swings from −1.119 to +1.232. Q4 2025 receives +1.684 from the same aggregate. These figures include more than trade working capital. Across the TTM, the aggregate falls from +2.002 to +1.557, so higher annual CFO is not solely attributable to a larger aggregate working-capital release. Neither level is assumed to repeat forever.

## 5. Symmetric reported-to-normalized operating bridge

Annual lease information comes from the audited 20-F. H1 2026/H1 2025 details come from the [September 23, 2026 interim 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1483994/000110465926109789/htht-20260923xex99d1.htm). H1 2024/H1 2025 comparisons come from the [September 12, 2025 interim 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1483994/000141057825002265/htht-20250912xex99d1.htm). That older interim filing is necessary for a symmetric prior-TTM lease adjustment.

Normalized cash-lease EBIT = reported EBIT − other operating income − (operating cash rent − fixed operating lease expense) + finance-lease ROU amortization − full finance-lease cash service.

Finance-lease interest expense is not assumed to equal cash interest. In FY2025, finance-lease interest expense of 0.135, total cash service of 0.139 and separately disclosed principal of 0.057 do not establish identical expense and cash interest. The bridge uses disclosed full cash service once. It does not invent a quarterly split or deduct principal twice. Operating lease liabilities are likewise not subtracted again from equity when cash rent is included in the operating forecast.

## 6. Comparable normalized operating cash proxy

CFO less capex and SBC is useful, but does not remove financing income, net interest, tax timing or liability releases. The stricter comparison below uses the same assumptions in both periods:

1. Start with normalized cash-lease EBIT, retaining SBC.
2. Apply a **30% normal operating cash-tax assumption**, matching the original central model. This is not reported cash tax paid.
3. Add back group D&A excluding finance-lease ROU amortization, which was already replaced with full cash service.
4. Deduct total reported cash capex, including purchased intangibles.
5. Budget incremental operating capital at **5% of year-on-year TTM sales growth**, the original model's assumption, rather than perpetuating aggregate cash releases. Prior comparison revenue is TTM June 2024: 21.882 + 11.426 − 10.010 = 23.298, using the [Q2 2024 primary 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1483994/000110465924091033/tm2421903d2_ex99-1.htm) for H1 2023. The resulting capital budgets are 0.0494 and 0.11585.

**Normalized FCFF proxy: 3.896 versus 5.238, up 34.5%.** On a constant 322 million economic ADS denominator, these equal RMB 12.10 versus 16.27 per ADS. At the original valuation FX rate of RMB 6.67233 per USD, these translate to approximately USD 1.81 versus 2.44. Fixed FX is used only for this illustration. Historical RMB growth is not manufactured by translating periods at different rates.

This proxy is not company-reported FCFF. It explicitly uses normalized tax and capital assumptions, while the earlier cash diagnostic reflects actual cash-flow timing. A 25–35% tax range yields prior/current normalized FCFF of 4.153/5.595 at 25% and 3.639/4.882 at 35%. Treatment remains symmetric.

**Capital-payable uncertainty:** the supplemental cash-flow disclosure reports “purchases of property and equipment included in payables” of 0.410/0.343/0.308 for H1 2024/25/26. Its caption does not independently establish a complete roll-forward of unpaid capital balances. Therefore the main comparison does **not** automatically treat the differences as changes in capital payables. A conditional alternative, if those amounts represent comparable end-period balances, would reduce prior/current economic capex by 0.067/0.035, yielding 0.776/0.723 of accrued capex and 3.963/5.273 of FCFF. Those are an explicitly conditional timing sensitivity, not a confirmed reconciliation. The source ledger and model retain the amounts so this can be updated if a fuller disclosure is obtained.

Current TTM cash capex of 0.758 is below the original model's 2027 cash budget of 1.30, analytically split into 0.80 maintenance and 0.50 growth. The issuer does not disclose that precise split. The model also deducts 0.30 of initial closure cash. Those requirements explain why forecast 2027 FCFF of 5.262 need not grow materially above the reconstructed TTM proxy despite operating improvement. This appendix does not lower capital or closure budgets to raise fair value.

## 7. ADS, currency, geography and legal claims

- **One Nasdaq ADS represents ten ordinary shares**, verified on the 20-F cover. Hong Kong 1179 is the same Cayman holding-company equity quoted in HKD, not a second equity pool. Comparison with USD per ADS requires both the 10:1 ratio and currency conversion.
- June 30 outstanding ordinary shares are **3,154,059,870**, or 315.405987 million ADS equivalents, excluding 78,984,260 treasury shares. Issued shares of 3,233,044,130 are not the economic outstanding count.
- The interim equity roll-forward records **133,496,890 ordinary shares** from conversion, or 13.349689 million ADS equivalents. The note rounds this to 13 million ADS and identifies USD 499.6 million of principal converted at May 1 maturity, with USD 0.4 million repaid. The old USD 500 million convertible is no longer debt. Its conversion must not be added again to June's outstanding shares.
- The original 322 million valuation ADS allowance is a forward economic proxy for June's outstanding count plus existing options/RSUs. It differs from the historical weighted 326.262 million ADS equivalents in H1 EPS, which reflects the pre-conversion period and convertible-dilution convention. Future SBC remains an expense, without automatically charging future dilution again for the same compensation. This is not an exact November share census.
- The USD 0.87 per ADS dividend declared in August and subsequent RMB bond proceeds are subsequent capital events, not TTM operating revenue. The original equity bridge adds both cash and debt for new borrowing and deducts the dividend once. Neither the converted debt nor the distribution should be deducted again.
- All operating comparisons are in **RMB**, not the issuer's convenience USD translations at different quarterly rates. The original model's RMB 6.67233 per USD is a November 3 valuation input, not the June 30 reporting translation. China and international currency exposures remain relevant to scenarios.
- Beginning in 2026, the issuer uses HWC/HWI geographic terminology instead of Legacy-Huazhu/Legacy-DH. Those segment labels are not silently spliced into a single organic-growth series. Consolidated accounts and the leased/owned and manachised/franchised categories reconcile. Same-hotel RevPAR, FX and hotel openings remain separate evidence.
- The 20-F identifies contractual affiliates/VIEs for restricted activities but quantifies combined revenue, net profit and assets at **less than 1%** for 2024/25. It also says those entities do not support material revenue in other subsidiaries. Describing the entire hotel business as a VIE would overstate that specific exposure. PRC regulation, remittance, convertibility, enforceability, depositary costs and holding-company claims remain. This finding does not make all onshore cash freely distributable.

The checked [issuer release calendar](https://ir.hworld.com/news-and-events/press-releases) and [events page](https://ir.hworld.com/news-and-events/events-calendar) did not provide a confirmed November 4–6 earnings date at the review cutoff. “Not announced in checked primary sources” is the correct statement, not a guarantee of no event. Refresh this at Stage 3.5 or when Q3 is announced.

Growth above 15% in normalized total earnings is a prioritization reference under the plan, not a universal minimum or automatic approval. Normalized operating profit and cash growth exceed that reference in this period but benefit partly from lower impairment and franchise mix. This operating-profit bridge is not a claim that parent net earnings will compound at the same rate. Corrected history strengthens the business case while leaving the original valuation, conditional reasonable-price watch and all Stage 3.5 requirements intact.

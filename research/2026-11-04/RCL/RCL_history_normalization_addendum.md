# RCL: historical normalization and cash-conversion addendum

**Research cutoff: November 4, 2026.** This completes the historical evidence omitted from the original Stage 3 review: three full fiscal years, the latest four quarters, current versus prior TTM, a pre-pandemic/downturn/recovery cycle, and a forward bridge. It supplements the existing ten-year model, reverse valuation, capital-claims analysis and scenario report. **No original DCF parameter or output has been silently changed.** The original bear/base/bull values remain **$73.30/$212.44/$363.83 per share**. These are scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals or an entry approval.

The added evidence makes the cash-conversion caution more concrete: TTM core operating profit rose 14.5%, but cash flow after capex fell 25.8%. Separately valued joint-venture cash in the original model is an improvement assumption, not already realized recurring cash. Neither finding reverses the existing valuation conclusion at the $313.46 November 3 scanner reference. That reference is not represented as a verified executable or November 4 quote.

## 1. Periods and definitions

RCL has a December fiscal year-end. Current TTM ends September 30, 2026; prior TTM ends September 30, 2025. The latest four reported quarters run October 2025 through September 2026. [Latest quarterly filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000044/rcl-20260930.htm).

All amounts are USD millions, except per-share values and percentages. Positive cash capex means an outflow. Historical CFO less capex is after interest and working capital; it is not enterprise FCFF. Subtracting SBC from this cash measure recognizes compensation economically because CFO adds SBC back. In contrast, **core EBIT, NOPAT and the core cash calculation already retain SBC: no second SBC deduction is made there**. Core cash before working capital = core EBIT × 98% + reported D&A − actual cash capex. The 2% tax rate is the valuation model’s stated assumption, not historical cash taxes. This diagnostic excludes changes in working capital, financing flows and non-operating income. It neither assumes all capex is maintenance nor replaces the ten-year ship-commitment model.

Quarterly income figures use the filed quarter where available. Quarterly CFO, capex and SBC are cumulative differences. Q4 is full year less nine months. Current TTM = FY2025 + 9M2026 − 9M2025; prior TTM = FY2024 + 9M2025 − 9M2024. Source statements round to millions, so direct quarterly rows can differ from cumulative-derived TTM by $1–2m. The data preserves these differences rather than creating fictitious precision. Twenty consistency checks pass within that disclosed rounding tolerance.

“Core EBIT” adds back only the disclosed operating restructuring/initiative expenses and operating asset impairment/credit-loss adjustments, while retaining ordinary depreciation, Silversea amortization and SBC. These small adjustments are analytical comparability choices, not a claim that restructuring never repeats. For FY2023/FY2024/FY2025, the bridge is reported EBIT plus restructuring **5/10/8** plus operating impairment and credit adjustments **8/9/0**. The 2024 $124m Havana Docks contingency release was **below operating profit**, so it is not subtracted again from EBIT. [FY2025 annual statement and reconciliation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000007/rcl-20251231.htm).

The normalized NI diagnostic uses the issuer’s adjusted **total net income**, not EPS: $1,827m/$3,237m/$4,286m. The corresponding annual net adjustments are +$130m/+$360m/+$18m. Among them, debt extinguishment/inducement costs are $121m/$463m/$16m, and the 2024 $124m litigation benefit is removed. Other adjustments include Silversea amortization, equity-investment impairments/recoveries, disposal gains and identified tax items, as detailed in the issuer reconciliation. These adjustments retain SBC. This NI series includes equity-method results and financing costs; it is a cross-check, **not** the unlevered cash flow to which separately valued JV equity is added.

Explicit reconstruction of the operating normalization: prior TTM core EBIT = **4,125 + 3,984 − 3,493 = 4,616**; current TTM = **4,918 + 4,351 − 3,984 = 5,285**. The respective issuer-adjusted NI calculations are **3,237 + 3,523 − 2,794 = 3,966** and **4,286 + 3,865 − 3,523 = 4,628**. Thus the growth conclusion is not merely lower shares lifting EPS. [FY2025 annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000007/rcl-20251231.htm); [Q3 2025 comparative filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488725000148/rcl-20250930.htm); [Q3 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000044/rcl-20260930.htm).

SBC fell from $294m to $133m in the two TTM windows. That $161m reduction accounts arithmetically for about 24% of the $672m reported EBIT increase, so the increase should not all be extrapolated as pricing or capacity growth. No SBC add-back is included in the DCF earnings base. On unchanged SBC expense alone, the arithmetic increase in EBIT would be $511m, still positive; this is a decomposition, not a revised forecast.

Cash capex rose $1,113m, outpacing the $588m CFO increase. In 9M2026, the six reported working-capital lines consumed about $246m versus $53m in 9M2025. Customer-deposit inflows increased to $295m from $108m, but receivables, prepaid assets, payables and accruals more than offset that benefit. CFO also includes JV cash distributions and interest, so it cannot be substituted directly for core FCFF and then supplemented with the same JV value. [Cash-flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000044/rcl-20260930.htm).

These are actual consolidated cash-flow line items, not earnings. The prior TTM JV dividend amount is $265m; current TTM is $310m, below TTM equity-method income of $429m. Loans advanced are not necessarily permanent maintenance reinvestment, but ignoring them would overstate the cash currently reaching RCL. [FY2025 cash-flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000007/rcl-20251231.htm); [9M2026 cash-flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000044/rcl-20260930.htm).

The original JV model starts at **$350m net annual cash**, grows it 5%/3%, and discounts at 11%, producing approximately $4.75bn of separate equity value. Its starting cash is 40.6% above observed $249m net TTM cash, or 12.9% above the $310m gross dividends. It is a forward improvement assumption that must be earned. Holding the other inputs unchanged but starting at $250m reduces JV value to approximately $3.393bn and base RCL value to **$207.38**, approximately $5.06 below the original $212.44. Core EBIT excludes equity-method income, and parent capital needs must not be counted both in this net distribution estimate and a second JV reinvestment deduction.

The pandemic shutdown and reopening make a mechanical seven-year average a poor definition of normal earnings. This complete 2019–2025 revenue/profit/cash sequence is included to show the real severity of the operating and financing cycle, not to assume another shutdown every seven years or to treat 2023–2025 recovery growth as perpetual. Fleet capacity, pricing, costs and capital structure changed substantially. The source-indexed SEC facts accompanying this report retain exact historical periods and filing accessions.

The original normalized starting EBIT of $5.50bn is 4.1% above the reconstructed $5.285bn core TTM figure. **The model then grows that anchor by 9% for forecast year 2027**, producing $5.995bn EBIT and $5.875bn NOPAT, about 13.4% above core TTM EBIT. Therefore $5.50bn must not be described as already being the forecast-year EBIT. The existing near-term earnings guidance is a management outlook; the model’s 2027 operating profit and reinvestment are analyst assumptions. This distinction matters more than rounding in reported quarters.

Ship commitments remain binding economic reinvestment: the original model uses the larger of growth/ROIC reinvestment or its annual capex floor, rather than adding both. Its 2027–2030 RCL net-reinvestment floors are approximately $2.132bn/$4.463bn/$3.096bn/$1.505bn. The current operating recovery does not make those cash outflows optional. The future Sandals investment is still a separate contingent transaction, not already consolidated operating profit or drawn debt.

There is also a valuation-date approximation in the original annual model: September balance-sheet claims are paired with full 2027–2036 cash flows and no explicit remaining-2026 stub. An incremental $0.5bn of net cash generated or consumed before the first modeled year would move equity value by roughly **$1.87/share**, before time discounting. This is a transparent bridge sensitivity, not a fabricated Q4 cash forecast; the annual model is an economic range, not an exact dated settlement-price calculation.

## 7. Decision and confidence

The historical/normalization requirement is now supplied. Evidence confidence is high for reported statements and moderate for normalization choices; valuation confidence remains constrained by cruise cyclicality, future pricing, fleet reinvestment, tax and discount-rate assumptions, and JV cash conversion. The original model still requires a much stronger combined outcome than the base scenario to justify the scanner reference. The reasonable-price/partial-entry framework remains applicable if price and jointly supported assumptions warrant it; no universal 15% discount is imposed. Stage 3.5 technical entry, position sizing, risk budget and execution conditions remain separate. **This addendum does not approve a trade.**

# Freshpet Q3 2026: post-release operating evidence

Prepared November 4, 2026, after the 06:30:34 EST release and before the 08:00 EST conference call. The call and Q&A have not occurred. This is an operating evidence update, not a DCF, valuation conclusion, trade or publication. Source availability was checked at approximately 11:38 UTC. Public Q3 prepared remarks and slides are downloaded; no Q3 10-Q or earnings 8-K was yet listed in the issuer results page or SEC submissions registry.

## Changes that matter

1. **Demand remains above reported shipments, but household expansion is slower.** Q3 net sales of $317.840m rose 10.0%, entirely volume-driven. Management reports 12.7% measured retail dollar growth including Costco, plus approximately 1–2 points from unmeasured channels. Reported sales face the previously disclosed greater-than-two-point club/July 4 comparison and a newly disclosed 1–2-point lag because a major customer's inventory grew more slowly than its sales. This describes inventory growth below sales growth, not proof of outright destocking. Retail consumption volume was up 14%, versus 15% in each of Q1 and Q2.
2. **Spend and MVP mix do more of the work.** The latest 52-week panel has 15.5m buying households versus 15.3m, rounded growth of 1%. MVPs are 2.6m versus 2.4m, up 8%, with $522 annual buy rate and 72% of sales. Total buy rate is $122 versus $112, up 9%. Q2's rolling panel showed 15.9m households and 5% growth; the different windows and estimates do not establish a same-cohort churn rate. The narrower household growth is nevertheless an important question for the call.
3. **Manufacturing improves materially; SG&A offsets some gains.** GAAP gross margin is 44.5% versus 39.5%; adjusted is 50.0% versus 46.0%. Better plant leverage, quality and input costs are the stated drivers, with only a limited technology benefit. Input costs are 28.9% of sales, down 20bp; quality costs 1.6%, down 50bp. Adjusted SG&A rises to 29.6% from 27.1% because of logistics and variable compensation. Media is 10.8% versus 11.2%. GAAP operating income is $35.130m; adjusted EBITDA $64.542m. Do not treat 50% adjusted GM or 20.3% adjusted EBITDA margin as GAAP profitability.
4. **Capex is deferred, not permanently eliminated.** FY2026 guidance falls from approximately $150m to $125m–$150m, still primarily capacity. Management says operating improvements unlocked more than $100m of capacity, equivalent to another line, allowing some planned investment to move later. YTD cash capex is $80.259m; quarterly $22.935m is unusually low relative to the $44.741m–$69.741m implied Q4 range. No maintenance/expansion split is disclosed.
5. **2027 headline targets are unchanged, but the earnings caveat is new.** Sales growth target remains above category, adjusted GM at least 49%, and adjusted EBITDA margin 20%–22%. Prepared remarks say the margin would be in the lower half absent significant improvement in inflation. That is not equivalent to fresh confidence in the midpoint or high end. Selective 2027 price increases are planned for a small number of items; ingredients and packaging inflation are described as more structural.

Sources: [Release](https://investors.freshpet.com/news-releases/news-release-details/freshpet-inc-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results); [Prepared remarks, pages 5–7](https://investors.freshpet.com/static-files/c7c74ec5-1ed3-410b-8411-deca2f2f769e); [Deck, slides 6, 10–11, 19–24](https://investors.freshpet.com/static-files/f82d5c97-c5cc-4996-bcf7-5114ee716771).

Therefore Q3's peak adjusted margin is not a clean Q4 run rate.

## Distribution, competition and innovation

September 30 network totals are 31,164 stores and 40,523 fridges; 25% of US/Canadian stores have multiple fridges. Store growth is 5%, fridge count growth 4%, fridge cubic capacity growth 5%, and total distribution points growth 14%. Management describes expanded rural retail distribution, testing three-door fridges with a major club customer, and added SKUs at two major club customers. These customers are not named in the prepared remarks.

Ecommerce grows 35% and reaches 16.9% of business, with 82% fulfilled through the retail fridge network. These overlap with store infrastructure; do not add digital and retail network business as exclusive segments. Homestyle Creations exceeded $100m of annual **retail** sales, not issuer net sales. Management describes better feedback and volume for Fresh from the Kitchen after conversion to the new bag technology but does not disclose a controlled quantified product-level margin or repeat-purchase bridge.

NIQ omnichannel share of US dog food and treats is 4.5%, up from Q2's 4.3% observation. This is share of the broad dog-food-and-treats category, not fresh-category share. The materials assert that product breadth outsells new competitors and that competition is unusually intense. They do not provide named competitor sales, matched-distribution velocity, or causal share-loss estimates. The earlier primary launch evidence for Hill's, Blue Buffalo, Purina and Nom Nom remains relevant; this release does not independently resolve their success. Management's 10m MVP/36m household and future $10bn fresh-category opportunities remain proprietary estimates, not new externally verified market size.

## Capacity, technology and logistics

There are still 16 production lines: Bethlehem 7, South 4, Ennis 5. Projected configuration remains 24-plus: 7, 7 and 10-plus, without a newly specified completion schedule. Three bag lines use the new technology, one full and two lite. Remarks describe more than 100bp full-business annualized adjusted GM benefit from the three installed lines and a substantial portion in 2027. They later use stronger wording about capturing substantially all annualized benefits. Do not assume the entire 100bp is incremental to 2026, because approximately 35bp is already expected in FY2026.

A dedicated leased Bethlehem distribution center will replace a third-party Allentown center in early 2027. Management says it needs little issuer capex and brings cold-chain operations closer to the kitchen. This can improve service/control, but low capex is not zero economic investment: rent, lease commitments, transition costs and automation spending need the filing/call. Logistics pressure is now projected at $10m–$12m over the initial FY plan, primarily fuel and line-haul capacity/rates.

## Governance, capital, awards and tax

Cyr remains CEO. Prepared remarks confirm Morris has moved to an advisory role and Baty is President and COO; this is not a CEO transition. No new employee award inventory or revised convertible terms appear in the release/deck/remarks. Retain the dated FY2025/H1 awards and October 58,245 retention-RSU grant only as historical evidence until a Q3 filing refreshes them.

September cash is $345.860m, convertible carrying value $399.007m, and finance-lease liabilities $28.539m. Release common shares outstanding are 48.329m, rounded to thousands. Q3 basic/diluted weighted averages are 48.426m/54.742m, not current outstanding shares and not a capped-call-adjusted equity denominator. Repurchases executed through September are $102.3m and 1.8m shares, rounded. No assumption is made about October activity or current shares.

Q3 CFO is $60.762m, down $6.000m year over year; FCF is $37.827m, up $6.267m, because capex drops more than CFO. YTD CFO $145.516m and FCF $65.257m should not obscure that quarterly distinction. Net DTA falls to $42.924m after subtracting the $0.158m DTL. The release does not provide remaining NOL principal, utilization limitations, a Q3 cash-tax disclosure or the cause of the 13.69% GAAP tax rate. Do not infer cash taxes from total tax expense minus deferred expense, and do not add DTA to equity if tax benefits already enter projected cash flow. Detailed accounting validation is in `accounting/`.

## Primary-source inconsistencies to retain

- Prepared remarks page 6 says net income $27.9m. The release's statement says $29.712m and deck says $29.7m. Use the financial statement, and preserve the remarks error rather than averaging.
- Remarks page 6 puts prior logistics at 5.5% versus current 7.1%; deck slide 6 labels the increase 170bp, implying roughly 5.4%. Exact reconciliation is unresolved and may reflect rounding or an error.
- Technology scope mismatch persists: remarks page 4 references three already-installed lines; deck slide 15 footnote references lines installed by end-2027. This could change capital/timing assumptions and should be asked explicitly.
- CFO D&A remains above the issuer EBITDA D&A: Q3 $23.791m versus $23.078m, a $0.713m difference. The prior finance-lease interpretation remains an inference, not management-confirmed policy. Separately, issuer EBITDA is $0.062m below GAAP EBIT plus issuer D&A because a residual other expense is not removed by the net-interest adjustment. Use direct GAAP EBIT rather than forcing an EBITDA-derived plug.
- The deck's embedded filename/title includes “draft,” but the issuer's official quarterly-results and home pages link it as the Q3 earnings presentation. Use it as published issuer material and note identified errors; do not imply it was obtained privately.

## Questions for the future call / filing

1. Why does total household growth now round to 1%, and how much is panel methodology/window versus acquisition/retention? Is MVP growth replacing declining occasional buyers?
2. How much customer inventory catch-up is embedded in Q4 guidance, and when does the club/July 4 comparison stop affecting growth?
3. Which cost/price assumptions put 2027 EBITDA margin in the lower half, and what elasticity/competitive response is assumed for selective increases?
4. What exact lines and installation dates support the 100bp technology benefit? What is 2027 incremental benefit after FY2026's 35bp?
5. Which capex projects moved out of 2026, into which year, and what is recurring replacement/maintenance versus added capacity and fridges?
6. What are the new DC lease commitment, start-up expense, overlap cost and expected savings? Are those already included in 2027 targets?
7. What portion of variable compensation and SBC is normal or a catch-up? Refresh employee options, strikes, RSUs, repurchases and cover-page common shares separately from convertibles.
8. Reconcile operating D&A/finance leases and disclose remaining NOLs, current cash-tax payments and the Q3 tax-rate drivers.

## Decision and valuation

The primary segment FCFF model gives **$346/share** in the base case. A central range is approximately **$305–395**, using normalized year-five refining EBITDA of $9–11 per throughput barrel and discount rates of 9.5–10.5% for MPC excluding MPLX and 8–9% for MPLX. These are present-value estimates, not future targets. Confidence is **medium in the direction of the price decision, medium-low in the exact fair value**, because future refining spreads and the taxable economics of MPLX are uncertain. Reported financial evidence has high confidence.

Separate stress cases are **$100 bear / $346 base / $773 bull**. Do not call that entire stress span the fair-value range. The bull case captures a much longer high-margin refining regime and stronger midstream growth; its upside is real in the model, not the central conclusion.

At $427.31, price is 23.6% above base value. The plan's margin-of-safety definition, (value minus price)/value, is **−23.6%**; the modeled return to base value, value/price minus one, is **−19.1%**. These are different denominators. A **25% discount to base value gives an approximately $260 conditional review price**, justified by commodity cyclicality, regulatory exposure and sale/reinvestment uncertainty. This is not a buy limit or stop, and the discount is applied to the base case, not stacked on the bear case. Stage 3.5 would still require updated price, structure, signal, risk budget and sizing.

## Business and the source of earnings

MPC combines a large U.S. refining and marketing system with approximately 64% ownership of MPLX. Refining benefits from scale, access to North American crude, complex processing and integrated logistics, but its product is economically cyclical. MPLX adds more stable infrastructure earnings and distributions, while carrying its own debt and public minority ownership. Renewable diesel depends partly on regulatory credits.

The drawdown is observed; a single definitive cause is not established by the reviewed disclosures. The available business evidence shows stronger, not deteriorating, current operations. Concern that exceptional refining spreads will normalize is a valuation hypothesis rather than a verified explanation for every day's price change.

The November results remove the old reason to defer research. The A119 review had deliberately left mid-cycle earnings, MPLX ownership and maintenance capital unresolved. This is the first completed segment valuation in that sequence, not a retrospective justification of the older scanner's roughly $750–770 indicative fields.

## MPLX ownership and economic treatment

MPC held 647m of 1,013m MPLX common units, **63.8697%** using rounded reported unit counts. The model values MPLX operations, subtracts MPLX debt and other claims, adds only its excess cash and its net disposal proceeds, and then multiplies the residual equity by 647/1,013. It never adds full MPLX cash to parent cash, never grants MPC 100% of midstream earnings, and never subtracts consolidated debt after adding an already-net MPLX stake.

MPLX Q3 adjusted EBITDA attributable to its LP was $1.859bn; TTM was $7.167bn. Its $1.537bn distributable cash flow is not FCFF because growth investments are not all deducted. Quarterly growth/maintenance spending including affiliate contributions was about $1.039bn, and the 2026 growth-capital outlook increased to $2.9bn, plus roughly $0.3bn maintenance capital. MPLX's distribution increased to $1.2111/unit, implying about $3.136bn annual MPC receipts at the reported $784m quarterly amount. That distribution is not added again to FCFF or stake value. [MPLX Q3 release](https://ir.mplx.com/CorporateProfile/press-releases/news-release/2026/MPLX-LP-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results-Announces-Increase-to-Quarterly-Distribution-of-12-5/default.aspx)

MPC's R&M earnings already bear logistics fees paid to MPLX. Those fees remain as an expense in refining while MPLX's economics are valued separately. MPLX's adjusted EBITDA substitutes affiliate distributions/adjustments for some equity-accounted earnings. The model consistently treats that as an economic cash EBITDA basis and includes affiliate investment in capex, rather than independently adding affiliate book value.

To avoid making SBC free, the MPLX starting EBITDA deducts the full annualized Q3 $48m “other” addback, a conservative $192m economic-cost reserve containing SBC and other adjustments. Then it removes $83.333m disposed EBITDA. The resulting $7.1607bn annual starting EBITDA is an analyst normalization, not a reported forecast. The already-retained $77m incentive-compensation expense is not separately stripped out. MPC corporate/segment compensation also remains an expense.

MPLX is a partnership. This model values its cash flows on a **25% corporate-tax-equivalent basis for MPC's taxable ownership**, not as if MPC's distributions were tax-free. The exact tax basis, depreciation shield and timing are not publicly resolved by a simple book model. A zero-owner-tax upper-bound sensitivity raises the total MPC base value to $400.70, still without a normal cyclical margin of safety at $427.31. This tax sensitivity is more informative than pretending the owner's tax position is known precisely.

## Exact FCFF construction and assumptions

There are **ten full forecast years, 2027–2036**, plus a Q4 2026 stub, anchored to September 30 reported balance data. The model does not pretend the 35-day difference between balance date and scanner observation is a fully marked-to-market roll-forward. Discounting begins from the balance anchor; year-end factors are 0.25, 1.25 through 10.25 years. This small timing limitation is far below the scenario uncertainty.

MPC excluding MPLX: R&M EBITDA = daily total throughput × 365 × EBITDA/barrel. Add renewable-diesel EBITDA and other owned midstream income, deduct corporate cost and recurring turnarounds, then deduct depreciation to get EBIT. NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − tax). **FCFF = NOPAT + D&A − economic capex − change in operating working capital.** Financing interest is excluded from FCFF; debt is subtracted in the equity bridge.

## Reverse valuation and cross-checks

At the same discount rates and cash/debt bridge, matching $427.31 requires either:

- Increasing the whole base refining margin path, including the stub, by **$2.729/barrel**: 2027 $26.73, year-five $12.73 and year-ten $14.05; or
- Keeping the early forecasts and stub fixed but raising the year-five-and-beyond normalized margin from $10 to approximately **$14.00/barrel** before subsequent nominal growth.

These are alternative paths, not independent validation. The required structural margin is achievable in a favorable industry regime, but not justified simply by a 95 Opportunity score or the exceptionally strong latest quarter.

At base operating assumptions, lowering the two WACCs to 8.5%/7.0% gives approximately $438 at base terminal growth; raising them to 11.5%/10.0% gives approximately $287. The full nine-point rate/growth grid spans about $285–468. This broader rate sensitivity is not the central fair-value range.

Using the September 30 market value of MPC's MPLX units, $36.46bn, instead of the corporate-tax-equivalent DCF stake gives a total check of approximately **$387/share**. It is a dated, market-dependent cross-check, not the primary valuation or an independently current quote. Removing all operating/WC reserves raises base value to about **$366/share**. The conclusion is therefore not driven solely by those cash reserves.

The latest four quarterly EPS sum to $51.17, implying about **8.35×** trailing P/E at the scanner price, compared with 32.3× 2025 EPS. That apparent cheapness is exactly why cycle normalization matters. Base parent EV is approximately 8.6× the year-five EBITDA **after recurring turnarounds**, not a peak-quarter annualized multiple.

For industry context, PBF's Q3 gross refining margin less refining opex is roughly $26.76 per throughput barrel before corporate and other scope differences. DINO's reported refining adjusted EBITDA is about $42.53 per throughput barrel, but approximately $32.94 after a $582m RIN-waiver reversal embedded in its adjusted numbers. - **Expected year-end 2026:** HSR and closing of the refinery/logistics sale. Watch actual tax proceeds, retained liabilities, commercial agreements and capex redeployment. No clearance or completed sale is assumed as a fact.
- **April 2027–July 2029:** SPR return obligations and hedges become cash/physical settlement events. A price spike or mismatched hedge adds risk beyond the booked amount.
- **Through 2027:** Gulf Coast project execution, MPLX infrastructure spending and subsequent utilization. Growth must produce incremental cash returns, not merely higher EBITDA financed by recurring new debt.
- **Every quarter:** refining spreads, utilization/capture, turnaround schedule, cash taxes, net working capital, MPLX leverage/coverage and organic growth. A sustained normalization above roughly $14/barrel would support a higher valuation than this base; a rapid move toward $5–6, lower utilization or poorer reinvestment returns would undermine it.
- **California:** potential inventory and resupply rules, environmental costs and demand transition. The CEC currently says the minimum-inventory framework is being assessed and that it deprioritized the gross-margin penalty in August 2025. Do not describe a new implemented penalty as established. [CEC current explanation](https://www.energy.ca.gov/programs-and-topics/topics/petroleum-fuels-transition/faq-california-gas-prices-and-impacts-iran)

Irreducible public-data uncertainty includes asset tax basis, final working-capital adjustments, precise refinery carve-out profits, future owner tax depreciation and the duration of war-related supply disruption. These are modeled and tested rather than hidden behind a false precision label. No earnings-date blocker remains. Next formal review: transaction closing or the next earnings release, earlier if spreads, policy or capital allocation materially changes. No future earnings date is invented.

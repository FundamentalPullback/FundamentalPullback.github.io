Six complex refineries and integrated logistics create crude-slate flexibility and regional supply advantages. They do not create control over product/crude spreads. Limited replacement capacity and regional shortages can sustain strong returns; outages, demand destruction, import response, higher feedstock costs and RIN/carbon costs can remove them. Operational complexity is valuable only if reliability and sustaining capital preserve it. Model conventions and reproduction

Run python pbf_history.py, then python pbf_normalized_model.py, then python write_pbf.py in the companion correction folder. The model imports the preserved refiners_core_original.py. JSON contains full inputs, annual calculations, original alternate, direct/reverse/joint scenarios and shareholder-flow estimates. No outside package is needed for valuation; the source extractor uses lxml.

Operating EBITDA = volume × (gross refining margin − refinery cash opex) − refinery SG&A + other consolidated segment EBITDA − corporate cost − incremental RIN stress. EBIT deducts economic D&A. FCFF = EBIT − cash tax + D&A − sustaining cash − growth capex − working-capital investment − special cash costs. SBC remains an expense. Equity-method earnings are excluded from operating EBIT and replaced with cash distributions after JV taxes, debt service and investment, valued at a separate equity discount rate. Owned logistics businesses are included once.

Taxes equal the stated normalized rate on positive EBIT, without immediate tax refunds in loss years. The Q4 bridge starts from September 30 cash/debt, rolls known financing actions, and includes the full unreported quarter; it does not claim to know actual November cash. Q4 is discounted 0.16 years and annual cash flows 1.16–10.16 years. Opening income-tax payables are settled in the stub, rather than assumed to be permanent financing. Working capital is an additional use of $100m in 2027, $50m in 2028 and roughly $25m plus inflation thereafter. These amounts cover normalized growth/inventory needs, not a prediction of volatile quarter-to-quarter oil-price collateral.

Terminal NOPAT grows at g. Terminal net reinvestment is the greater of NOPAT × g/ROIC and any recurring sustaining-capex excess over D&A, with the incremental capex stress included. Terminal FCFF cannot be manufactured by removing turnarounds. The terminal value is floored at zero if continuing operations produce negative terminal cash; this is not a claim that closure is free. Base real physical volume is flat and other-segment EBITDA grows 2% nominally. Cost inflation is 2%. Holding-company debt/finance leases and legacy capital claims are then reconciled to equity. Operating leases are not deducted again because rent remains in operating costs. There is no automatic multiple uplift, no acquisition growth for free and no perpetual subsidy extrapolation.

Valuation consequence and decision

The old 2027 margin of $23 is close to the latest nine-month $22.96 but above full-TTM $19.98 and much higher than 2023–25’s $16.07/$7.89/$8.77. It is a firmer-cycle view, not a neutral historical fact. The revised central starts at $19.98 and fades, while keeping volume, tax, maintenance, claims, shares and the previously estimated Q4 stub unchanged. That isolates the forecast correction. Reducing the stub margin from $30 to $20 as well gives $61.06/7.6%, demonstrating that the central still credits near-term strength.

The original $73.08/11.0% path remains an explicit alternative. The revised ordinary central is $66.16/8.9%; a modestly firmer persistent margin path reaches $77.61/12.4%, near the 12.15% analytical equity hurdle, and a constructive combined margin/cost/logistics/WACC case reaches $85.41/13.9%. The latter is not a certainty or a probability-weighted expected return. A reasonable-price partial therefore has an intelligible conditional case, but the full audit does not establish an unconditional approval. An actual trend/entry/stop and market/sector check remain separate. The severe bear value is not a stop.

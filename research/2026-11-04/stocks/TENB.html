What the business is and what is improving

Tenable sells vulnerability and exposure management across IT, cloud, identities, operational technology and AI systems. Its installed sensors, vulnerability research and customer workflows create switching costs and opportunities to cross-sell the broader Tenable One platform. This is a credible franchise, not a broken-company thesis. The issuer reports over 40,000 customers and over 5,000 using Tenable One; the latter was 59% of Q3 new sales, not 59% of revenue [S5].

The growth evidence is encouraging but does not yet prove a new 20% company-wide growth regime:

  • Q3 subscription revenue $254.600m, +9.64%; recurring revenue approximately 95% of total. The company uses ARR in its retention calculation but does not disclose a standalone total ARR balance/growth series in the reviewed filing and results. Subscription growth and RPO must not be relabeled ARR growth.
  • Dollar-based net expansion 107%, versus 106% in Q2 and 106% a year earlier. This measures the prior-year cohort including expansion/contraction/churn. It is not gross retention, and it does not independently establish pricing power.
  • Short-term RPO $747.222m, +11.7%; total RPO $1,063.044m, +14.4%. Longer duration partly explains total RPO growing faster than short-term RPO; neither is immediate revenue.
  • 413 new enterprise-platform logos; six-figure customers 2,275 versus 2,156, +5.5%, with 39 net additions in Q3 [S2,S3,S5].

Against July guidance, Q3 revenue beat the $271.5m midpoint by $4.887m, or 1.8%, and non-GAAP operating income beat $67.5m by $7.388m, or 10.9%. FY26 revenue midpoint rose $1,078m to $1,085.5m, only 0.7%; non-GAAP EBIT midpoint rose $261m to $271.5m, 4.0%. Unlevered FCF guidance stayed $289–295m, rather than being raised with earnings. Q4 revenue guidance of $277–280m implies about 6.9% growth at midpoint against Q4 2025. These facts support real operating leverage but caution against extrapolating EPS acceleration [S3,S4].

Normalization: genuine progress, not free SBC

The prior TTM includes $14.6m from accelerated former-CEO equity vesting. Removing that discrete charge changes prior EBIT to +$9.552m; current EBIT is still $46.695m. Recovery is real even after removing the favorable comparison. Restructuring and acquisition charges are disclosed, not all automatically removed from a business that has repeatedly incurred them. Acquisition amortization stays within EBIT and is included once in D&A for the cash bridge.

TTM expensed SBC $185.922m is 17.4% of revenue. Q3 SBC rose 14.1%, faster than revenue, to 18.5% of revenue. Thus no straight-line assumption that SBC quickly disappears is justified. Q3 non-GAAP EBIT $74.888m less SBC $51.217m is only $23.671m, or 8.56% of revenue, before restoring other excluded expenses. FY26 non-GAAP EBIT midpoint $271.5m less forecast SBC $185.3m leaves $86.2m, or 7.94%; restoring amortization/restructuring brings GAAP EBIT to $55.5m, or 5.11% [S3].

SBC alone is not a veto. It is a recurring labor cost, and the DCF can value a business with it if margins and growth justify the price. This model keeps it in EBIT and does not additionally impose future dilution growth or hypothetical buyback spending.

Cash-quality reconciliation

Cash FCF is CFO less PP&E less capitalized software, not CFO alone. The company’s “unlevered FCF” adds cash interest and financing costs without fully normalizing taxes, financial income, SBC or working capital; it is not the model’s FCFF.

TTM cash FCF $266.925m less expensed SBC $185.922m gives $81.003m, a useful owner-cost diagnostic, not formal FCFF. Additional capitalized SBC is approximately $2.719m, so a broader all-compensation diagnostic is $78.284m. At $4.915bn basic market equity this is only about 1.6%.

The exact reported-to-normalized bridge is:

  1. Cash FCF 266.925.
  2. Remove expensed SBC −185.922, giving 81.003.
  3. Remove TTM reported working-capital contribution −38.913 and other non-D&A noncash adjustments −6.389.
  4. Replace net income with EBIT taxed at 23%: +20.174, giving 55.875.
  5. Include capitalized SBC as economic investment −2.719, giving 53.156m normalized operating FCFF before incremental working capital.

Equivalent formula: EBIT 46.695 ×77% + D&A 41.228 − cash capex 21.308 − capitalized SBC 2.719 =53.156. This bridge removes financial income and financing expense along with tax timing rather than counting them in operating value and cash twice. It is a normalization diagnostic, not a claim that cash taxes or working capital were actually zero.

Nine-month cash software investment rose $2.676m to $11.712m. Deferred commissions declined $124.629m to $115.148m: cash capitalized commissions of34.056m versus amortization 43.537m released 9.481m. The DCF does not perpetuate this release. Commission investment is included in forecast working capital, not also in PP&E capex. Revenue is predominantly USD-billed, reducing direct revenue FX exposure; international expenses still face FX risk.

Historical acquisitions consumed $243.3m/$29.2m/$196.2m in 2023/24/25. The forecast assumes growth from the already-owned business and expensed R&D, no free future acquisitions. A new acquisition requires deducting its price and reassessing its growth/margin contribution.

Balance sheet, ownership and convertible reconciliation

September cash plus short-term investments is $495.261m. Debt is $800m principal, not its $779.016m carrying amount. The operating-value-to-equity bridge subtracts $800m and adds $395.261m excess cash after a disclosed $100m operating-liquidity reserve, net −$404.739m. Releasing that entire reserve adds only $0.88 per modeled share. Operating lease payments remain inside operating costs, so $62.076m lease liabilities are not deducted a second time.

The convertible coupon is 0.25%, maturity September 15, 2031, conversion price about $44.84, and capped-call protection extends to $64.06. Principal must be settled in cash. Therefore the model subtracts $800m and does not also add the 17.84m gross underlying conversion shares. Above the cap, residual dilution is possible; all direct scenarios here are below it. Capped-call counterparty/early-conversion risks are real, not zero. At a $47.03 stock price, loss of the hedge’s current intrinsic offset is only about $39m, or $0.35/share, but exposure grows with the stock. The central case is already an improvement case: current owner-cost margin is only 4.4% TTM/6.0% latest quarter. Reaching 22% needs considerable leverage while retaining investment and compensation. Qualys shows mature software margins above 30% are possible, but Tenable’s product breadth, sales spending and acquisition footprint differ. The favorable case requires much stronger sales execution and cost leverage, not merely continuation of Q3.

WACC anchor: the November 3 Federal Reserve H.15 release gives 4.91% for the November 2 10-year Treasury. A modeled beta 1.2 and equity premium 5.5% imply cost of equity about 11.5%; 85% equity/15% debt, 6.5% economic debt cost and 23% tax imply about 10.5% WACC. These are underwriting assumptions, not a falsely precise measured beta or a claim the 0.25% convertible coupon is the economic cost of capital [S9].

In transition years, net investment/revenue glides from year 5 to the terminal requirement. Terminal reinvestment = NOPAT ×g/ROIC. The central terminal FCFF is 313.705m after 34.856m reinvestment. D&A is 2% in years 6–10; gross investment is solved so D&A, capex and ΔWC reconcile to the stated net requirement. All three cases satisfy WACC>g. This avoids an abrupt free-growth terminal.

Federal/state/foreign NOLs and credits exist, but jurisdiction, ownership limits and expiry prevent treating the full face amount as freely spendable cash. The base uses normalized tax rates throughout and adds no separate tax asset. Even giving full undiscounted credit to the FY25 NOL deferred-tax asset plus tax credits would add less than $2/share, before limitations. This conservative omission cannot explain the roughly $27 price/base-value gap [S1].

Decision, triggers and risks

The Stage 3 business-quality gate passes: recurring demand, positive owner-cost cash, improving GAAP profit and adequate liquidity. The valuation/expectations gate fails at $47.03. This is a price decision, not rejection of the company or SBC as an automatic exclusion.

A $17–20 research-revisit zone is a practical alert range around or modestly below today’s central estimate. It is neither an authorized buy ceiling nor a standing order. At a genuinely well-supported reasonable value, the plan permits reviewing a partial position without an automatic 15% haircut. Price falling alone would not suffice if operations deteriorated. Conversely, verified improvement in growth, retention and margins can raise the valuation without requiring the market to fall to this range.

Reassess after FY26 results/guidance, a material change in financing or acquisitions, or major new operating evidence. Evidence that could support a higher central value: company-wide growth sustainably above 12%, retention reaching 110% with better large-account growth, GAAP margins improving without capitalized-cost or compensation distortions, and owner FCFF rising faster than revenue. These are research signposts, not new mechanical user rules.

Thesis risks/invalidation: sustained retention deterioration or outright cohort contraction; failure of Tenable One to translate bookings mix into aggregate growth; repeated dilution/repurchases financed by debt without owner-value growth; AI/cloud competition forcing investment or price cuts; adverse cyber or product-performance incidents; and new acquisitions whose returns fail to cover cost. A material financing issue, accounting contradiction or recovery reversal would move the verdict from price-wait to watch/reject. No such hard blocker was established in the reviewed statements.

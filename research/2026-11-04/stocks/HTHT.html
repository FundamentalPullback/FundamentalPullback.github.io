Business quality, durability and the pullback thesis

The durable asset is the hotel brand, member distribution and operating system, not simply China hotel property. Hanting, Ji Hotel and the wider brand ladder let franchisees fund most new hotel development while H World earns management/franchise, reservation, technology and manager-service revenue. At 2025 year end the loyalty program had over 311m members; about 77% of legacy China room nights were sold through owned channels. The company typically earns ongoing franchise fees of 3%–6.5% of hotel revenue and contracts generally last 8–10 years in China. Manager reimbursements and upfront-fee amortization are part of reported M&F revenue, so it is not a pure royalty stream. 2025 annual report, business and revenue recognition

June 30 scale was 13,539 hotels and 1.335m rooms, including 13,417 China hotels. Q2 China opened 498 hotels and closed 176; 497 openings were managed/franchised. This is a real shift toward lighter capital intensity, not elimination of the legacy leased estate. Q2 M&F revenue grew 25.2%, while leased/owned revenue fell 4.9%. The latest 2026 guide is 4%–8% total revenue growth, 7%–11% China and 16%–20% M&F. These are management ranges; the valuation’s 2026 revenue anchor is RMB26.8bn, approximately the total midpoint. Q2 growth, network and guidance

The important contrary evidence: Q2 China blended RevPAR rose from RMB235 to RMB238, but same-hotel RevPAR fell 3%, from RMB240 to RMB233, with flat ADR and occupancy down 2.4 points. New hotel/brand mix is not the same as pricing power at established hotels. International RevPAR declined from US$102 to US$98 and occupancy from 74.0% to 70.5%. 4. Headline earnings, unusual items and cash seasonality

H1 2026 EBIT rose 29.2%, from 2.869 to 3.706, while attributable net income fell 1.8%, from 2.438 to 2.394. The income-statement FX line moved from +0.574 to −0.117, a 0.691 swing, and reported tax rose by 0.226. Financial marks, FX and taxes therefore obscure some improvement in hotel operations. They are not recurring hotel cash in the operating DCF.

Q4 2024 had only 0.049 of parent net income on 0.693 of pretax income and 0.578 of tax expense, alongside investment and other effects. Growth off that base is not a sustainable run-rate. The cash-flow impairment adjustment also falls from 0.539 in prior TTM to 0.270 in current TTM. That decline contributes 0.269 to the improvement. The normalized EBIT bridge retains impairment rather than automatically adding it back. For context, imposing a common 0.400 impairment burden on both periods would produce normalized EBIT of 5.271 versus 6.996, growth of 32.7% rather than 38.9%. This diagnostic is not a second valuation deduction.

Seasonality is material. Q1 2026 CFO of 0.233 and cash after capex/SBC of −0.035 are followed by Q2 CFO of 3.408 and cash after capex/SBC of 3.062. The operating-asset/liability cash aggregate swings from −1.119 to +1.232. Q4 2025 receives +1.684 from the same aggregate. These figures include more than trade working capital. Across the TTM, the aggregate falls from +2.002 to +1.557, so higher annual CFO is not solely attributable to a larger aggregate working-capital release. Neither level is assumed to repeat forever.

7. ADS, currency, geography and legal claims

  • One Nasdaq ADS represents ten ordinary shares, verified on the 20-F cover. Hong Kong 1179 is the same Cayman holding-company equity quoted in HKD, not a second equity pool. Comparison with USD per ADS requires both the 10:1 ratio and currency conversion.
  • June 30 outstanding ordinary shares are 3,154,059,870, or 315.405987 million ADS equivalents, excluding 78,984,260 treasury shares. Issued shares of 3,233,044,130 are not the economic outstanding count.
  • The interim equity roll-forward records 133,496,890 ordinary shares from conversion, or 13.349689 million ADS equivalents. The note rounds this to 13 million ADS and identifies USD 499.6 million of principal converted at May 1 maturity, with USD 0.4 million repaid. The old USD 500 million convertible is no longer debt. Its conversion must not be added again to June’s outstanding shares.
  • The original 322 million valuation ADS allowance is a forward economic proxy for June’s outstanding count plus existing options/RSUs. It differs from the historical weighted 326.262 million ADS equivalents in H1 EPS, which reflects the pre-conversion period and convertible-dilution convention. Future SBC remains an expense, without automatically charging future dilution again for the same compensation. This is not an exact November share census.
  • The USD 0.87 per ADS dividend declared in August and subsequent RMB bond proceeds are subsequent capital events, not TTM operating revenue. The original equity bridge adds both cash and debt for new borrowing and deducts the dividend once. Neither the converted debt nor the distribution should be deducted again.
  • All operating comparisons are in RMB, not the issuer’s convenience USD translations at different quarterly rates. The original model’s RMB 6.67233 per USD is a November 3 valuation input, not the June 30 reporting translation. China and international currency exposures remain relevant to scenarios.
  • Beginning in 2026, the issuer uses HWC/HWI geographic terminology instead of Legacy-Huazhu/Legacy-DH. Those segment labels are not silently spliced into a single organic-growth series. Consolidated accounts and the leased/owned and manachised/franchised categories reconcile. Same-hotel RevPAR, FX and hotel openings remain separate evidence.
  • The 20-F identifies contractual affiliates/VIEs for restricted activities but quantifies combined revenue, net profit and assets at less than 1% for 2024/25. It also says those entities do not support material revenue in other subsidiaries. Describing the entire hotel business as a VIE would overstate that specific exposure. PRC regulation, remittance, convertibility, enforceability, depositary costs and holding-company claims remain. This finding does not make all onshore cash freely distributable.

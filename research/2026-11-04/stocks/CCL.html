Consistent treatment

  • Deposits are operating liabilities, not excess cash or ordinary debt. They are captured through working capital and future voyage costs. Subtracting all deposits as debt after also modeling those obligations would double count. The model gives no recurring cash-flow bonus from rising deposit balances.
  • Analyst operating-cash reserves are 0.50 RCL and 0.75 CCL. Only the remaining 0.351/0.470 is credited as excess cash. These are assumptions, not disclosed minimum-cash requirements.
  • Core enterprise-to-equity claims are therefore 22.721 RCL = 22.872 − 0.351 + 0.200 minority interest; 24.108 CCL = 24.559 − 0.470 + 0.019 minority interest. Minority interests use carrying values as approximations.
  • Operating rent stays in EBIT/FCFF, so operating lease liabilities are not deducted again. Finance leases already included in debt are not added twice. A lease-capitalized alternative would require adjusting both cash flow and claims.
  • Ship capex is charged in forecast FCFF, not subtracted again as a lump-sum debt claim. Future borrowing is financing, not a source of FCFF. Likewise, don’t both subtract current debt and deduct its principal amortization inside FCFF.
  • An unmodeled 1.0 additional equity-funded liability reduces value by approximately $3.73 RCL / $0.73 CCL, before any operating effect. Extra 10% dilution reduces per-share value approximately 9.1%, all else equal.

Discount rate and 10-year model

Underwriting assumptions, not claimed observed regression estimates: ERP 4.5%; levered beta 1.35 RCL/1.50 CCL; marginal debt costs 6.0%/6.5%; 2% debt tax shield. At approximate market-value capital weights these indicate roughly 10% WACC. Historical coupons are not current marginal yields. Use 9%, 10%, and 11% rather than false precision. No consensus estimates are substituted for these assumptions.

Core model years are 2027–2036, end-year discounted:

  1. NOPAT = recurring consolidated EBIT × (1 − cash tax).
  2. Economic net reinvestment = NOPAT × growth / incremental ROIC.
  3. In 2027–2030 use the greater of economic net reinvestment and a ship-payment floor: contracted newbuild cash payments + assumed other capex − modeled D&A + 0.10 working capital. This is a floor, not an additional second reinvestment deduction.
  4. Terminal FCFF = year-10 NOPAT × (1 + terminal growth) × (1 − terminal growth / terminal ROIC).
  5. Equity = core EV + existing-JV equity value + net acquired value − net capital claims; divide by diluted shares.

Sector comparison and thesis tests

At the supplied prices, RCL is approximately 17.5× FY adjusted EPS versus 13.0× for CCL. Approximate enterprise-value/adjusted-EBITDA is 13.6× RCL / 8.9× CCL, using gross principal less reported cash and approximately 7.79/7.14 FY EBITDA. Their corporate ROIC definitions also differ and should not be used as directly comparable incremental returns.

Operational quality ranking is RCL, then CCL, then turnaround-risk NCLH; valuation attractiveness between the two covered names favors CCL. This is an analytical judgment, not a universal sector ranking. NCLH reported Q3 constant-currency net yields down 6.7%, FY EBITDA guidance 2.55, net debt 14.8, reported net leverage 5.5×, and October 8.75% refinancing. Its bookings remain below the desired range. It is not a clean quality/multiple anchor for either company’s premium. NCLH Q3 company release

What would strengthen RCL: same-ship yield growth after timing effects; destination and new-fleet earnings that convert to cash after upkeep; existing JV distributions; Sandals earning an adequate after-capex return on its funded cost; and capex/debt staying within the modeled envelope. What would weaken it: price-led growth giving way to discounting, capex overruns, a heavier resort funding requirement, or buybacks masking weak aggregate cash conversion.

What would strengthen CCL: same-ship yield and onboard growth exceeding unit-cost inflation, delivery-year capex remaining controlled, sustained debt reduction after dividends/buybacks, and margin expansion sufficient to clear the 8.4% reverse growth bar without years of unusually high incremental returns. What would weaken it: the cash-flow advantage disappearing when deliveries resume, deposit growth reversing, or earnings per share improving mainly through refinancing/share-count effects.

For both, fuel, currency, refunds, environmental/port charges, tax changes, destination concentration, consumer spending and shipyard timing remain material. Deposits create funding resilience in normal conditions but can reverse during cancellations; a balance-sheet claim and cash-flow stress must not be counted twice.

Stage 3 versus Stage 3.5

The completed fundamental work does not clear either name at the quoted price under the base return hurdle. CCL deserves the earlier re-underwriting check because its value reaches the quote around a 9% WACC; RCL remains above the shown base sensitivities even there. Lower quotes or stronger evidenced cash economics can change either conclusion. No fixed discount percentage is imposed.

A valid technical 3:1 uses (credible target − actual entry) / (actual entry − technically justified stop) and incorporates execution/slippage risk. It does not use this DCF’s bear scenario as a stop, and a 3:1 chart setup cannot by itself resolve a failed fundamental valuation. Verify current market/sector conditions and price structure separately before considering promotion or a trade.

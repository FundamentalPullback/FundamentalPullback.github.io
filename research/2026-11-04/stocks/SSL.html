Decision

Observation. No approval at the scanner price of $16.00 per ADS. Operational improvements are real, but the current multiple conceals weak cash conversion after sustaining capital, significant non-debt claims, and a fuel/chemical upcycle influenced by supply disruptions.

The ten-year FCFF model gives $8.38 per ADS in the base case, with a central sensitivity range of approximately $6.5–11. This is a valuation estimate, not a guaranteed trading range. The favorable operational scenario is $26.61; the adverse scenario exhausts equity value after claims and is floored at zero. Those stress outcomes are not the central range. At $16, base margin of safety, defined as (value minus price)/value, is −91.0%; price-to-value downside is −47.7%. Do not confuse those denominators.

Confidence is medium in the observation decision, low-to-medium in the point valuation. No mechanical buying price or stop is assigned. An additional percentage discount would not resolve the production, maintenance and regulatory conditions that determine value.

Business and the investment question

Sasol combines coal mining and coal/gas-to-liquids production at Secunda, Mozambique gas, fuel distribution and its Natref refinery interest, African chemicals, and US/European chemicals. Its integration and South African distribution position are valuable; most output still sells into commodity-linked markets. US feedstock advantage and specialty products do not eliminate global chemical oversupply.

The favorable thesis is improved coal quality and equipment reliability, cheaper own coal, stronger product placement, a successful chemical reset and lower debt. The opposing thesis is that elevated fuel cracks and temporary global supply constraints are being mistaken for durable free cash flow, while sustaining/decarbonization capital, gas depletion and carbon costs absorb the operating gains. A higher earnings headline does not demonstrate the plan’s preferred sustained 15% EPS growth.

This is the first full valuation replacing the limited A137 review. Its reason is completion of the financial/currency/maintenance analysis, not a change of method to justify a desired answer. No issuer-specific cause of every point in the recent pullback is established.

September-quarter mining production was 8.5Mt, up 18% year-on-year, while external coal purchases fell 48%. Secunda production was 1.667Mt, down 6%, reflecting its planned shutdown. Gas production was 28.6bscf, down 5%, with well availability and natural decline. Fuel sales rose 21%, helped by inventory drawdown and extra use of Prax capacity. South African, operating and regulatory risks

  • Gas and production: the issuer’s impairment case assumes Secunda above 7Mt through 2030 and about 6.4Mt from 2035 as gas availability declines. Imported LNG is not currently economic in that case. The model does not assume a free LNG rescue. Mozambique PSA production and Junction Compression can help near-term supply, but do not prove a permanent solution.
  • License: Secunda’s load-based SO2 dispensation runs through March 31, 2030. A further arrangement is not guaranteed. The separate Highveld 40% emissions-target interpretation remains in litigation.
  • Carbon: FY2026 net carbon taxes wereR2.1bn, with R0.952bn purchased credits. The issuer discloses rising statutory rates and uncertainty around allowances/budgets after 2030. Its accounting case assumes allowance reductions and eventual penalties; those are assumptions, not a guaranteed policy settlement. The base forecast includes increasing real carbon/energy burden within Southern African margins and sustaining capital for compliance.
  • Power/water/logistics: self-generation covers roughly half of SA electricity requirements, not all. Grid reliability has improved, so historical load-shedding should not be described as today’s confirmed condition. Above-inflation tariffs, water infrastructure, Transnet reliability, labor disruption and political/regulatory change remain important risks.
  • Natref/Prax: Sasol is operating 100% capacity under arrangements with the business-rescue practitioner; that does not mean it permanently owns 100%. The RCD restart was expected at endOctober, but a completed restart was not separately verified by this cutoff. The base does not capitalize permanent ownership of Prax’s share or assumed acquisition synergies.

Ten-year FCFF model: real/nominal currency discipline

The model covers FY2027–2036, five explicit detailed years plus five transition years. Figures below are real FY2026 Rbn. They are research assumptions, not issuer guidance. The balance-sheet anchor is June 30, 2026, the latest audited balance available. The dated November price and ECB FX are disclosed separately; no invented September balance or cash estimate is presented as reported.

FCFF = EBIT×(1−tax) + non-lease D&A − maintenance capex − growth capex − change in nominal operating working capital, deflated into FY2026 rand. Separately add sustainable net cash distributions from equity-accounted ventures. Their accounting profit is excluded from core EBITDA, and their book investment balance is not added again.

Base inflation is 4% in ZAR and 2% in USD. 12.5% nominal-ZAR WACC becomes 8.1731% real, calculated as 1.125/1.04−1. Terminal real growth is 0%, equivalent to 4% nominal-ZAR or 2% nominal-USD growth. The equivalent USD discount rate is 10.3365%; FX evolves only for the cross-check at 1.04/1.02 per year. Discounting the translated nominal-dollar cash flows reproduces the real-rand present value exactly. Using an unchanged FX with nominal-ZAR growth and a USD discount rate would not.

The rate is an analyst estimate anchored to issuer impairment rates: SA11.5%, US8.3%, Mozambique 16.4%, with corresponding inflation 4%/2%/2%. A rough 60%SA/25%international/15%Mozambique real-rate mix is around 8%, supporting a rounded 8.17% real group requirement. Those exposure weights are a risk proxy, not a claimed market-value segment weighting. Sensitivities cover 11.5–13.5% nominal-ZAR; scenario discount rates are not a substitute for modeling weaker cash flow.

Core SA EBITDA transitions 54→49→47→46→45, then 40 byFY2036; International Chemicals 8.6→9→10→10.5→11, then 12. This grants operational/cost improvement and a chemicals recovery, while removing exceptional fuel spreads and absorbing declining gas-linked output and growing carbon/energy cost. A price decline alone is insufficient if the underlying production economics deteriorate. Failure of the 2030 operating dispensation or materially higher unfunded transition spending would reduce value beyond an ordinary rate sensitivity.

This analysis is complete on the public evidence available. Unpublished quarterly financials, undisclosed transaction consideration, future regulations and an unconfirmed equipment restart are genuine limits on what can be known, not promises of missing research. The result remains observation, no Stage 3.5 entry approval, no trade.

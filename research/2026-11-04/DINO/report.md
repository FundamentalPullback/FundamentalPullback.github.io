## Reproducibility and model conventions

Run `python dino_history.py`, `python dino_normalized_model.py`, `python write_dino.py` and `python write_dino_revised.py` in that order in the revised research folder. The original model is preserved as `refiners_core_original.py`; revised outputs are `DINO_normalized_model_results.json`. All amounts in the code are USD billions, volumes are million barrels and share counts are billions. Automated checks require finite values, monotonic margin/claim/WACC behavior and bear < base < bull. No external Python package is needed.

Operating EBITDA = volume × (gross refining margin − refinery cash opex) − refinery SG&A + other consolidated segment EBITDA − corporate cost − incremental RIN stress. EBIT deducts economic D&A. FCFF = EBIT − cash tax + D&A − sustaining cash − growth capex − working-capital investment − special cash costs. SBC remains an expense. Equity-method earnings are excluded from operating EBIT and replaced with cash distributions after JV taxes, debt service and investment, valued at a separate equity discount rate. Owned logistics businesses are included once.

Taxes equal the stated normalized rate on positive EBIT, without immediate tax refunds in loss years. The Q4 bridge starts from September 30 cash/debt, rolls known financing actions, and includes the full unreported quarter; it does not claim to know actual November cash. Q4 is discounted 0.16 years and annual cash flows 1.16–10.16 years. Opening income-tax payables are settled in the stub, rather than assumed to be permanent financing. Working capital is an additional use of $100m in 2027, $50m in 2028 and roughly $25m plus inflation thereafter. These amounts cover normalized growth/inventory needs, not a prediction of volatile quarter-to-quarter oil-price collateral.

Terminal NOPAT grows at g. Terminal net reinvestment is the greater of NOPAT × g/ROIC and any recurring sustaining-capex excess over D&A, with the incremental capex stress included. Terminal FCFF cannot be manufactured by removing turnarounds. The terminal value is floored at zero if continuing operations produce negative terminal cash; this is not a claim that closure is free. Base real physical volume is flat and other-segment EBITDA grows 2% nominally. Cost inflation is 2%. Holding-company debt/finance leases and legacy capital claims are then reconciled to equity. Operating leases are not deducted again because rent remains in operating costs. There is no automatic multiple uplift, no acquisition growth for free and no perpetual subsidy extrapolation.

## Normalization bridge

Recurring-accounting EBIT = GAAP EBIT + lower-of-cost-or-market inventory expense (subtract a benefit) + identified impairment + separately identified Mississauga severance and accelerated depreciation + the 2024 one-time regulatory penalty − RIN-waiver benefits.

SBC, ordinary plant costs, SG&A and ordinary depreciation remain expenses. Adding back an impairment is an accounting normalization, not a claim that the lost investment was costless. The forward valuation separately funds replacement investment and announced closure/separation cash. Other small trading/FX marks remain in reported operating costs where the cash-flow statement does not isolate the operating component; the table is not described as a perfectly hedgeless earnings measure.

### Constant refining margin rather than the cycle peak

Adjusted refinery gross margin is sales less feedstock/material cost before refinery operating cost and depreciation. It is **not EBITDA per barrel**. Waivers remain in the company's adjusted gross-margin number, so they are explicitly removed. Produced-product sales barrels are used consistently; crude charge and total throughput are different denominators.

Fixed-margin EBIT = recurring-accounting EBIT − (reported adjusted refinery gross-margin dollars − waivers) + $19 × produced barrels. Replacing the full gross margin also removes its waiver benefit; it is not removed twice. $19 is the original model's 2031 nominal gross-margin anchor applied at a constant dollar level to every historical period for comparability. It is an analyst test, not a verified timeless industry average.

Fixed-margin FCFF proxy = fixed-margin EBIT × 75% + ordinary D&A − PP&E cash spending − turnaround cash spending, assuming neutral working capital. SBC is already in EBIT and is not deducted again. This is unlevered consolidated cash before equity-method JV receipts and outside minority claims, with a normalized tax rate and actual period capital. It is **not reported CFO, company adjusted FCF, or a pure maintenance-only cash measure**. Differences in turnaround timing and growth investment still affect the proxy. TTM rises **28.0%**.

The fixed-refining-margin EBIT proxy retains other segments' actual economics, including renewable credit/feedstock conditions. It does not imply that those are riskless perpetuities. As a diagnostic, TTM Renewables EBIT is about $286m after inventory/impairment normalization versus a roughly $129m loss prior TTM, while Lubricants EBIT is about $411m after closure accounting adjustments versus $192m. Thus much of the non-refining improvement reflects renewables and lubricants, not simply refinery utilization. The original forward model normalizes those businesses materially below their latest annualized results: $150m Renewables EBITDA and $350m Lubricants EBITDA. PTC eligibility, feedstock economics and separation execution can still change the outcome. ## Valuation reconciliation and publication status

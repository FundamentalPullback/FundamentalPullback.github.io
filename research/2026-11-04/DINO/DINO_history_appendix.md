# HF Sinclair historical financial and normalization appendix

**Added November 4, 2026, 12:34 a.m. EST. This appendix completes the required historical component omitted from the earlier Stage 3 report.** It should be read with the ten-year valuation and claims analysis, not treated as a new price target. Financial cutoff remains September 30, 2026; market reference remains the November 3 regular close of $113.36, versus scanner $113.56 with unspecified export timezone.

## Finding and effect on the decision

The complete history supports operating improvement, but makes clear how much of current earnings is cyclical or exceptional. TTM reported EBIT rose from **$694m to $4,694m**. After inventory valuation, RIN exemptions and identified impairments/closure charges, recurring-accounting EBIT is **$640m to $3,584m**. Replacing refining gross margin in both periods with the same **$19/produced barrel** gives **$2,179.7m to $2,674.6m**, or **22.7% growth**. This is a more useful growth test than the 576% headline EBIT increase.

**The historical audit changes the central underwriting and the reasonable-price classification.** The earlier $110.74 case is retained as an elevated-spread alternative. A central path anchored to clean TTM refining margin, $22.87→21→20→19→19, gives **$90.95/share and 7.6% illustrative annual equity return** at $113.36. A firmer normal-cycle path gives $95.98/8.5%; a joint case with that firmer path, better non-refining EBITDA and lower WACC gives $108.45/10.1%. Those returns remain below the analytical 11.5% cost of equity. **Ordinary-case reasonable-price partial: not supported at the reference price.** The earlier conditional “yes” required more persistent elevated margins than the headline conclusion made clear; it is superseded. This is an economic-return revision, not a mechanical price-discount rule.

## Normalization bridge

Recurring-accounting EBIT = GAAP EBIT + lower-of-cost-or-market inventory expense (subtract a benefit) + identified impairment + separately identified Mississauga severance and accelerated depreciation + the 2024 one-time regulatory penalty − RIN-waiver benefits.

SBC, ordinary plant costs, SG&A and ordinary depreciation remain expenses. Adding back an impairment is an accounting normalization, not a claim that the lost investment was costless. The forward valuation separately funds replacement investment and announced closure/separation cash. Other small trading/FX marks remain in reported operating costs where the cash-flow statement does not isolate the operating component; the table is not described as a perfectly hedgeless earnings measure.

### Constant refining margin rather than the cycle peak

Adjusted refinery gross margin is sales less feedstock/material cost before refinery operating cost and depreciation. It is **not EBITDA per barrel**. Waivers remain in the company's adjusted gross-margin number, so they are explicitly removed. Produced-product sales barrels are used consistently; crude charge and total throughput are different denominators.

Fixed-margin EBIT = recurring-accounting EBIT − (reported adjusted refinery gross-margin dollars − waivers) + $19 × produced barrels. Replacing the full gross margin also removes its waiver benefit; it is not removed twice. $19 is the original model's 2031 nominal gross-margin anchor applied at a constant dollar level to every historical period for comparability. It is an analyst test, not a verified timeless industry average.

Fixed-margin FCFF proxy = fixed-margin EBIT × 75% + ordinary D&A − PP&E cash spending − turnaround cash spending, assuming neutral working capital. SBC is already in EBIT and is not deducted again. This is unlevered consolidated cash before equity-method JV receipts and outside minority claims, with a normalized tax rate and actual period capital. It is **not reported CFO, company adjusted FCF, or a pure maintenance-only cash measure**. Differences in turnaround timing and growth investment still affect the proxy. TTM rises **28.0%**.

The fixed-refining-margin EBIT proxy retains other segments' actual economics, including renewable credit/feedstock conditions. It does not imply that those are riskless perpetuities. As a diagnostic, TTM Renewables EBIT is about $286m after inventory/impairment normalization versus a roughly $129m loss prior TTM, while Lubricants EBIT is about $411m after closure accounting adjustments versus $192m. Thus much of the non-refining improvement reflects renewables and lubricants, not simply refinery utilization. The original forward model normalizes those businesses materially below their latest annualized results: $150m Renewables EBITDA and $350m Lubricants EBITDA. PTC eligibility, feedstock economics and separation execution can still change the outcome. [Q32026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1915657/000191565726000074/dino-20260930.htm); [Q32025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1915657/000162828025047446/dino-20250930.htm); [FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1915657/000191565726000016/dino-20251231.htm).

TTM PP&E plus turnaround cash is $899m, versus $960m prior TTM. The existing model's 2027 $800m sustaining plus $150m growth, with additional Western Gateway funding outside that core capital line, is compatible with funding a genuine capital burden. Quarterly turnaround outlays and tax settlement timing explain part of the cash volatility: CFO was only $8m in Q4 2025 versus $1,784m in Q3 2026. Extrapolating the latter would overstate cash earning power. The forward model already settles the opening tax payable in its Q4 bridge and reserves known environmental/closure obligations separately.

## Valuation reconciliation and publication status

The original executable was rerun and reconciles $110.74, $17.65 and $218.99. That verifies the old arithmetic, not the old choice of central margin path. The versioned normalized model changes only the 2027–30 margin bridge initially, producing $90.95. It then tests firmer ordinary margins, non-refining improvement and lower discount rates jointly. The original $35→28→23→20→19 path remains an explicit elevated-spread alternative, not the central estimate.

The 22.7% fixed-refining-margin EBIT increase supports improved underlying performance, but the 2023–25 fixed-margin series of roughly $2.02bn/$2.27bn/$2.19bn is not an uninterrupted accelerating trend. At $113.36, the normalized equity return is 7.6%, while even the constructive joint case is 10.1%, versus an analytical cost of equity around 11.5%. The appropriate ordinary-case status is watch, not reasonable-price partial. A partial could still be justified by an investor deliberately underwriting stronger persistent economics or a lower required return, but that is an additional investment view, not a conclusion established by the ordinary normalized case. Stage 3.5 remains separate and uncleared.

# Gartner (IT): completed Stage 3 remediation and dated valuation revision

**4 November 2026, before the US market opens. USD millions except per-share amounts.** Research cutoff: November 3 SEC 10-Q, earnings release and supplement, with November 3 closing quotations. This revision supersedes A153's categorical valuation-based refusal to consider Stage 3.5. It does **not** approve an entry, establish a stop, or alter the investment plan.

## Decision and what changed

At **$204.22**, Gartner is approximately reasonably priced under a sourced cost-of-capital framework, rather than clearly too expensive under every defensible framework. Revised central FCFF value is **$213.76**; holding the return implied by NYU's own risk-free assumption constant instead gives **$203.44**. A credible operating recovery gives **$243.96**. Bear/base/bull are **$116.85 / $213.76 / $310.36**. These are conditional scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals, price targets with a deadline, or loss limits.

The fundamental disposition is **eligible to be considered for Stage 3.5 at a reasonable price, with a thin cushion; no current entry approval**. A partial purchase could be considered only if the separate technical, portfolio, event and execution checks pass. Neither growth of 15% nor a 15/20/25% discount is a universal admission rule. Nor does a merely positive DCF upside compel purchase. The central upside is only 4.67%, and loss of excess-cash credit moves value to $196.05.

The old model was not arithmetically broken: its $188.58 central value and $194.00 same-mechanics update reproduce exactly. The material change is the explicit return calibration, partially offset by newly quantified capital claims. This is an analyst revision, not a change in reported results or an attempt to force a buy conclusion.

## Scope, evidence and audit findings

- **Unsupported assumption, material:** original 10.5% equity hurdle had no beta/ERP calibration. It was a stated research-required return, not a mathematical error. It now appears as a sensitivity, alongside a current calibrated framework.
- **Incomplete claims, modest:** pension deficit, net deferred compensation and possible past-tax cash obligations were described vaguely. We quantify them and show alternative treatment; combined central deduction $194.061m, or $3.03/share.
- **Not comparable without normalization:** raw TTM owner cash growth 7.10% benefits from tax timing and lower property capex. A consistent tax-and-reinvestment diagnostic is approximately flat, not 7.1% sustainable growth.
- **Missing current comparables, repaired:** prior report explicitly used October 27 peer quotes. The peer table below now uses November 3 closes, with fiscal periods, share proxies and accounting differences disclosed.
- **Missing decision output, repaired:** joint growth/margin/discount-rate reverse cases replace an exclusive reliance on one-variable requirements.

The financial model is suitable for fundamental research with the caveats disclosed here. It cannot by itself support a current trade. No original source report has been silently overwritten.

## Results and business assessment

The November 3 earnings event has occurred and is incorporated. FY2026 guidance is at least $6,405m revenue,$1,615m adjusted EBITDA,$14.65 adjusted EPS and $1,235m free cash flow. Guidance includes about $177m SBC,$181m D&A,$95m capex and 22% adjusted tax. The model does not mistake adjusted earnings for unencumbered cash.

Contract-value growth accelerated from 1.7% in Q2 to 2.5% in Q3, with 1.1% sequential growth. Q3 revenue fell 1.3% reported but rose 2.4% excluding Digital Markets; adjusted EBITDA excluding that business grew 7.5%. This supports a modest recovery, not high-growth extrapolation. Global wallet retention remains 99%, below 100%; enterprise-client count 12,680 is below 13,218 a year earlier. Global client retention improved to 85.4% from 84.7%, but weaker customer counts and AI-assisted alternatives to advisory research remain real risks. Gartner has recurring subscription economics and a trusted research/network franchise; those attributes do not eliminate competitive substitution or customer-budget risk. [S1–S3]

## Current return calibration and why it matters

Official November 3 US10-year Treasury is **4.95%**. NYU Damodaran's November 1 implied ERP is **4.41%**, trailing 12 months with adjusted payout. The market-data page reports **0.91 five-year monthly beta** for IT at November 3; this is a provider estimate, not an independently reconstructed regression. Central beta is normalized to **1.0**, rather than taking the low historical estimate literally during a business transition.

Cost of equity=4.95%+1.0×4.41%=**9.36%**. Marginal debt cost **6.25%** is an analyst refinancing assumption (Treasury+130 bp), supported directionally by investment-grade credit ratings, not an observed bond yield. With 24% tax and equity weight $204.22×64 versus 3,000 debt, **WACC=8.4994%**. Legacy fixed coupons are not used as a perpetual marginal borrowing cost. [S6–S8]

An important date/method bridge is explicit: NYU's ERP page uses its own 5.29% Treasury assumption. Rolling its 4.41% premium to the latest official 4.95% yield is the central convention, not a synchronized daily ERP estimate. Preserving NYU's combined 9.70% market return instead yields **8.7759% WACC and $203.44**. Using provider beta 0.91 without normalization gives $227.06. The old 10.5% equity hurdle, with its original 5.5% debt cost, yields 9.3202% WACC and $185.55 after the newly quantified claims. These are defensible rate sensitivities, not a reason to present one cent-precise intrinsic value as certain.

## Catalysts, failure conditions and Stage 3.5 boundary

Watch the next contract-value release, client count and wallet retention, actual conversion of improved sales productivity into recurring subscriptions, and whether cash capex normalizes without destroying owner cash. The next official earnings date was not verified from an issuer announcement; third-party estimated dates are not promoted to scheduled events.

A sustained move from 2.5% contract growth toward 4–5% with wallet retention above 100% would support the recovery case. Renewed CV deceleration, ongoing client erosion, price pressure from AI alternatives, or retention below 100% with margin protection through underinvestment would weaken it. Acquisitions, large new repurchases or debt issuance require a fresh cash/share/claims bridge rather than extrapolation.

The adverse fair value is **not a stop-loss**. Even bull upside divided by adverse-value downside is only about 1.21:1; this ratio is not the plan's chart-based reward/risk gate. No current technical invalidation, volume confirmation, portfolio concentration or executable entry/stop package was independently certified here. Stage 3 research is completed; Stage 3.5 and a trade authorization remain separate.

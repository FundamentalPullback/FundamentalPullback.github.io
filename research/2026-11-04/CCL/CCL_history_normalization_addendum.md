# CCL: historical normalization and cash-conversion addendum

**Research cutoff: November 4, 2026.** This completes the historical evidence omitted from the original Stage 3 review: three full fiscal years, the latest four quarters, current versus prior TTM, a pre-pandemic/downturn/recovery cycle, and a forward bridge. It supplements the existing ten-year valuation and capital-claims report. **No original model parameter or output has been silently changed.** Bear/base/bull values remain **$5.13/$22.41/$41.12 per share**. The scenarios are not statistical confidence bands or an entry approval.

The important added distinction is that TTM reported net income rose 18.8%, but core operating profit rose only 5.5%. Lower interest and refinancing charges explain much of the improvement. Cash flow after capex remains positive, but increases in customer deposits and other working-capital movements must not be mistaken for permanent operating margins. The valuation conclusion at the $29.16 November 3 scanner reference is unchanged; that reference is not represented as a verified executable or November 4 quote.

## 1. Periods, entity and definitions

CCL has a November fiscal year-end. Current TTM ends August 31, 2026; prior TTM ends August 31, 2025. Q4 FY2025 is September–November 2025, followed by quarters ending February, May and August 2026. After the 2026 unification, the current security is the unified CCL issuer: do not add the former CUK market capitalization or former plc shares a second time. Older net income figures are the issuer’s reported common-earnings series; recent statements explicitly separate noncontrolling interests. Historical immaterial NCI presentation changes mean these should not be relabeled as an independently reconstructed, perfectly uniform consolidated-NI series. Operating profit and cash-flow comparisons are unaffected. [Latest filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/815097/000081509726000107/ccl-20260831.htm).

All amounts are USD millions, except per-share values and percentages. Positive cash capex means an outflow. Historical CFO less capex is after interest and working capital; it is not enterprise FCFF. Subtracting SBC from this cash measure recognizes compensation economically because CFO adds SBC back. In contrast, **core EBIT, NOPAT and the core cash calculation already retain SBC: no second SBC deduction is made there**. Core cash before working capital = core EBIT × 98% + reported D&A − actual cash capex. The 2% tax rate is the valuation model’s stated assumption, not historical cash taxes. This diagnostic excludes changes in working capital, financing flows and non-operating income. It neither assumes all capex is maintenance nor replaces the ten-year ship-commitment model.

Quarterly income figures use the filed quarter where available. Quarterly CFO, capex and SBC are cumulative differences. Q4 is full year less nine months. Current TTM = FY2025 + 9M2026 − 9M2025; prior TTM = FY2024 + 9M2025 − 9M2024. Source statements round to millions, so direct quarterly rows can differ from cumulative-derived TTM by $1–2m. The data preserves these differences rather than creating fictitious precision. Twenty consistency checks pass within that disclosed rounding tolerance.

The normalized NI diagnostic is this report’s transparent comparison, **not** the issuer’s adjusted NI: reported NI + 98% × (debt extinguishment and modification costs − net disposal/impairment gains). Annual debt extinguishment **and modification** costs are $111m/$79m/$409m. Cash-flow add-back tags alone show $98m/$76m/$401m and omit part of modification expense, so they must not be substituted for the full income-statement amounts. The companion data preserves this distinction. The diagnostic is not FCFF and does not remove recurring SBC.

For the prior TTM, net disposal/impairment gains are **39 + 101 − 6 = 134**. The $6m comparative-period amount is the identified $8m ship-sale gain less $2m impairment in the 9M2024 cash-flow reconciliation. For current TTM, the gain is **110 − 2 − 101 = 7**. Thus core EBIT is **4,309 − 134 = 4,175** versus **4,413 − 7 = 4,406**. These adjustments explain why normalized operating growth differs from reported EBIT growth of 2.4%. Reported NI increased by $496m. The $569m pretax financing-cost improvement is larger than that increase, although taxes and other operating/non-operating changes prevent a one-to-one net-income attribution. Refinancing and deleveraging are valuable, but the $301m reduction in one-off transaction charges cannot recur indefinitely. Normalized NI rises 11.3% with those charges and net asset gains adjusted consistently; core EBIT rises 5.5%. Neither measure relies on share buybacks to manufacture growth.

Current TTM CFO increased $1,210m while cash capex increased $948m. During 9M2026 alone, the six working-capital lines consumed approximately $113m versus $340m in 9M2025, a roughly $227m cash benefit. Customer-deposit inflows increased to $318m from $171m. The $603m year-to-date CFO improvement therefore includes a meaningful timing contribution. TTM FCF less SBC increases 8.7%, but the tax-normalized core cash diagnostic before working capital decreases 13.3% because the current capex window is heavier. This is not a contradiction: the measures have different financing, timing and tax content. [Cash-flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/815097/000081509726000107/ccl-20260831.htm).

The full 2019–2025 sequence spans normal operations, shutdown losses and recovery. A mechanical mean would confuse an exceptional shutdown with a recurring annual operating cost, while extrapolating 2023–2025 recovery growth would ignore normalization. More recent profits are earned on a changed fleet, price level and capital structure; pre-pandemic EPS is not a comparable share-count anchor. The source-indexed SEC facts retain the historical accession for each observation.

The original model starts from **$4.22bn normalized EBIT**, based on its stated $7.14bn FY2026 EBITDA outlook less $2.92bn depreciation. That anchor is 4.2% below reconstructed TTM core EBIT of $4.406bn. **It then applies 7% growth in 2027**, producing $4.515bn forecast EBIT and $4.425bn NOPAT, about 2.5% above current core TTM EBIT. Thus the first forecast year is not itself $4.22bn, and a below-TTM starting anchor does not mean the entire forecast assumes declining profits. The EBITDA outlook is management guidance; growth, ROIC and future cash conversion are analyst assumptions.

The model retains ship and other-capex floors, using the maximum of that requirement and growth/ROIC reinvestment, so growth capex is not deducted twice. Its CCL 2027–2030 floors are approximately $1.400bn/$1.281bn/$1.564bn/$1.450bn of net reinvestment. Current reported cash capex is not labeled maintenance spending. Debt amortization, buybacks and dividends are financing allocations rather than operating FCFF; the December 2025 convertible settlement and resulting shares are already reflected in current reported capital structure and must not be charged again.

As with the original RCL model, annual 2027–2036 forecast timing is an approximation rather than an explicit remaining-2026 stub. An additional $0.5bn of net cash generated or consumed before the first modeled year would move CCL equity value by about **$0.37/share**, before time discounting. This is a bridge sensitivity, not a claim to know the quarter’s eventual cash balance.

## 6. Decision and confidence

The required historical coverage is now supplied. The data supports ongoing operating recovery, but not using the full reported net-income growth rate as perpetual operating growth. Historical-source confidence is high; the tax-adjusted normalization and capital-cycle forecast warrant moderate confidence, and the scenario range remains wide because of demand, pricing, fuel, tax, reinvestment and leverage. The original reasonable-price analysis remains conditional: a supported lower discount rate or stronger combination of operating assumptions can matter, and a partial entry does not require an arbitrary universal 15% discount. Stage 3.5 technical entry, size and risk controls are separate. **No new entry approval or trade instruction follows from this appendix.**

# Atour: post-results continuation of A360

November 10, 2026. RMB millions unless stated. NASDAQ ATAT represents three Class A ordinary shares per ADS. November 9 scanner: $34.32, Opportunity 80, QP 70. Research eligibility is not entry approval. Hospitality and retail are operating businesses, not a real-estate investment trust. This updates the substantive A360 dossier using today's issuer 6-K and its dividend exhibit, preserving prior history and identifying where interim disclosures remain incomplete.

## Decision

Growth is substantial but should not be confused with comparable-hotel recovery or adjusted earnings being distributable cash. Q3 revenue grew 30.5%, retail 61.0%, and hotel count increased. Meanwhile same-hotel RevPAR declined about 4.2%, operating costs and compensation rose, GAAP net profit fell 5.5%, and quarterly operating cash fell 4.2%. The operating franchise has positive attributes, but the cash, compensation, currency and terminal assumptions materially affect the ADS value.

The conditional model produces approximately $13.93/$26.57/$53.75 at a common 12% USD analyst hurdle, including the separately declared dividend. These are conditional research scenarios, not approved fair values or entry ceilings. The central case is below the scanner price; the favorable case depends on sustained retail expansion, improved costs and greater cash accessibility. The model does not infer that an apparently wide range supplies a margin of safety. No entry approval.

## Latest results and the distinction that matters

The November 10 release reports Q3 revenue RMB3,429.321m, operating income 664.181m, net income 448.471m and CFO 604.046m. Adjusted net income of 615.360m removes 166.889m SBC, versus only 13.314m in Q3 2025. The adjustment is economically meaningful compensation, not free profit. G&A alone contains about 138m SBC versus 10m previously; cash G&A also rose, to 3.7% of revenue from 3.4%. Technology spending rose to 2.0% of sales from 1.7%.

The primary footnote says SBC is non-deductible in PRC. Our tax calculation therefore adds it back to taxable operating income while retaining it as a shareholder expense. Applying a tax benefit to this charge would overstate owner earnings. The release does not provide a current award rollforward sufficient to treat the spike as a documented one-time grant with no ongoing replacement expense. Base forecasts retain the current TTM SBC rate; double/triple-rate sensitivities are supplied.

Manachised hotel revenue rose 20.4% to 1,878.117m; leased hotel revenue fell 18.6% to 133.648m; retail rose 61.0% to 1,362.217m. Supply-chain services are included in hotel revenue, so this is not pure royalty growth. The network comprises 2,271 manachised and 17 leased hotels. Same-hotel occupancy fell from 81.1% to 79.5%, ADR from 443.1 to 433.7 and RevPAR from 372.2 to 356.6. Aggregate RevPAR 373 versus 371 benefits from mix/network changes and does not contradict the same-hotel decline.

Retail gross costs increased from 47.3% to 47.9% of retail sales. Hotel costs improved from 62.7% to 62.1% of manachised plus leased revenue, but selling/marketing and technology investment must also be paid. Government-related other operating income was only 1.342m this quarter. TTM other operating income is much larger because of earlier periods; it is excluded from recurring forecast EBIT rather than assuming all subsidies persist.

FY2026 revenue guidance is 33–35% growth over FY2025. It is an issuer outlook, not our forecast certainty. Current TTM revenue is not full-year guidance. Retail demand, inventory/product innovation, marketing efficiency, franchisee supply-chain service economics and same-hotel demand are the principal operating checks.

## Cash migration is not capex or cash burn

Q3 investing outflow was 1,702.530m. Net purchases of short-term investments were 1,700.072m; actual physical/intangible purchases were 3.241m, with 0.783m disposal proceeds. Thus cash moving into wealth-management products largely explains investing outflow. It is neither acquisition spending nor evidence of operating losses. Conversely those products are not risk-free cash merely because they are current assets.

September unrestricted cash was 2,747.126m, short-term investments 3,509.560m and separately restricted cash 21.732m. Debt was 237m. The 20-F classifies investment products with maturities below a year as short-term investments, measured using level-2 inputs. Scenarios haircut their value by 5%/2%/0%, explicitly an analyst stress, not a disclosed impairment.

The annual note identifies 720.937m collected or receivable on behalf of franchisees within 1,090.394m accrued liabilities; these funds are not automatically shareholder surplus. Applying the same proportion to current accrued liabilities gives a 723.477m segregation proxy. This is a conservative gross proxy: some underlying collections may still be receivable, and the current composition is unpublished. It is not an exact current trust balance. No permanent forecast growth is funded by increasing franchisee float.

## Legal distribution and declared dividend

The second 6-K exhibit declares $0.17 per ordinary share/$0.51 per ADS, approximately $69m total, payable around December 9 to November 23 holders of record. Deposit fees and applicable taxes may affect the amount received. We do not certify individual entitlement from the record date alone. The May payment of approximately $73m was already paid; it is not deducted again from the September balance.

The corporate cash bridge deducts the new $69m liability. For a cum-distribution valuation of the existing shareholder claim, its discounted payment is added separately. Ex-declared-dividend values are also provided. Subtracting the liability and omitting the corresponding shareholder distribution would understate the current combined claim; adding it without reducing corporate cash would double-count it. The approximate $69m total and dated share proxy will not reconcile exactly to $0.51 per ADS.

Annual statutory reserve was 375.495m, with at least 10% of statutory profit generally appropriated until 50% of registered capital. Restricted subsidiary net assets were 1,156.707m. These are not identical to restricted cash or amounts that can mechanically be netted from bank balances. They demonstrate limits on upstreaming. The annual parent-only balance had cash 697.085m and debt 210.120m, but is too old to certify current parent liquidity after dividends, buybacks and subsidiary transfers.

At December 2025, 2.4bn undistributed PRC earnings had no accrued withholding because of intended indefinite reinvestment. Full upstreaming could incur tax. The model uses domestic core tax 30%/25%/25%, adds back non-deductible SBC to taxable income, funds growth, and then applies 10%/10%/5% withholding to positive remittable cash. The favorable 5% requires treaty eligibility and is not certified. A past reported effective tax rate that already included withholding is not layered onto another identical withholding charge.

The closing bridge segregates third-party funds and retains 2,500/2,000/1,500m operating/legal capital reserves, roughly 1.5–3 months of current operating expenses. These incorporate buffers for restricted capital; the full restricted-net-asset amount is not deducted again. Only 50%/75%/100% of the remaining gross pool is assumed accessible, then withholding is charged. Debt, declared dividend and NCI allowances are deducted in full after this accessibility adjustment, not multiplied by a cash haircut. NCI book value is negative but not added as an asset; scenario claims 30/15/0m are explicit bounds, not fair-value estimates. Access sensitivities include zero distribution of the residual pool. No scenario is legal certification of distributability.

## Forecast assumptions

Revenue is forecast separately for manachised hotels, leased hotels, retail and other activities. Forecast operating costs use source-based hotel, retail and other gross costs plus marketing, G&A and technology. All SBC is presented on a separate shared line solely for modeling; the aggregate initial EBIT exactly matches reported EBIT less other operating income. This allocation does not assert that all reported SBC actually belongs to G&A or that reported segment profit exists.

Hotel growth assumptions are 8%/14%/20%; retail 12%/22%/30% for the first five years. Leased revenue initially declines 5%. Over years six through ten growth fades to 3%/4%/5% RMB nominal. Costs move gradually to explicit case assumptions: central holds trailing ratios, adverse increases hotel/retail/marketing/G&A burden, favorable assumes limited productivity. All are analyst scenarios, not issuer guidance.

Annual reinvestment equals the greater of positive incremental NOPAT divided by 15%/20%/25% incremental ROC, or physical/software capex net of depreciation plus retail working capital and hotel startup capital. Retail incremental capital is 20%/15%/10% of added sales, hotel startup 8%/5%/3%, gross physical/software capex 1.0%/0.8%/0.6% of total sales. This explicitly rejects extrapolating current 0.22% TTM capex forever. The returns are scenarios, not measured segment ROIC; public data lack current segment assets. Terminal reinvestment respects the same economic floor and NOPAT × g/ROC.

Domestic negative flows, if any, consume only the post-reserve pool; franchisee cash and restricted reserves cannot finance them for free. All principal cases pass this model's floor, but it is not a quarterly bank or legal-distribution test. The interim CFO residual remains unresolved and the forecast does not claim it is reproduced by an undisclosed working-capital schedule.

## Currency, hurdle and sensitivities

The reference exchange rate 6.7045 CNY/USD is explicitly the release's September 30 translation rate, not a verified November 9 spot quote. Forecast CNY depreciation is 2%/1.5%/1% annually. Each RMB cash flow is converted at that year's modeled FX before USD discounting. Terminal USD growth is (1 + RMB growth)/(1 + currency depreciation) − 1. This avoids discounting perpetually growing RMB cash at a fixed FX without recognizing the currency assumption. Reference FX sensitivities 6.4/6.7045/7.0 are included.

The common 12% USD hurdle is an analyst return requirement, not a company-estimated WACC. Current context is November 9 Treasury 4.88%, November ERP 4.41%, and inherited July China premium about 0.873%. A loading of 1.0–1.5 gives a rough equity-cost context of 10.16–12.37%; the beta is not independently regressed and the country premium is dated. The old A360 short-listing-history beta 0.67 and roughly 8.5% discount are not mechanically carried forward. No exact market cost is claimed.

At 10% discount the conditional case values are about $17.27/$36.08/$78.10; at 14%, $11.67/$20.63/$39.74. At 12%, terminal weights are 44.8%/67.5%/76.2%. Central value falls to about $24.77/$21.99 if future SBC rate doubles/triples, including its nondeductible tax treatment. The favorable case and apparent upside are especially sensitive to long-run capital returns and terminal economics.

One price-matching solution at the 12% central framework requires approximately 32.64% annual retail growth for five years, fading thereafter, versus 22% in the central case. It passes the model's domestic funding floor, but carries 81.2% terminal weight and does not certify legal distributions. It is one conditional solution, not the growth rate the market uniquely requires. Changing hotel growth, costs, tax, cash accessibility, FX or discount produces other solutions.

Huazhu and other hotel operators are useful operational comparisons, but Atour retail is over 40% of trailing sales and has different marketing/working-capital economics. No unverified peer multiple is used to validate the DCF. Homogeneous historical/peer valuation remains limited.

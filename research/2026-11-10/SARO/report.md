This is a dated bridge, not a certified November 10 balance.

The credit agreement resolves the springing covenant: section 7.08 uses a 40% revolver test net of qualifying adjusted cash, excluding letters of credit; if triggered the first-lien net leverage ceiling is 7.00×. Triggering a test is not a breach. Restricted-payment permission and exact lender EBITDA require their own contractual certification; we do not infer compliance simply from the company's ASR announcement.

The $180m OEM license was paid in Q3 and is already in the cash balance. It must not be deducted again as an unpaid obligation. Yet eliminating it permanently from owner costs would make renewal and access free. The model charges an annual economic license reserve with 6/8/12-year scenarios, plus a separate reserve for legacy licenses. These durations are analyst assumptions, not the new contract's disclosed term. The 10-Q's 7.9-year weighted remaining life applies to the entire $281.998m net OEM portfolio, not solely the new $180m.

## License and replacement framework

Operating cash earnings retain corporate costs and all historical excluded costs including SBC. They deduct physical depreciation, an economic annual license charge and partial customer/trademark/other intangible replacement. This is an economic EBIT, not GAAP EBIT.

New-license annual cost is $180m divided by the assumed 6/8/12 years. Legacy reserve is $129.474m divided by its prior 12.4-year remaining life. Customer/trademark/technology replacement starts with current net balances divided by their reported remaining lives, then charges 75%/50%/25% by adverse/central/favorable case. These are disclosed carrying-value heuristics, not certified renewal quotes. Source remaining lives are 11.3 years for customer relationships, 7.8 for trademarks and 4.9 for other technology.

The model taxes economic EBIT at 27%/25%/24%. It does not assert that this economic expense equals the deductible cash-tax schedule. Discrete PPA tax shields, jurisdictional tax attributes and license deductions could change cash tax timing. No separate tax-shield asset is added. Annual replacement is modeled as a steady reserve instead of an exact future lumpy renewal payment. A lumpy contract-cash schedule could worsen liquidity.

## Funded growth rather than free margin expansion

The first five years separately forecast Engine and Component sales and margins. The following five fade growth and margins toward mature assumptions. Annual reinvestment is the greater of:
1. Physical maintenance above physical depreciation plus incremental sales × marginal working-capital intensity.
2. Positive actual incremental economic NOPAT divided by incremental ROC.

Thus both sales growth and margin-driven incremental profit receive funding, unlike a revenue-growth-only reinvestment rule. This can be conservative where genuine cost savings need little capital; it is not proof that every dollar of productivity requires the same investment. Costs already charged are not relabeled cash investments without disclosure. Terminal reinvestment is the greater of the physical/working-capital floor and NOPAT × g/ROC.

Marginal working capital assumptions 35%/28%/22% are anchored to material cash tied in receivables, contract assets and inventories. At September 30, broad operating NWC is about $1,669.515m, or 26.15% of TTM sales. Nine-month changes are seasonally adverse and not annualized mechanically. NWC plus net PPE, intangibles and goodwill is approximately $5,234.640m; GAAP after-tax EBIT over this ending capital is about 8.9%. Incremental ROC 10%/14%/18% explicitly assumes varying utilization/productivity, not an observed company ROIC. The favorable return is demanding relative to the historical proxy.

## Conditional results and discount calibration

At a common 12% analyst discount hurdle, no-ASR diagnostic values are approximately $3.62/$7.30/$13.35; hypothetical ASR $2.65/$6.47/$12.75. They are not approved fair values or trade limits. Terminal weights are 38.7%/52.1%/59.5%. Lower growth can initially produce more cash than favorable growth because the latter requires more investment. This is deliberate, not a case-order error.

The 12% is an analyst hurdle, not a statistically estimated company WACC. Dated November 9 Treasury 4.88% and November implied ERP 4.41% supply market context; a judgmental risk loading of 1.5 produces 11.495% cost of equity before any model-risk overlay. Debt weighting would reduce a conventional WACC. We therefore show common 10%/12%/14% sensitivities rather than portray 12% as an empirical estimate. At 10%, the same cases are about $6.32/$12.04/$21.07; at 14%, $1.81/$4.26/$8.55. The market inputs are dated November 9 and November 1 respectively. No independently regressed beta is claimed.

The model does not reach the $28.97 scanner price even when the inverse search increases Engine margin endpoints by 30 percentage points while retaining its growth/capital rules. That extreme branch requires about $8.1bn revolving financing, far above the facility. It is an infeasible diagnostic, not a meaningful required margin or valid price downside. There is no certified inverse valuation. Changing growth, capital efficiency, replacement needs and discount rate together could produce different economics; none is selected solely to match price.

## What remains to establish

1. The actual ASR agreement, funding date, initial delivery, ultimate average price and settlement. Current disclosures establish intention, not consummation.
2. New OEM contract term, renewal/replacement pricing and volume obligations. Aggregated amortization lives cannot answer these exactly.
3. Segment physical capital and working capital requirements. The issuer does not disclose segment asset totals; consolidated investment bounds remain estimates.
4. Formal lender EBITDA, restricted-payment basket, qualifying cash and covenant calculation after any ASR; quarterly rather than annual liquidity and license payment timing.
5. Tax-shield schedule, current award register and option valuation; homogeneous historical/peer valuation comparison remains limited.

These limits are not excuses to omit available data: the report supplies explicit operating, replacement, funding and share scenarios. They prevent labeling the output an unconditional target. GE Aerospace/RTX are OEM-heavy and AAR has distribution/airframe mix, so their headline multiples cannot simply value independent engine MRO. No unsupported peer multiple is substituted for cash analysis.

## Additional capital and funding sensitivities

At central operating assumptions, changing the new-license economic life from 6 to 12 years and PPA replacement from 100% to 0% produces conditional no-ASR diagnostics of approximately $6.11–$8.47 at the 12% hurdle. This deliberately varies uncertain economics rather than pretending the eight-year central life is a contractual fact.

If margin gains are attributed to low-capital productivity and the second reinvestment rule uses NOPAT × revenue growth / ROC instead of actual incremental NOPAT / ROC, conditional diagnostics become $3.62/$8.24/$14.97. This does not grant the favorable productivity for free: observed operating expenses, maintenance, license replacement and marginal NWC remain charged. Neither rule is certified as the single correct forecast.

ASR liquidity is especially sensitive to retained cash and seasonal needs. At a $100m reserve/$250m additional seasonal draw, or a $150m reserve/$200m seasonal draw, the nominal $750m revolver would be exceeded by about $38.671m. At a $200m reserve/$250m seasonal draw, the excess is $138.671m. Such branches are unfunded unless additional cash generation, a smaller/delayed ASR or independently supported financing closes the gap. Their arithmetic equity outputs are not feasible valuation endpoints. Changing the retained reserve does not itself evade the covenant spring test: net utilization remains approximately $438.671m before subsequent operating cash or other changes.

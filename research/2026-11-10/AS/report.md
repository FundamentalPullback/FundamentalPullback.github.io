## What changed

Outstanding refund claims are immaterial and no receivable was recognized at June 30. Recognition-versus-collection uncertainty is resolved, but gross receipts and the cash tax timing cannot be reverse-engineered from the net COGS benefit.
2. Removing that $64.3 operating benefit lowers Q2 EBIT from $191.7 to $127.4, or 7.80% of $1,632.6 sales. H1 EBIT falls from $512.7 to $448.4 (12.53%); TTM EBIT from $956.6 to $892.3 (12.00%). These are ex-refund bridges, **not fully normalized owner earnings**: other nonrecurring items, leases and growth investment still require separate work. The $3.3 refund interest is nonoperating and must not also be removed from EBIT.

## Cash economics and obligations
H1 CFO $338.5 less cash PPE $135.3 and intangibles $36.2 yields $167.0. Further subtracting financing-classified lease principal $87.2 and direct ROU cash acquisitions $5.7 gives $74.1. This is a transparent cash bridge, not FCFF or a sustainable annual run rate. It retains seasonality, working capital, interest and refund cash effects; acquisition cash of $10.0 is also outside that bridge. Do not simply subtract $64.3 from CFO and claim exact refund-adjusted cash, because net cost recognition and gross receipts differ.

At June 30 cash is $720.4, borrowings $147.4 and lease liabilities $954.2. Ex-lease net cash is $573.0; debt plus leases less cash is $381.2. An operating-rent valuation should deduct appropriate rent cash flows and exclude lease debt from the equity bridge; a capitalized-lease valuation must explicitly fund new leased assets and deduct lease debt. Mixing the two would double count obligations. H1 total D&A $215.1 does not itself identify the ROU allocation, and cash PPE alone understates store expansion investment.

The March offering issued 23,695,055 shares for gross proceeds $862.5. Cash-flow net share proceeds are $836.1, while note 11 records issuance costs $27.1; timing/classification differences should not be silently forced to equal. H1 debt principal repayment $800.0 and cash premium $43.1 demonstrate that debt reduction was largely equity-financed rather than internally generated. Extinguishment expense $50.5 includes $7.4 noncash discount/cost write-off. Do not subtract the expense again as a cash payment.

Q2 weighted-average diluted shares are 589,086,863 versus basic 582,577,137; neither is automatically current spot fully diluted shares. At June 30, 7,338,878 options (weighted strike $10.06), 1,699,195 RSUs and 4,039,836 PSUs remain. Avoid adding all awards on top of an already diluted weighted average or treating all performance awards as unconditional shares. Recurring SBC remains economic compensation; the prior checkpoint distinguishes H1 $29.9 noncash cash-flow addback from $30.9 total compensation expense.

Consolidated H1 profit includes $16.0 attributable to minority interests, versus $7.7 a year earlier. Balance-sheet minority equity is $21.9; this book value is not a defensible market-value deduction for minority earnings. $13.0 dividends were declared to minorities, $6.7 paid and $6.3 payable. Ownership-level valuation and any put/claim review remain open. A consolidated DCF cannot silently give all subsidiary profits to AS shareholders.

## Business, upside and downside
Amer's premium sporting-goods portfolio includes Arc'teryx, Salomon and Wilson. DTC expansion and premium product demand can compound revenue and improve mix. FY2025 revenue grew 26.7% to $6,566.2; H1 2026 revenue grew 32.1% to $3,578.1. However, current margin improvement must be separated from refunds, channel/geographic mix, lease-funded store expansion and equity-funded deleveraging. Premium-brand execution, Greater China exposure, tariffs, inventory accumulation, currency, discretionary demand and governance/concentrated ownership warrant explicit risk treatment. June inventory $1,896.7 versus December $1,622.1 is a 16.9% increase; that sequential seasonal change alone does not prove deterioration.

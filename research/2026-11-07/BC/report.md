# Brunswick (BC): recovery is real, the price already demands a great deal
Research date: 7 November 2026. Evaluation price: $77.23, 6 November scanner snapshot. Opportunity 80; quality points 70 are supplied scanner scores, not independent fundamental quality conclusions. The $77.23 close was independently checked against Stock Analysis for 6 November 2026, 16:00 EST. This is research for paper trading, not an order or an entry approval.

## Decision
Do not advance an entry at the observed price. This is a **price-dependent valuation rejection**, not a permanent rejection of Brunswick's business. A funded ten-year FCFF model gives $5.62 adverse, $37.25 central and $78.81 favorable. These are conditional scenarios, not probabilities or hard bounds. The favorable case provides only 2.1% upside, insufficient compensation for marine cyclicality, leverage and execution risk. Central value is 51.8% below the observed price. No approved valuation, entry level, stop or position size is established.

Primary investor calendar checked on 7 November: Q3 was reported 29 October; no upcoming event was scheduled. Do not invent the next earnings date. CEO succession to Aine Denari is effective 1 January 2027, a known execution checkpoint.

## Business, durability and the cycle
Mercury engines and high-horsepower mix are the most attractive manufacturing franchise. Engine parts and accessories monetize the installed fleet and usage, which helps separate cash generation from new-boat unit purchases. Navico electronics offer cross-selling and integration benefits but repeated impairments reveal capital-allocation mistakes. Boats and Freedom Boat Club add distribution and customer access, with greater consumer, inventory and fleet-investment sensitivity.

FY2025 external revenue was $1,908.1M propulsion, $1,210.1M Engine P&A, $720.5M Navico and $1,524.1M boats. These sum to consolidated sales; segment headline sales before eliminations must not be added. Approximately 36%, 23%, 13% and 28% of consolidated revenue came from those businesses. Management reports lean boat/engine pipelines; this is a qualitative claim, not independently measured end-customer sell-through. OEM shipments, retail registrations and factory inventory are different measures. Latest factory inventory was $1,289.5M, versus $1,192.3M at year end. An improvement in absorption as factories produce more does not establish sustainable end demand.

Pros: five consecutive quarters of reported sales growth across all segments; aftermarket participation; pricing and premium mix; lower debt; substantial undrawn revolver. Cons: discretionary financing-sensitive purchases; recurring restructuring; Navico and Fliteboard impairments; production absorption volatility; acquisition and software investment; working-capital timing; and ambitious margins already required by the market price.

## Balance sheet and contingent claims
Debt carrying value is $2,001.5M, down from $2,102.2M at year end; current debt $195.1M. Available revolver capacity is $984.2M and management reports covenant compliance. Cash $257.8M plus liquid securities $10.8M excludes $10.8M restricted cash. Reserve $150M for operations and seasonal needs, leaving $118.6M excess; this is an analyst liquidity assumption, not management's minimum. The debt bridge uses carrying value as a proxy for principal: year-end gross debt of $2,120.8M was reduced by $18.6M of discounts and fees to $2,102.2M book debt. The latest quarterly bridge does not separately identify the remaining discount/fees. Using the prior $18.6M as an illustrative additional deduction lowers value $0.28/share; this is not an assertion of current principal. Add equity investments at book $30.3M, without capitalizing their earnings in operating FCFF; subtract $35.4M postretirement benefits. Book investment value is an approximation: writing it off lowers value only $0.46/share. All-cash-as-excess would add $2.29/share and does not change the verdict.

Operating leases remain operating costs in modeled margins and are not also deducted as debt. Latest noncurrent operating lease liability is $197.8M; the lease asset is $223.1M. This convention prevents double counting. Pension/postretirement liabilities are deducted once; no separate pension deduction is made from the normalized cash flow.

Dealer finance creates contingent exposure beyond ordinary debt. FY2025 maximum repurchase cash exposure was $85.9M and recourse customer guarantees $9.7M. These gross contractual amounts are not expected losses: recovered boats/engines mitigate loss and historical accruals were immaterial. They are not blindly deducted at face value, but $95.6M gross stress would reduce equity value by $1.46/share before recoveries. A dealer-default wave could also damage future sales, so gross cash stress alone understates a severe downturn. Updated quarterly maximum exposure was not separately identified; annual amounts are explicitly dated, not presented as current balances.

## Ten-year FCFF and reinvestment
Nominal USD, five explicit years plus five-year transition. Year one is a full forward model year from the November 6 evaluation using the TTM run-rate, not a precise FY2026 cash entitlement. There is no partial-year stub; this convention is approximate and explicit. Starting sales are actual TTM 5,723.6. Tax is normalized at 23%, not Q2's temporary tax benefit. Forecast EBIT is an economic pre-intangible-amortization margin: physical depreciation, SBC, R&D, lease expense and ordinary operating costs stay deducted. The model does not also add intangible amortization to FCFF, and it conservatively gives no separate acquisition-amortization tax shield.

Central revenue growth: 5%, 4.5%, 4%, 3.5%, 3%, then transition to 2.5%. Margins: 7.5%, 8.5%, 9%, 9.5%, 10%, then 9.5% through-cycle. Adverse growth −5%, 0%, 2%, 2.5%, 2.5%; margins 5.5% to 7.5%. Favorable growth 8%, 6.5%, 5.5%, 4.5%, 4%; margins 8.5% to 12%, fading to 11%. These scenarios deliberately do not extrapolate peak 2023 profitability or the Q3 2026 margin indefinitely.

Physical maintenance D&A and replacement capex both equal 3.8% of revenue. Additional capacity capex is positive incremental revenue divided by 1.4/1.8/2.2 adverse/base/favorable capital turnover. Additional working capital is 15% of positive revenue change. Downturns receive no free liquidation cash. These are conservative capital-intensity judgments, not company guidance. All explicit-year growth is funded. Terminal reinvestment is the greater of NOPAT × g / incremental ROIC and incremental terminal revenue × (1 / sales-to-capital + 15% working capital), with g 2%/2.5%/3%, ROIC 10%/13%/16%. Terminal capex equals physical replacement plus terminal reinvestment less incremental working capital, funding both physical replacement and the explicit expansion/working-capital floor in every case. The physical floor binds in all three direct cases; the economic ROIC formula binds in the inverse solutions. This avoids silently improving capital efficiency after year ten.

WACC 11.5%/10%/9% reflects cyclicality, debt and execution, not a reverse-engineered rate. The Federal Reserve's 6 November H.15 release showed a recent 10-year Treasury observation of 4.84%; the release date is not necessarily the observation date. Central calibration uses roughly 4.9% risk-free + assumed 1.5 beta × 5% equity premium = 12.4% equity cost; 28% debt weight and 5.7% pretax debt cost give about 10.2% WACC, rounded to 10%. Beta, ERP and marginal debt rate are analyst assumptions; not live vendor measurements. A 9% bull WACC is already generous for a leveraged leisure manufacturer.

Shares stay at 65.6M, a conservative recent diluted count above 64.455M period-end common shares. No future repurchase reduction is credited. Terminal value accounts for 42.5%/54.1%/61.5% of EV. Files expose every yearly row, terminal calculation and equity bridge.

## Inverse, sensitivity and corroboration
At central growth and discount assumptions, $77.23 requires adding 4.25 percentage points to the entire modeled margin path, taking terminal economic EBIT margin from 9.5% to 13.75%. Growth one percentage point slower/faster in years 1–5 requires terminal margins 14.08%/13.45%. These are combinations, not unique market forecasts; inverse and direct DCF are the same model, not independent evidence. Every root is bracketed, monotonic and verified with a residual below $0.000001/share.

Changing central sales-to-capital from 1.8 to 1.3/2.3 produces $29.29/$41.76. Terminal ROIC 10%/16% gives $36.56/$37.25. Slower recovery gives $33.21. The WACC/g matrix is in valuation data. All variations preserve the same terminal physical funding floor. None of these individual reasonable changes closes the market-price gap.

At $77.23, company guided adjusted EPS $4.65 implies 16.6×; TTM continuing GAAP earnings imply about 23.7×. Neither multiple proves cheapness because GAAP/adjusted definitions and tariff refunds differ. Malibu's September 2026 quarter adjusted EBITDA margin was 5.7%, while MasterCraft FY2026 was 13.1%, with acquisitions and changed reporting periods. These are operating-cycle checks, **not homogeneous EV/EBITDA comps**, and must not be equated with BC's EBIT margin. Mercury and recurring aftermarket deserve a different mix premium from boat-only peers. No current same-date peer valuation or historical homogeneous multiple panel is claimed. That corroboration remains a research limitation, not the reason the FCFF fails at the observed price.

## Revisit criteria and limitations
Reconsider with lower price or evidence of sustained organic retail demand, stable dealer financing, cash conversion without refund/working-capital boosts, controlled software/fleet/capacity spending, and achieved margins through weaker seasons. Demand weakness, dealer defaults, renewed tariff pressure or persistent impairment/restructuring would weaken the thesis. Confidence is medium in accounting bridges and low-to-medium in terminal cycle estimates. No quantified retail-registration series or independent channel inventory audit was established; management pipeline commentary is clearly labeled. Detailed homogeneous peer/historical trading-multiple work remains open, so this is conditional research rather than a fully approved Stage 3 valuation. Ordinary forecast assumptions are explicit, not artificial missing-data blockers. Stage 3.5 was not undertaken because observed price fails the valuation test.

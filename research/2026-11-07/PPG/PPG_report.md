# PPG | Better sales, limited operating leverage, insufficient valuation support
Research date: 7 November 2026. USD unless stated. Decision: **Observation / wait for valuation; no entry approval.** This is a completed conditional operating valuation, not a trade recommendation or an assertion that legal liabilities have been quantified.

## Resumen en español
PPG mejora ventas, pero el crecimiento sostenible del beneficio es mucho menor que el crecimiento publicado. El beneficio operativo antes de otras partidas aumenta aproximadamente 4% en TTM, frente a 23% del EBIT publicado desapalancado. A $122,38, cierre del 6 de noviembre, el precio supera los tres escenarios FCFF de esta revisión: aproximadamente $40 / $74 / $106. No se aprueba entrada ni se inventa un soporte técnico. El juicio por emisiones de óxido de etileno es un riesgo vivo: el 10-Q programa selección de jurado para primeros de noviembre y finalización prevista en diciembre; no confirma que la selección haya ocurrido ni permite estimar pérdidas. Se conserva en observación por valoración, crecimiento moderado y riesgo jurídico no cuantificado.

## Business quality and the current recovery
PPG combines global architectural coatings, performance coatings, and industrial coatings. Aerospace qualification, customer-specific formulations and repaint demand support pricing and retention. These advantages do not eliminate automotive/industrial cyclicality, raw-material inflation, customer concentration or currency risk.

Third-quarter sales increased 6.6% to $4,353m: company attribution is 3% price, 2% volume, 1% currency and 1% acquisitions, rounded. Organic growth is 5%, not 7%. Global Architectural segment income rose from $184m to $225m and Performance from $272m to $332m, while Industrial fell from $233m to $200m despite higher sales. Nine-month Industrial income fell from $675m to $622m. Corporate costs also rose. Thus aerospace and Latin American architectural strength are offset by other businesses and costs. The base case assumes organic growth, not free future acquisitions.

The US/Canada architectural business was sold in December 2024 and is discontinued operations. Historical financials below use the 2025 annual report's continuing-operation presentation, not old consolidated sales including that business. Other portfolio disposals within continuing operations still impair strict like-for-like annual comparisons. Segment presentation is the current three-segment basis. Do not add divestiture proceeds to operating FCF or treat shrinking divested sales as organic deterioration.

## Earnings and cash normalization
1. TTM sales rose 6.39%. Published unlevered EBIT rose 22.65%, but the operating subtotal rose just 4.01%, from $2,172m to $2,259m. Subtotal margin declined from 13.84% to 13.53%. The large published increase is substantially comparison-base effects, not broad margin expansion.
2. FY2024 included $233m restructuring and $146m impairment/related charges; FY2023 included a $190m pension settlement. FY2025 included $6m restructuring and $24m impairment. These are excluded from the before-other subtotal, but costs inside cost of sales/SG&A/depreciation remain. The forecast does not presume restructuring becomes free: it starts from that subtotal less an additional $50m annual recurring other-cost allowance, producing $2,209m EBIT (13.23%). Applying the same allowance to prior TTM gives $2,122m, or 4.10% growth. This is an analyst normalization, not management's adjusted EBIT or a measured exact recurring-cost estimate.
3. That $50m allowance covers a recurrent mix of environmental, reorganization and miscellaneous operating cash costs otherwise lost by excluding the net Other line. Current environmental outflows are guided at $20–60m annually for 2027–2030; 2026 restructuring outlays are $60–80m, some costs already embedded in operating expense. A further $50m recurring pre-tax cost scaled with sales reduces base value to approximately $72.74; this does not rescue a purchase. The separate unquantifiable ethylene-oxide liability is not included in this operating allowance.
4. Acquisition amortization remains an EBIT expense; it is added back only once with all D&A in FCFF. SBC remains an economic expense, with no addback in FCFF. TTM SBC is $48m. Q3 diluted shares 223.2m versus 226.5m a year earlier and nine-month shares 223.8m versus 227.7m show buyback support to EPS. Basic shares outstanding at September 30 are 220.9m; the model conservatively uses the 223.2m diluted quarterly denominator without projecting buybacks. Anti-dilutive options remain a contingent dilution risk.
5. Cash FCF rises from $697m to $1,284m, but this is not a new permanent margin: CFO is affected by receivables, inventories, payables, tax timing and restructuring. Nine-month CFO +$113m includes more favorable payable and tax/interest movements, partially offset by other current assets and other items. Q3 standalone CFO falls to $566m from $676m, while capex rises to $155m from $147m. A strong TTM cash headline therefore coexists with softer quarterly conversion.
6. Operating working capital at September 30 is $3,323m, or 19.1% of annualized quarterly sales, versus 19.6% a year earlier. The forecast charges 19% of incremental sales (20% bear). This differs from total balance-sheet working capital. Do not extrapolate the seasonally lower year-end working-capital ratio or assume perpetual releases.
7. Capex was $778m in FY2025, $765m TTM, and $464m nine months 2026. Current 2026 guidance is $650–700m. The model funds 4.0% of sales (4.2% bear), plus additional investment whenever growth/ROIC requires more. No separate software-capitalization amount is identified in the retrieved statements. The forecast treats total investment as encompassing necessary software and plant investment, without inventing a software addback or assuming it is zero; undisclosed amounts outside this envelope would reduce value.
8. The 24% cash-tax modeling proxy is near nine-month 24.0% and Q3 24.2%, rather than extending FY2025's lower effective rate. An effective-rate calibration is not proof of a normalized cash-tax rate. Non-service pension costs are removed with Other; service cost stays in operating expense. The balance bridge deducts an after-tax proxy for noncurrent recognized pension and other postretirement obligations, avoiding treating them as free capital. This after-tax treatment assumes deductibility; current claims remain unreconciled and are sensitized below.

## FCFF valuation: ten years, three funded cases
Years 1–5 are explicit, years 6–10 transition linearly to terminal growth/margins. Forecast years are rolling annual periods after the September 2026 TTM base, valued at the November 6 quote; no five-week stub adjustment is applied. Year-one cash arrives at the end of the first full forward year, all cash flows use end-year discounting, and terminal value is at the end of year ten. All figures are nominal USD. There is no sale multiple substituted for FCFF.

FCFF = EBIT × 76% + D&A − capex − change in operating working capital. D&A = 3.2% of sales, near TTM. Additional reinvestment is charged when max(0, current NOPAT − previous NOPAT) / incremental ROIC exceeds physical capex less D&A plus working capital. Terminal economic reinvestment is terminal growth × next-year NOPAT / incremental ROIC. Terminal reinvestment is the greater of this economic requirement and physical investment plus working capital; it is not capped at the lower figure. Acquisition growth already in the base is not purchased again; the forecast includes no additional acquisition contribution or proceeds.

|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|Bear|−2%, 0%, 1%, 2%, 2%|12% / 12%|10%|2%|10%|$39.78|
|Base|4% each year|14% / 14%|9%|2.5%|13%|$73.69|

In year one, opening normalized NOPAT is $1,678.84m and forecast NOPAT $1,766.21m. At 13% incremental ROIC, funding the $87.37m earnings increase requires $672.07m net reinvestment. The physical baseline is $694.39m capex less $555.51m D&A plus $126.86m working capital, or $265.74m. An additional $406.33m economic investment brings modeled gross investment to $1,100.72m, above company capex guidance. This broad investment envelope funds both revenue and margin improvement and is not reported capex guidance. Terminal base reinvestment is $496m versus $306m physical requirement.

The base grows more slowly than current organic sales and funds modest margin recovery above 13.23% normalized starting margin, not a return to an arbitrary peak. Bull requires sustained aerospace/architectural strength, industrial recovery, scale leverage and better incremental returns. Bear allows weak industrial demand and persistent cost pressure. Terminal 3% in bull is nominal and requires continuing reinvestment, not a free perpetuity.

Discount calibration: November 6 US ten-year yield is approximately 4.86%. A 5% equity risk premium and beta near 1 give roughly 9.86% cost of equity; 20% debt weight at 6% marginal pretax debt cost and 24% tax imply about 8.8% WACC, rounded to 9%. These are analyst financing assumptions, not observed company borrowing quotes. The historical low accounting interest rate is inappropriate for marginal funding. The 8.5–10% case range reflects operating/financing scenarios; **no additional legal-risk WACC surcharge** is imposed. [Rate reference](https://api.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/11/06/treasury-yields-snapshot-november-6-2026).

Enterprise-to-equity bridge, $m: balance-sheet carrying debt proxy $6,841 less cash/short-term investments $1,642, plus $333.84 minimum operating cash (2% sales), plus $707.56 after-tax noncurrent recognized pension/other postretirement liabilities (76% × $931m), plus $143m noncontrolling interests = $6,383.40 deduction. The carrying-debt proxy is not verified contractual face: discounts and derivative adjustments remain unreconciled. Finance leases already included in debt must not be deducted again. The $931m pension/OPEB figure omits current liabilities. At December 2025, current pension/OPEB was $75m and pension assets were separately $126m. Applying that dated current-claim figure diagnostically reduces equity by $57m after assumed tax, or $0.2554/share; it is not a September 2026 measurement. No pension asset is treated as unrestricted cash. The September current-claim/asset reconciliation remains an explicit gap. Lease rent remains in EBIT; operating lease debt is not deducted a second time. Investments of $403m receive no separate credit, because realizable excess value is not established; this is conservative and would add at most $1.81/share at full book value. Giving credit for all operating cash adds $1.50/share; using gross pension obligations instead reduces value by $1.00/share. Neither changes the decision.

Terminal value represents 44.6% / 59.3% / 66.0% of enterprise value in bear/base/bull. This dependence makes growth, margin and reinvestment sensitivity essential.

### Conditional five-year shareholder returns
Exit EV at year five equals the discounted remaining FCFF and terminal value at that date. Debt/cash bridge and diluted shares are held fixed; $2.96 annual dividends are assumed, without growth or reinvestment. There is no buyback benefit or credit for excess cash accumulation. These restrictive financing assumptions are explicitly conditional, not expected total returns. From $122.38, calculated five-year annual returns are approximately −15.1% bear, −2.0% base, +5.8% bull, with exit prices $43.15 / $96.43 / $145.80. Changing dividend policy, debt or cash retention changes these returns; dividends are not added to present intrinsic value.

## Risks, evidence gaps and decision gates
The first ethylene-oxide trial is a real near-term catalyst. The issuer cannot reasonably estimate losses. Do not translate awards against unrelated defendants into a PPG liability, assume insurance recovery, or multiply plaintiffs by an arbitrary award. Pure arithmetic stress: an additional after-tax cash loss of $100m / $500m / $1bn would subtract $0.45 / $2.24 / $4.48 per share at today's share count. These amounts have no assigned probability and are not predictions or an upper bound. They are separate from recurring environmental costs and the WACC assumptions.

Other risks include industrial price/cost lag, aerospace capacity constraints, FX translation, geopolitical/raw-material disruption, pension assumptions, restructuring execution, legacy environmental obligations and the possibility that investment required for growth exceeds the modeled floor. An apparent adjusted-EPS beat can coexist with weaker cash or consolidated margin.

Exact unresolved disclosures: separately quantified software investment, realized value of non-operating investments, a reliable ethylene-oxide loss range, and a newly confirmed next earnings date. The current pension-claim and face-debt reconciliations are also incomplete. These gaps limit the bridge to a conditional proxy; they are not reasons to leave all operating valuation work undone. Forecast growth/margin/ROIC, operating-cash requirement, taxes and recurring-cost allowance are ordinary modeling judgments, transparently sensitized rather than labeled disclosure blockers.

Reopen the valuation if industrial profit stabilizes, normalized TTM EBIT growth sustainably exceeds 5%, cash conversion improves without relying on payable/tax releases, the trial materially changes legal exposure, or price changes sufficiently. A possible future entry additionally needs current EMA20/50/200 and long-trend assessment, support/stop, market/sector filters, settled cash, portfolio equity and aggregate risk. The scanner does not contain those proofs. No trade, order, position size or technical entry has been created.

## Audit conclusion
Three annual periods, four latest quarters, TTM and prior TTM are reconstructed from primary statements; all reported and derived measures are separated. The reproducible model includes ten-year scenarios funding revenue and margin growth, inverse valuation, reinvestment floors, equity bridge, rate/growth sensitivity and explicitly financed conditional returns. Subtotal-to-normalized EBIT remains an analyst framework, not a claim that every adjustment is nonrecurring. Observation is supported by valuation even before unquantifiable litigation; it is not a definitive assertion that the company is impaired.

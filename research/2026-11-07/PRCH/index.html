Gate and business perimeter

The complete loaded published-report library and stock-profile engine were checked: no PRCH report, analysis, completed decision or management entry was found. The business is economically insurance services, an owned captive reinsurer, software/data and consumer services, rather than ordinary industrial operations. It is not a property-owning Real Estate business or Healthcare business. Formal vendor sector classification was not independently established; do not silently treat a historical software label as the economic model.

The primary October 19 announcement and October 28 release establish that Q3 results have already occurred. No future earnings date is certified here. Allied Trust closing is expected in Q1 2027, not before January 1, subject to regulatory approvals and closing conditions. That is an acquisition catalyst, not an earnings date.

In January 2025 Porch sold Homeowners of America to the policyholder-owned Reciprocal. Porch consolidates it as a variable-interest entity but does not own its residual economics. The owned Insurance Services business earns management/distribution fees, surplus-note interest and captive reinsurance economics. Software & Data and Consumer Services are owned. Consolidated revenue, cash, equity and CFO therefore must not automatically be valued as shareholder-owned assets or owner cash. The transition is not a conventional listed-company spinoff distributing independently owned carrier shares to Porch shareholders.

Cash, capital and shareholder claims

At September 30 Porch cash was 80.063, Porch restricted cash 7.092, and Reciprocal cash including restricted amounts 143.387. Together they reconcile to consolidated cash 230.542. The Reciprocal cash is not an acquisition financing source for Porch shareholders. No separately quantified free fiduciary/customer-money pool is assumed. Restricted amounts include warranty claims collateral; premium and policyholder balances remain in the carrier perimeter.

The entire 77.512 of Porch short- and long-term investment securities is disclosed as held by the captive as collateral for the Reciprocal. It is not surplus cash to add to enterprise value. The approximately 106 of surplus notes owed by the Reciprocal earn SOFR + 9.75%, are eliminated on consolidation, and form part of its statutory surplus. Do not both capitalize that interest in owner cash and add full note principal without eliminating double counting. Repayment/distribution capacity needs insurer regulatory and capital analysis.

Reciprocal statutory surplus is 174.4, which is neither shareholder equity nor automatically distributable. Reciprocal losses and loss adjustment expense reserves on the balance sheet are 57.920, up from 49.159 at December 2025. This is the reported liability before separately presented reinsurance recoverables, not a net-of-reinsurance reserve measure; it must not be compared directly with net reserve triangles. Q3 Reciprocal loss attributable to policyholders was 3.172 and 9M loss 16.276. The 2026 catastrophe program starts at 35 per occurrence through a loss of 365; approximately 100 of catastrophe bond protection attaches above that tower. The preceding program's effective retention was approximately 23.1. Greater retained exposure and uncertain reinsurance renewals matter to fee capacity and contingent support, even though Porch does not own the carrier's residual losses. Captive risks and its own regulatory collateral are owned exposures.

Valuation posture, thesis and next decision

The evidence supports continued diligence, not automatic exclusion because the structure is complicated. Positives: fast policy growth, material operating leverage in the owned insurance service business, positive shareholder-perimeter CFO and raised guidance. Negatives: lower noninsurance revenue, substantial debt/discount amortization, real SBC and capitalized development spend, catastrophe/reinsurance and regulatory dependence, acquisition cash needs, potential dilution and unresolved quarterly cash classifications.

A defensible valuation would separately value after-tax fee/software/consumer operating cash, value captive distributable earnings after required capital, and separately treat the surplus-note claim without double counting. An equity cash-flow or sum-of-parts cross-check is more meaningful than feeding consolidated CFO into industrial FCFF. Broker peers such as Brown & Brown and Ryan Specialty may benchmark distribution economics; insurer peers may inform captive capital returns, but neither is an interchangeable consolidated PRCH multiple. No current peer multiples were verified in this package.

Discount rates, terminal growth, future claims, reinvestment and peer premiums are model assumptions that can be chosen and sensitized; they are not missing disclosures. The binding unresolved data are shareholder/captive capital distributions, a clean normalized cash bridge and consistent acquisition funding/share scenarios. No discount rate, terminal multiple, target, fair-value range or margin of safety is presented as established before that work. Explicit scenarios can later model organic growth and catastrophe stress, with base/stress regulatory capital and post-Allied dilution. Stage 3 and entry approval remain pending; long-term trend, EMA200 and sizing were not certified. Screening enthusiasm alone cannot establish undervaluation.

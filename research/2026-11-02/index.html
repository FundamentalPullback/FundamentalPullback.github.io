Business and investment thesis

AR produces Appalachian natural gas and liquids and owns approximately 29% of listed Antero Midstream (AM). Its differentiation is liquids exposure, transportation access, drilling inventory and lower operating costs after acquisitions and optimization. The most credible positive evidence is lower production cost and a stronger unhedged operating margin—not the scanner score or derivative-mark gains. Q3 production was 4.234 Bcfe/day; cash production cost was $2.05/Mcfe, versus $2.44 a year earlier. The company reports unhedged EBITDAX margin up 27% despite a lower Henry Hub benchmark. Acquired production and the retained royalty interest explain part of the improvement.

The countercase is substantial: the current EIA October outlook forecasts 2027 Henry Hub at $3.16, not the obsolete January forecast near $4.60. AR itself expects fourth-quarter regional-price discounts and 15–20 Bcfe of curtailments. NGL prices, fixed transportation commitments, shale depletion and replacement drilling can absorb apparent cash yield. The November 2 decline cannot be assigned confidently to a single company-specific event; regional gas pressure is a documented risk, not a proven explanation of every price move.

Cash bridge and sustainable growth

TTM CFO was $2,243.851m versus $1,538.189m. After cash drilling/land/other capital, economic SBC and cash minority distributions, owner cash was $1,040.016m versus $659.334m. Removing AM dividends and working-capital sources gives upstream levered cash of $899.335m versus $530.318m. This is after interest and is not FCFF.

TTM deferred-tax addbacks were $381.893m versus $41.338m. Deducting the entire addback as a conservative stress gives $517.442m versus $488.980m, only 5.8% growth; this stress is not a claim that all deferred taxes immediately become recurring cash taxes. NOLs and credits can legitimately delay payment, but permanent zero taxes would overstate value. The forward model therefore uses a normalized 25% operating tax burden and separately tests 20%, 21% and 28%.

A constant realized-price deck (gas $3.50/Mcf, ethane $12/bbl, C3+ $40/bbl, oil $65/bbl) produces TTM normalized EBIT of $1,169.016m versus $852.594m, up 37.1%, after SBC and depletion. This supports a real operating improvement beyond derivative marks. It is not organic or equal-perimeter growth: HG, July property purchases, the Utica sale and royalty reversion changed the business. The historical proxy also excludes termination/contingency charges; the forward model more prudently retains a $50m annual reserve, reflecting recurring reported expense.

The growth gate is therefore supported on the enlarged reported perimeter, but cannot be represented as a fully certified same-business normalized growth rate. Constant-deck NOPAT divided by average book upstream invested capital gives an indicative 8.92% ROIC; acquisitions and book values limit comparability.

The critical maintenance-capital ambiguity

February 11 guidance explicitly identified $900m maintenance D&C, plus $100m from not using a drilling joint venture and up to $200m discretionary growth capital. The October release raised total annual D&C to $1.2bn, discusses incremental pads and high-single-digit 2027 production growth, while the October hedge/guidance presentation labels fourth-quarter $325–350m “D&C Maintenance Capital Expenditures.”

A quarterly completion schedule cannot automatically be annualized as perpetual maintenance, but the older $900m figure cannot be treated as certified current replacement capital either. The model conservatively provisions $1.2bn at 4.3 Bcfe/day, then scales by volume and inflation, in addition to land, other investment and separately estimated growth spending. That may overstate reinvestment if the $1.2bn already contains meaningful growth. The $900m and $1.350bn alternatives quantify the issue rather than hide it. Obtaining a comparable sustaining-capital reconciliation is approval-critical.

FCFF model and assumptions

The model has a 59-day residual 2026 stub followed by 2027–2031 detailed years and 2032–2036 transition years. Annual cash flows are discounted at 1 + 59/365 through 10 + 59/365 years. September debt is the latest reported bridge; no unreported October cash generation is credited.

Revenue is physical gas/ethane/C3+/oil production times explicit realized prices, plus specified 2027–28 gas swaps. The central deck is Henry Hub $3.16/$3.35/$3.50 in 2027–29, then 2% nominal growth; initial C3+ $38, ethane $11 and oil $65. The $0.20 gas discount is an aggregate realized-basis assumption, including basis-settlement effects; a fully mapped regional and complex-collar hedge model is not completed. No new hedging benefit is assumed after 2028.

Central volume is 4.5 Bcfe/day in 2027, with physical growth slowing to zero by 2036. Cash production costs start at $2.15/Mcfe; 35% is held fixed relative to reference capacity to reflect transportation underutilization risk. G&A, marketing, SBC, exploration and recurring other expenses remain. DD&A starts at $0.62/Mcfe. Cash taxes are 25%; incremental working capital is 3% of physical-revenue growth. All assumptions are mapped in source_map.json.

Growth spending uses $4,500m times next-year physical growth. This is an analyst approximation derived from the company’s $200m discretionary investment potentially lifting daily production from 4.3 to 4.5 Bcfe (roughly $4,300m per 100% increment), rounded upward. It is not an engineering estimate and must not be confused with a verified marginal development-cost schedule.

WACC is 10% centrally, supported by the verified October 30 Treasury 10-year rate of 4.94%, analyst ERP 5% and beta 1.25, a 6% prospective debt cost and a tax-adjusted operating capital structure. The rounded result includes cyclicality risk; neither beta nor ERP is represented as a measured current market consensus. Adverse/favorable WACC is 11.5%/9%.

Reserves and terminal discipline

Year-end 2025 proved reserves were 19.149 Tcfe, including 4.671 Tcfe undeveloped, requiring approximately $2.3bn development spending over five years. Those reserves precede HG and the Utica sale; they are not the current combined reserve total. Forecast ten-year production is approximately 17.88 Tcfe. Inventory conversion and reserve replacement are therefore essential, not a rounding issue.

Terminal nominal growth is 2% with zero real volume growth. Net reinvestment is the greater of NOPAT × g / 10% incremental ROIC and the ongoing replacement-capital floor less DD&A. The maintenance floor binds centrally, making the effective implied incremental return lower than the 10% ceiling. Terminal value is 54.5% of operating EV. Gross transportation/lease commitments remain operating costs and are not double-counted as debt. Finite-continuation sensitivities retain full replacement spending and assume no residual value after the stated horizon; they are cautionary scenarios, not engineered reserve-runoff forecasts.

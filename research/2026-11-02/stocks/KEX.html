Thesis and what changed

Kirby combines domestic liquid-barge transportation with equipment distribution/service. Constrained barge supply and pricing recovery support marine earnings; behind-the-meter/backup power demand supports the second segment. Counterweights are fuel-cost recovery lags, weather/shipyard costs, labor and steel inflation, OEM delivery constraints and cyclical customer activity. These are not a subscription-software business's margins or reinvestment economics.

Latest sales growth is approximately 12%, net income growth 20% and EPS growth 27%; the difference is partly the shrinking share count. Marine utilization is already high, limiting easy volume upside. Management's FY2026 EPS-growth range is 10–15%, toward the upper end; CFO guidance is $575–675 million versus capital spending $220–260 million. October earnings release

The price rose 6.57% on release day, then retreated to $140.64. Accordingly this is a post-results retracement, not evidence the results were rejected. No company-specific November 2 adverse event was established; fuel/working-capital concerns are documented fundamentals, not a proven explanation of that day's move. Price history

Sustainable profit and cash

  • Removing the 2024 impairment and actual disposition gains produces normalized TTM EBIT $509.2 million versus $468.3 million, +8.7%, not the 22.5% GAAP growth headline. This sits in the plan's 5–15% quality-and-price band, not automatic high-growth priority.
  • Nine-month 2026 FCF is $124.1 million versus $140.6 million, despite the higher earnings. TTM FCF rises mainly because capex falls by $77.8 million; CFO increases only $20.2 million. The model does not extrapolate capex suppression forever.
  • FY2023 CFO included a $70.4 million tax refund. Earlier-cycle CFO was $321.6 million in 2021 and $294.1 million in 2022; 2022 capex was $172.6 million. Today's profit is substantially above the weak-cycle baseline. 2023 annual report
  • Q4 2025 alone generated approximately $265 million FCF. Seasonal collections therefore matter; no single 2026 quarter is annualized. FY2026 cash guidance needs a substantial final-quarter recovery.
  • Major ocean-vessel maintenance is deferred and amortized through operating expense. The FCFF model leaves this recurring amortization in EBIT and does not add it back; it assumes maintenance cash approximately matches the expense over the cycle. If shipyard cash persistently exceeds amortization, value falls. Ordinary repairs are also left in costs. Acquisition spending is separate from reported capex and must be paid for.
  • SBC is an operating cost throughout, never added back to FCFF. Reported SBC was $17.6 million in 2025 and $12.4 million in the first six months of 2026. Exact Q3 SBC is not disclosed in the release. Existing dilution is represented by 53.019 million third-quarter weighted-average diluted shares, a conservative proxy rather than a verified November 2 point-in-time count; no free future buybacks are forecast.

Central case

First-five-year marine growth: 6%, 5%, 4%, 3.5%, 3%; distribution growth: 8%, 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%. Marine margins: 19.5%, 20%, 20%, 19.5%, 19%; distribution: 10.5%, 11%, 11.5%, 11.5%, 11%. Corporate cost is 0.6% of revenue. SBC and ordinary maintenance remain in these margins. High utilization, modest renewal pricing and faster power demand motivate the segment distinction; these are analyst assumptions, not management forecasts or consensus.

Tax is 25%; D&A 7.6% of revenue; capital investment 8.2%; incremental working capital 15% of added sales. Adverse assumes volume weakness, compressed margins and ongoing replacement investment. Favorable assumes much stronger power-generation sales, marine margins around 22%, distribution margins around 14%, and a lower risk discount. Scenarios are neither probabilities nor guaranteed bounds.

Using the same model, $140.64 requires either a parallel 7.25 percentage-point increase in both segment-margin paths (terminal marine ~26.25%, distribution ~18.25%) or 8.28 percentage points more annual growth in each segment for the first five years, followed by the same transition. Alternatives can combine smaller growth/margin improvements; the price has no unique implied forecast. These are demanding relative to current business mix and replacement needs.

Revisit conditions, gaps and Stage 3.5

  1. Obtain the September 10-Q: reconcile cash/debt principal, SBC, receivables, inventories, tax timing, maintenance obligations and debt-financed repurchases.
  2. Validate acquisition closing, funding and operating contribution; validate sustainable fleet replacement capex rather than label all acquisitions discretionary growth.

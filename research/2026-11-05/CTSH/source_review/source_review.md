# CTSH: source review for the November 5, 2026 valuation

**Assessment:** Cognizant remains a viable cash-generating business, but the latest evidence does not support calling its headline growth predominantly organic services growth. The old $66.47 value is a model outcome, not a factual ceiling. Its 10% WACC deserves an independently grounded update. Its 4%-of-sales acquisition allowance is economically defensible only when paired with future acquired growth; it is neither a proven maintenance cost nor an amount that can simply be removed while retaining the same growth. Evaluate funded growth as the main capital-allocation case and an already-owned-business case as a cross-check.

**Price supplied by parent, independently visible in market source:** $65.78 regular close November 4, 2026. $64.18 is after-hours and is not the regular close. No valuation target or entry approval is issued here. This review does not publish anything or alter the preserved A120 analysis.

## 2. Revenue quality and earnings outlook

Primary Q3 filings/supplement disclose Q3 revenue +4.6% USD/+4.8% constant currency. Recent acquisitions contributed approximately 210 bps; third-party product sales contributed approximately 190 bps. Therefore organic including resale is directionally about 2.7% CC. Subtracting both contributions suggests only about 0.8% underlying services growth, **but this is a diagnostic, not a reported series**: rounded contributions and possible overlap between acquired businesses and resale prevent treating it as an exact organic-services bridge.

YTD acquisition contribution is about 140 bps and resale about 170 bps. FY2025 CC growth was 6.4%, with Belcan contributing 260 bps, implying about 3.8% organic including resale. The near-term organic growth rate has slowed. Q4 2026 guidance of 3.2–4.7% CC includes about 200 bps of acquisitions, implying roughly 1.2–2.7% organic including resale, around 2% at midpoint. Guidance does not establish an acceleration in 2027.

There are credible positive drivers: financial-services Q3 CC growth 12.3%; large-deal ramps; annualized bookings $28.7bn and book-to-bill about 1.3; demand for cloud/AI deployment; and management reports that AI-benefiting service lines are around 40% of business. But financial-services resale contributed about 400 bps, and other segments remain weaker. Health Sciences CC declined 0.9%, Products & Resources declined 0.8%; Communications/Media/Technology +10.7% includes about 875–900 bps of acquisition contribution. The latter is not broad-based double-digit organic growth.

Bookings are not cash or revenue: Q3 bookings fell 7%; TTM bookings rose 4%, while TTM annual contract value declined modestly as contract duration lengthened. Many contracts can terminate on short notice. Thus the 1.3 book-to-bill does not justify mechanically forecasting 5–6% organic revenue growth. [Q3 10-Q, S02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058290/000105829026000041/ctsh-20260930.htm); [issuer-hosted call, S07](https://cognizant.q4cdn.com/123993165/files/doc_earnings/2026/q3/transcript/Transcript_CTSHQ3-2026-Earnings-Call.pdf).

## 3. Margins, restructuring and FX

Q3 GAAP/adjusted margins are 15.4%/16.3%; FY2026 adjusted guide is approximately 16.1%. This measure retains SBC and acquired-intangible amortization. It excludes Leap and the India reversal. Do not add back acquisition amortization once more when using that EBIT forecast.

The INR depreciation benefited operating margin, net of hedging, by about 120 bps in Q3 and 90 bps YTD versus the respective prior-year periods. Adjusted margin improved only about 30 bps. That does not require automatically reversing all current FX benefit, but it means the improvement is not purely productivity. Hold an explicit FX level; do not add another 90 bps every year. Source sensitivity: a further 1% INR/USD change moves operating margin about 17 bps before hedges.

Leap total cost estimate fell to $180–250m, with $140–160m in 2026 and the remainder through Q1 2027. The remaining 2027 accounting charge is consequently $20–110m based on the endpoints, not a confirmed point estimate. About $200m of 2026 savings is expected, primarily reinvested in AI/offerings/upskilling. Do not add this entire $200m to forecast earnings on top of the guided margin. YTD charges $135m; employee-separation payments $68m and separation accrual $34m at September 30. Total future program cash cannot be inferred solely from that separation accrual.

A reasonable central GAAP-style normalized EBIT-margin range after SBC and a recurring transformation allowance is roughly 15.5–16.0%, with 2027 at the lower part if remaining Leap charges are retained. 16.3–16.8% belongs in an upside case requiring actual delivery economics and mix improvement; 14–15% is an operating downside. These are analyst ranges, not issuer guidance.

## 4. Cash conversion: the deterioration is real, but not necessarily perpetual

YTD CFO fell $189m to $1,836m. The filing attributes this primarily to higher incentive compensation paid, partly offset by lower cash taxes. Changes in operating assets/liabilities used $818m versus $454m, a $364m deterioration. The individual YoY differences are receivables −$18m; other assets −$154m; payables +$74m; deferred revenue −$33m; other liabilities −$233m. Together these exactly reconcile the −$364m.

Specific balance-sheet evidence:
- DSO 89 days Sep2026 versus 82 Sep2025 and 81 Dec2025. The call says up five days YoY, inconsistent with the filing's seven; use the filing for history.
- Contract assets $466m at Dec2025 → $690m Sep2026; receivables $4,439m → $4,850m.
- Costs to fulfill: YTD capitalization $94m versus $31m; amortization $44m versus $59m; balance $161m → $209m. TTM capitalization is $105m and amortization $63m, net cash-versus-expense difference $42m before impairment effects.
- Deferred revenue $538m → $444m; resale-related liabilities $242m → $380m; compensation/benefits accrual $1,490m → $1,228m.

This is more informative than calling all working-capital use temporary. However, holding today's higher DSO constant does not require a fresh seven-day deterioration every year. For forecasting, use a stable operating working-capital intensity plus an adverse DSO scenario. A further five days at the Q3 daily revenue rate is around $308m cash use; a seven-day normalization could free roughly $431m, but neither is in the base case here.

TTM CFO minus capex is $2,363m. Subtracting SBC gives $2,170m. An illustrative enterprise-cash proxy, additionally adding after-tax interest expense and removing after-tax interest income at 26%, is $2,143m. This is a diagnostic, not a fully normalized FCFF: actual taxes, provisions and non-operating employee-benefit costs remain in cash flow. TTM cash paid for acquisitions is $1,313m; subtracting it leaves only $1,050m strict FCF, or $857m after SBC. Acquisitions are investment, not a recurring operating expense, but the economic cash cost cannot be ignored.

## 5. Software, D&A and SBC

Capex already includes internal-use and saleable software in property/equipment. Separate software development expensed in operations remains in EBIT. Some cloud implementation and client-transition costs are deferred in other assets and affect CFO, not property capex. Include those in operating working capital or a separate net-capitalization schedule, not both.

FY2025 property/equipment D&A is $332m and acquired-intangible amortization $218m, total $550m. TTM total D&A is $573m, 2.62% of sales, versus capex $331m, 1.51%. FY2023/2024 cash-flow D&A $555m/$542m exceeds income-statement D&A $519m/$529m because accelerated depreciation $36m/$13m was inside restructuring. This is not missing cash investment.

FY2025 computer-software gross assets $1,123m. Saleable software gross/net assets $377m/$125m and annual amortization $43m. These are existing assets, not additional undisclosed capex to subtract on top of the cash-flow statement.

For a funded-growth model, D&A around 2.5–2.7% and capex around 1.5–1.8% of revenue are reasonable near-term ranges. In a no-new-M&A model, do not perpetually keep acquired amortization at a constant sales percentage without a replacement/reinvestment rationale. The FY2025 book of intangibles projected amortization $217m/$209m/$187m/$169m/$144m in 2026–2030 before the 2026 acquisitions. Their $380m customer relationships, average useful life9.2 years, add roughly $41m annual amortization at a full run-rate. This is only an approximate accounting schedule, not a tax-depreciation schedule.

SBC: $176m/$175m/$181m in FY2023–2025; $193m TTM; $149m YTD2026. Use 0.85–1.0% of revenue in operating costs and do not add it back in economic FCFF if holding shares constant. Historical buybacks reduced shares substantially, but are not free prospective growth.

## 6. Acquisition reinvestment: what is and is not defensible

3Cloud closed January 1, 2026; Astreya closed June22, 2026. Both contributed a full Q3. Latest preliminary consideration is $728m and $670m, total $1,398m, including Astreya's $25m contingent payment. Acquired cash totals $55m. Goodwill $1,004m and customer relationships $380m dominate the purchase allocation. Individual acquired revenue, EBIT and economic ROIC are not disclosed in the primary reports reviewed.

Approximate Q3 acquired revenue = 2.1% × prior Q3 revenue$5,415m = $113.7m; annualized $454.9m. Consideration/annualized revenue is ~3.07x gross, ~2.95x net of acquired cash. This is a usable scenario anchor, **not** a precise transaction sales multiple: the growth contribution is rounded, seasonality and post-acquisition growth matter, and no exact standalone sales denominator is supplied. A 2.5–3.5x range is appropriate for model sensitivity; a directly disclosed target-run-rate denominator was not found.

The FY2026 acquired revenue contribution (~1.5% of FY2025 revenue) is about $317m. Against the ~$455m Q3 annualized rate, inherited full-yearization is roughly $138m, or about 0.6% of FY2026 group sales, in 2027 before target growth/mix. This is already-paid-for revenue, not an extra $1.4bn future acquisition requirement. Treat 0.4–0.7% as an illustrative range, not certified guidance.

Cash acquisition outlays were $409m in 2023, $1,615m in 2024, zero in 2025 and $1,313m in 9M2026. The latter consists of $730m Q1, $604m Q2 and a $21m net refund/adjustment Q3. Total $3,337m is ~4.35% of revenue across those same reporting periods. 2024 also issued $113m stock in Belcan consideration. Thus the old 4% reserve is broadly consistent with recent capital intensity; it was not an invented impossible expense.

But capital-allocation policy (~50% of FCF to M&A in the FY2025 10-K) does not by itself establish a maintenance expense. Model new acquired revenue and its profit contribution alongside the purchase cost. At a 3x purchase/sales ratio, steady-state acquired growth1% costs roughly 3% of group sales; acquired growth1.5% costs4.5%. If deals close midyear, current-year recognition is lower and the rest carries into the next year. Do not create fully recognized growth immediately without a timing convention.

Recommended comparison:
1. **Main funded-growth case:** organic including resale2–3%, plus recurring acquired growth around1–1.5%, funded at 2.5–3.5x purchased revenue. This broadly supports total growth3–4.5% and acquisition spend3–4.5% of group sales under a steady pipeline. Use actual inherited 2027 acquisition tail separately.
2. **Owned-business cross-check:** no speculative new acquisitions, lower subsequent revenue growth around2–3%, existing acquisition amortization run-off, full cash costs of maintaining skills/customer relationships, and no free assumed buybacks. DyFlex is already agreed for approximately $210m cash payable upon closing, subject to customary closing adjustments. A literal no-new-M&A path is therefore not management's announced trajectory: the owned-business cross-check should include this announced-deal exception, or explicitly label its exclusion as counterfactual. Standalone revenue/EBIT and net cash acquired remain undisclosed in the reviewed sources. In the funded case, count the $210m within the 2027 acquisition budget, not on top of it if that budget already covers the deal.
3. **Downside:** low organic growth, margin14–15%, persistent costly M&A, and another working-capital step-up. A central worth above market only in the zero-M&A case does not establish robust cheapness.

## 8. What would make $65.78 reasonable, and what would refute it?

The favorable argument need not require explosive AI growth: a durable15.5–16% operating margin, ordinary2–3% owned-business growth, controlled acquisition cost per acquired revenue, stable rather than worsening DSO, and a separately justified lower cost of capital can support materially different value from A120. The business has low funded leverage and meaningful recurring cash earnings. The older DCF may be conservative on cost of capital, while still needing a realistic acquisition bill.

The weak argument would be to remove4% of sales of future M&A, retain4.5% growth, perpetuate a D&A credit from old acquisitions, exempt every restructuring charge, assume a working-capital release, and forecast buybacks without spending cash. That combination would manufacture upside.

The main model should remain viable with tax26%, recurring transformation costs, an acquisition budget tied to growth, and no working-capital release. If reasonable combined downside cases readily fall below current price, label the valuation's limited protection rather than imposing a universal10/15/20% margin-of-safety rule.

Proof points: Q4 FCF around the implied$0.73–1.03bn range; stable/improving DSO and contract assets; organic growth excluding the temporary acquisition anniversary contribution; margins supported without repeated FX gains; DyFlex standalone revenue/EBIT, closing and annualization; acquisition-driven incremental returns; 2027 guidance.
